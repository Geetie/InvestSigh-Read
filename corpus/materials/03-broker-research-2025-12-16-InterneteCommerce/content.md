Hyperscaler capex: A look at potential AI revenues and expected returns
Industry Overview
AI and capex returns remain top sector theme into 2026
Sentiment on AI capabilities, long-term incremental revenues, and capex returns are expected to remain a critical driver of mega-cap Internet performance into 2026. In this report, we review: (1) Major sources of incremental revenue underpinning the AI investment cycle, (2) A framework for Street expected returns on AI capex, (3) Funding capacity for incremental capex in 2026, and (4) Key AI positioning factors that could shape hyperscaler sentiment in 2026. Our key takeaways include the following:
Hyperscalers chasing \$1tr+ in incremental revenues
We estimate a \$1tn+ AI driven incremental revenue opportunity over next five years across: (1) Cloud: \~\$500bn incremental revenues (per IDC) driven by accelerated Cloud adoption as enterprises deploy Gen-AI workloads, (2) Digital Advertising: \~\$400bn incremental revenue opportunity driven by AI-enabled improvements in targeting and measurement improvements that enhance ROAS and expand advertiser budgets; and (3) AI Subscriptions: \~\$200bn+ incremental revenue opportunity as businesses & consumers adopt paid AI tools for productivity, learning, and agentic tasks.
A look at expected returns on burgeoning AI capex
While not a true ROI measure, one way to measure potential returns on capex is to look at estimates for incremental revenue & Adj. EBITDA generated in the year following capex investment. Historically (2021-24), each dollar of capex helped generate an average of \$0.90 incremental revenue and \$0.42 of incremental EBITDA in the following year. Looking forward (2025-2028), Street estimates suggest that each dollar of capex will generate \$0.50 of incremental revenue and \~\$0.27 of incremental EBITDA in the following year. Given 5-year depreciable lives, we think cash returns over 20% can be attractive, but hyperscalers will need to find Opex (lower headcount) and capex (custom silicon) efficiencies to maintain operating profit margins.
Hyperscalers tapping debt and innovative financing
The Big-5 hyperscalers (excluding Oracle) generate enough cash flow to fund capex in Street estimates but are also tapping debt to maintain flexibility & optimize cost of capital. Some are also pursuing innovative off-balance sheet credit structures, which provide greater flexibility by matching long-duration infra. costs with revenue. At the end of 2025, we estimate Big-5 hyperscaler debt at \$433bn, and a debt to OCF ratio at around 0.8x, suggesting more debt capacity is available.
Alphabet best positioned across categories for AI
We think four key elements that could define structural moats in the Gen-AI era and can drive sentiment include: (1) leadership in foundational models, (2) consumer distribution, (3) enterprise distribution, and (4) custom silicon. We review each hyperscaler’s positioning across these four dimensions (see page 16) and, while we expect multiple AI winners, we see Buy-rated Alphabet as best positioned across all segments given company’s strength in foundational models (Gemini 3.0), custom silicon (TPU demand), enterprise Cloud, & multiple consumer distribution surfaces (for training and revenues).

Part 1: Chasing a \$1trn+ revenue opportunity
In Part 1 of our report, we examine some of the largest revenue opportunities that are driving AI investment. These include Cloud, Digital Advertising, and AI Subscriptions, that together represent a \$1tn+ incremental revenue opportunity over the next five years. These opportunities include: (1) approximately \$500bn in incremental Cloud revenue (per IDC) driven by accelerated enterprise adoption of AI workloads; (2) roughly \$400bn in incremental Digital Advertising revenue as AI improves targeting,
measurement, and ROAS, expanding overall advertiser budgets; and (3) more than \$200bn in incremental AI Subscription revenue as enterprises and consumers adopt paid AI tools and agentic services across productivity, search, shopping, and entertainment.
\$500bn+ incremental infrastructure revenue opportunity
IDC estimates that the global cloud market is expected to grow from \$213bn in 2025 to \$576bn by 2029, generating roughly \$500bn incremental revenue, with nearly half from AI workloads. AI workloads are expected to grow from \$46bn in 2025 to \$291bn to 2030, driven by accelerating deployment of generative AI applications, migration of legacy workloads, and rapid expansion of AI inference and training demand across industries.
\$400bn+ incremental digital advertising opportunity
Consensus estimates call for combined digital advertising revenue for Google, Meta, and Amazon to grow from \$525bn in 2025 to \$934bn by 2030, generating nearly \$400bn in incremental revenue. Growth will be driven by improving Online shopping and booking experiences, and Increasing AI integration across the ad stack, enabling more nuanced understanding of intent and improving targeting. AI is also increasing capabilities to measure outcomes, driving share gains for scaled ecosystems.

Google has introduced Smart Bidding Exploration, a major update to its bidding framework that expands advertisers’ reach by identifying and bidding on a broader range of high-intent search queries beyond traditional keyword targeting. Built on existing AIpowered tools like AI Max, Smart Bidding Exploration helps campaigns capture highperforming searches from new, unique categories. For example, a mortgage lender that previously bid on exact terms like “mortgage” or “home loan” can now use Smart Bidding Exploration to capture broader, but still relevant, queries such as “how to buy a home,” “best time to apply for a mortgage,” or “what credit score do I need to buy a house.” These are intent-rich, upper- to mid-funnel queries where AI should help Google to capture leads earlier in the decision-making process.
AI Max marks early shift toward fully automated ad targeting
In May ’25, Google introduced AI Max, a suite of targeting and creative enhancements for search advertising. AI Max helps advertisers (1) expand reach through search term matching that identifies broader high-intent audiences, and (2) deliver more relevant ads by generating new text assets such as headlines and descriptions based on landing pages, ads, and keywords. Google’s asset optimization features can also route traffic to the most relevant merchant landing pages. We view AI Max as an important step toward moving beyond traditional keyword bidding to capture emerging AI-driven search opportunities. We think AI Max could support incremental monetization growth, and over time, we expect portions of keyword bidding to shift toward fuller AI automation, with Google dynamically generating multi-modal ad content and selecting the most relevant phrases and contexts for placement.

Part 2: Implied Capex returns
With industry capex growing to historically high levels both in terms of absolute dollars, and as a percentage of revenue, we think a top mega-cap investor question is potential returns on capex spending. Estimating future ROI using traditional return measures is inherently difficult given uncertainties about capex funding, industry infrastructure capacity (an overbuild would lower returns), future innovation in AI models, token pricing and macro conditions. However, we have Street estimates for capex, revenues, margins, and operating cash flow, which can provide an interesting framework for how the Street is estimating potential returns. We think one potential way to evaluate returns on capex spending is to look at incremental revenue and operating profit (ex-D&A) generated in the year following the capex investment, and we go through this exercise below.
Capex expected to increase close to 400% from 2023 to 2027
Combined capex (per consensus estimates) for the Big-5 hyperscalers is expected to grow from \$154bn in 2023 to \$612bn in 2027. While estimates in 2025 and 2026 appear reasonable, we think out-year estimates will move higher if macro conditions are favorable.

If we assume incremental EBITDA generated in the year after a capex investment (Y+1) persists for five years (potential server life), we can approximate a return on that capex. Potential implications:
1. Street still expecting positive returns on capex
Assuming data centers have at least a 5-year life (land, buildings longer, infrastructure 4- 7 years) we think incremental Y+1 EBITDA returns of over 20% suggest returns on capex can be positive.
2. Potentially lower long-term margins without cost savings initiatives
If each dollar of capex is producing less forward-year EBITDA, and depreciation costs are increasing, sector margins could be structurally declining. This raises the risk of margin compression unless companies reduce other operating expenses, such as headcount and marketing, to offset. Current Street estimates call for flattish operating margins at around 35%, despite higher expected depreciation expense as a percentage of revenue.
3. A need to build custom silicon
We think the companies in the sector have high incentive to improve capital returns by reducing compute costs. We expect an increasing focus on custom silicon (such as TPUs, MTIA, Tranium, etc.) across the industry. Over time, lower compute costs could increase incremental revenue-to-capex ratios.

Below, we assess each company’s relative positioning across these four dimensions. While we expect multiple winners in an incremental AI market estimated at more than \$1tr, we think Alphabet is best positioned as the only company with strong relative positioning across all four areas (Gemini model, TPUs, enterprise Cloud business and multiple consumer distribution surfaces). We think Meta’s stock performance in 2026 will be highly dependent on the company’s ability to build and release a competitive foundational model in 1Q, while Amazon’s performance could be tied to progress with, and adoption of, Trainium chips.

Leadership in foundational models
Owning a leading frontier model reduces dependency on external providers and creates long-term strategic advantages through integration with core consumer and enterprise products. We think most applications, agents, and enterprise workloads will have advantages by being built on best performing models. Also, companies with sustained leadership in model quality and iteration speed will attract the highest-value developers and enterprise customers. Currently Google and OpenAI have a measurable lead in model capability and ecosystem traction, giving them structural advantages. However, as Gemini’s improvement has shown over the past two years, these advantages may not be permanent.
Consumer distribution & touchpoints
We think consumer distribution will also emerge as a major differentiating factor. Platforms with large user bases and multiple surfaces for AI integration are best positioned to train models, and drive adoption and monetization of AI at scale. In our view, Meta and Google are strongest on this dimension given the breadth of their app ecosystems and ability to distribute and commercialize AI features across massive, highly engaged audiences. OpenAI is rapidly building scale in this area with 900mn WAUs. Access to high-quality first-party user data and large-scale feedback loops will also be a key factor in frontier model improvement, as demonstrated by Gemini’s recent performance gains.

We believe enterprise distribution will be another critical moat as deep integration into mission-critical systems creates high switching costs and long-term stickiness for AI services. All hyperscalers except Meta possess strong enterprise channels, with Google, Microsoft, and Amazon having the largest and most mature footprints.
