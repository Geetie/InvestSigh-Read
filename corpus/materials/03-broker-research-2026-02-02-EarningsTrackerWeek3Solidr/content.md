Tracking a 6%\* EPS beat at halftime
Following week 3, results from 167 S&P 500 companies (\~50% of index earnings) are now in. Reported earnings came in 6% ahead of consensus \*excluding Boeing’s one-time gain from an asset sale. 69% of companies beat on EPS, 63% on revenue and 51% on both – all metrics are below last quarter (76%/76%/63%) but above the week 3 avg. (65%/60%/46%). 4Q EPS growth (blended measure of actuals & consensus estimates) is now tracking 11% YoY (up from 7% last week), pushing the index toward its fifth consecutive quarter of double-digit EPS growth.
Upbeat commentary; guidance off peak but still strong
Corporate sentiment based on an NLP analysis of transcripts is tracking at a record high in 4Q, now above pre-Liberation Day levels of optimism (Exhibit 24); similarly, “weak demand” mentions are trending lower (Exhibit 25), and guidance improved last week after starting off cautious (see Week 2 recap) – there were 1.2x more above- than below-consensus EPS guides in Jan., a decline from the 1.9x we saw in the prior three months but much better than the Jan. avg. of 0.6x. Consensus 2026 S&P 500 EPS of \$312 (+14% YoY) has held steady YTD, revised higher by 0.2% since Jan 1.
AI’s “show me” moment splits hyperscalers
Hyperscalers’ divergent price action after posting beats (Meta up 10%, Microsoft down 10%) underscores AI’s transition from “tell me” to “show me”: beats without clear progress on AI monetization aren’t rewarded for upping capex. Meta’s above-consensus revenue guide, plus indications of AI driving usage and ad efficiencies, were enough for investors to feel comfortable with the above-consensus capex guide (see META recap). But Microsoft’s underwhelming Azure growth failed to dispel concerns (see MSFT recap).
Muted reactions to beats/misses: guidance driving alpha
Companies that beat on EPS and revenue have not seen as big of a pop as usual this quarter, but companies that missed have not sold off as much either (see Exhibit 9). Guidance has been a much stronger driver of price action, such that companies that issued above-consensus EPS guidance outperforming the S&P 500 by 410bp the next day (6x the reward for top- and bottom-line beats) whereas companies issuing belowconsensus EPS guidance lagged by 320bp (2x the penalty for misses).
Up next: the busiest week of earnings (130 companies)
130 companies representing 25% of index earnings are slated to report this week. By Friday, 75% of earnings will be in. Big tech earnings continue with Google (Wednesday) and Amazon (Thursday). So far, the sector has exceeded the high expectations set for it – Tech + Mag 7 earnings are tracking +24% YoY in 4Q, up from the projected 20% heading into reporting season.

Swing factor: IEEPA
Our economists argue that if the IEEPA (International Emergency Economic Power Act) tariffs are struck down, the net effect on economic activity would likely be positive but depends on i) the extent to which policy uncertainty increases as the administration moves to replace the IEEPA tariffs with other measures, ii) how much revenue can be recovered from those measures, and iii) the bond market response to higher deficits (see Remember, remember the 5th of November).
Our economists estimate that IEEPA accounts for 9.5ppt of the 14.7% effective tariff rate. If the IEEPA tariffs are struck down, the administration will likely turn to other trade authorities (e.g., section 122, which allows a broad-based tariff of up to 15% to be put in place for a maximum of 150 days). Our economists’ base case is for the effective tariff rate to settle 3-5ppt below current levels if IEEPA tariffs are ruled unlawful.

Appendix - Methodology
Guidance Ratios
Earnings guidance: We track the number of instances of above- vs. below-consensus management guidance for earnings over the last three months for S&P 500 companies. If a company issues changes to its outlook more than once in a one-month period, we incorporate all instances of guidance into our aggregate number. The ratio also includes all instances of above- or below-consensus earnings guidance issued by a company (for example, if they issue both quarterly and annual guidance). The one-month and threemonth revision ratios are calculated as they are for estimate revision ratios. The data source is Bloomberg. For companies that provide both GAAP and Operating guidance, or for REITs that provide both EPS and FFO guidance, we remove one data point if both data points provide the same guidance direction, otherwise both data points are used.
Capex guidance: We track the number of instances of above- vs. below-consensus management guidance for planned capex over the last three months for S&P 500 companies, calculated the same way as above; data source is FactSet.
Earnings Revision ratios
Beginning in April 1986 (and January 2006 for Real Estate), we calculate the ratio between the number of companies in the S&P 500 for which Thomson Financial consensus earnings estimates have been raised versus those that have been lowered. We apply the same calculation to the S&P 500 GICS Sectors (where we use FFO for REITs within Real Estate). One-month revision ratios are computed as the number of companies for which estimates this month are higher than they were last month, divided by the number of companies for which estimates this month are lower than they were last month. For example, within a particular subgroup of the market, if consensus estimates have increased for 5 companies and have decreased for 10, the estimate revision ratio will be 0.5 (5 divided by 10).
The three-month revision ratio is similarly defined as the total number of earnings estimate increases divided by total number of earnings estimate decreases during the last three months. For example, in February 2002, the three-month revision ratio is computed as the total number of companies with increased monthly estimates, divided by the total number of companies with decreased estimates throughout December 2001, January 2002, and February 2002.
We similarly calculate the S&P 600 (small cap) earnings revision ratios (one-month and three-month) by the same methodology, with data beginning in February 2001.
For earnings/sales estimates, we look at the change in the FY1 estimate (current unreported year) if there's more than 120 days between the current date and the company's fiscal year-end date, otherwise we look at the change in the FY2 estimate (next unreported year).

Methodology: Earnings Calls Sentiment
With the help of BofA’s Predictive Analytics team, we parsed through earnings calls transcripts to calculate sentiment for the S&P500 universe of companies that have reported since 31st March 2020. We use the Loughran McDonald’s financial dictionary to calculate sentiment scores as per the definition below.
Sentiment score = No. of Unique positive words – No. of unique negative and uncertainty words
The sentiment score is computed with three different filters: the full transcript, management discussion and answers of CEO/CFO from Q/A section. Calculated scores were then averaged on the Sector level. Loughran-McDonald Sentiment and Uncertainty:
1. Loughran-McDonald Sentiment: Examples of positive words include accomplish, achieve, outperform, stabilize, strength and negative words such as abandon, abnormal, downturn, evade, failing, stagnate. In total, the lexicon has 2,355 negative words and 354 positive words.
2. Loughran-McDonald Uncertainty: Examples of uncertain words include almost, ambiguity, hidden, fluctuate, doubts, unclear. In total, the lexicon has 297 words.
Note that the charts show the sentiment score multiplied by (- 1) and after applying the natural log to normalize the data on the same scale. Our Predictive Analytics team is currently researching more advance modeling approaches including Deep Learning so please stay tuned for future enhancements.
