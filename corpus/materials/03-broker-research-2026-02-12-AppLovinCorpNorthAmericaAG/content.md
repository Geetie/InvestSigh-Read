The biggest focus of the call remained the ongoing web opportunity, with management sounding constructive on progress, ahead of the broader GA rollout targeted for 1H:26. Importantly, management highlighted ~30-day LTV-to-CAC payback on early marketing tests and improving advertiser conversion rates, suggesting the self-serve launch could meaningfully accelerate customer acquisition once fully opened. From a guidance perspective, the company's mid-point 1Q targets of $1,760mn of revenue (6% q/q growth) and $1,480mn of EBITDA (84% margin) were modestly ahead of our prior estimates.
We Were Also Encouraged to See the Company Push Back on Bear Cases Around AI/World Models, Meta, and Cloudx: Management also pushed back on bear concerns across Meta, CloudX, and broader AI disruption narratives, all of which have contributed to APP's underperformance in recent weeks. On META, the company emphasized that Meta Audience Network has been a longtime bidder within APP's ad supply and that incremental bidding activity on IDFA traffic is not new. Importantly, they also argued the competitive landscape today is fundamentally different than five years ago (when META was last dominant) given APP's performance gains and the closed-loop data advantage the company has built. On CloudX, the company highlighted the moats around its MAX mediation business, arguing it is protected by the integrated 360 solution (i.e. combining best-in-class monetization with differentiated UA performance), which drives meaningful switching costs and share-of-wallet concentration for customers.
What Are We Watching from Here? Monitoring data points around those bear cases remains important, fundamentally and thematically. However, our main focus remains on the ecommerce ramp through 1H:26 and into the general launch of the web self-serve tool. With APP now trading at 17x our '27 EBITDA, a breakthrough in ecommerce/web ads would be likely be an important driver of multiple expansion and earnings revisions for '26 and beyond. APP's pace of ad targeting innovation over the past 3 years leaves us constructive on their ability to consistently and meaningfully improve on the performance of the ad network across verticals, with ecommerce the key opportunity to watch.
Maintain OW, PT to $720: Turning to our model, we raise our ‘26/’27 EBITDA by 2%/2% to account for the company’s 4Q results, 1Q guidance, and forward commentary. We continue to expect ~$1.8bn of non-gaming ad revenue in ’26 and we note the 1H:26 GA launch remains a key opportunity to drive faster growth in web. We maintain our OW rating but lower $720 price target, based on a target multiple of 28x EV/EBITDA. Our target multiple is also informed by a regression of peers, which have derated significantly in recent weeks, informing our decision to mark our APP multiple to market. While 28x EV/EBITDA remains a significant premium vs. the peer regression, we believe it is justified by APP’s large, multi-vertical opportunity and rapid pace of innovation. We also note that APP has (in the context of broader Internet) a high level of valuation support on GAAP PE, as our $720 PT implies 38x our '27 GAAP EPS. While the market's willingness to pay for gaming/mobile ad tech assets is clearly under pressure, we view APP as the bluechip name in the space and therefore more likely to outperform as demand for growth stocks normalizes.
