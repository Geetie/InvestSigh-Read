Building on our 3Q25 AI tracker, this 4Q25 Tracker focuses on four key areas: 1) summarize “circular financing” between AI giants 2) updates on AI capex and data center projects; 3) shipment progress of GPU and ASIC servers; 4) the financial performance of suppliers in the AI supply chain.
Total investment in upcoming and under-construction data centers stands at ~US $840B. Over the past quarter, OpenAI has entered into several agreements with semi and CSP companies, raising the concerns over circular financing (Exhibit 1). Meanwhile, there are increasing confirmed AIDC projects, including the 7GW initial investment for Stargate in the U.S., Google’s $15B investment in India data center, and Microsoft’s Fairwater project. ASIC adoption also increases. Amazon’s Indiana datacenter is guided to operates a million Trainium 2 chips for Anthropic by the year-end. Post-3Q25 earnings, consensus raised 2026 capex estimates for the major CSPs by nearly 20% vs. 2 months ago, and model total capex to grow 36% CAGR to ~US$630B in 2024-27.
We model global server/high-end GPU AI server shipment to grow at 3%/31% CAGRs in 2025-27E, and expect total server market to reach c.US$650B. We expect high-end GPU servers shipment (8-GPU equivalent) to grow 46% next year (Model), and total rack shipment to increase from 30K this year to 58K next year (Exhibit 11). For Vera Rubin, there are different design options for each type of components (PCB, power, etc.), and the final design is pending on chip integration and CSPs choice. We project ASICs to comprise 47% of the total CoWoS-based AI chip shipment in 2026, and TPU shipment to grow c.75% YoY. The engagement on TPUs & possibly projects with other customers is also why our Asia Semis team rates MediaTek Outperform (report, model).
CoWoS & HBM demand will grow strongly in 2026 too. With GPU/ASIC featuring larger package size & memory capacity, we project robust HBM/CoWoS growth (report, model), & a record year for DRAM suppliers & TSMC in 2026. Conventional DRAM supply is tight but should not affect AI investment as it represents a small faction of the data center capex.
ASIC supply chain stocks continue to outperform GPU supply chains recently (Exhibit 26), boosted by Broadcom’s $10B+ new orders, OpenAI announcement, and Google TPU’s commercial success; however we view recent GPU angst as overdone, and continue to like both NVIDIA and Broadcom stocks. Despite recent soft sentiment over GPU supply chain, tech hardware sectors like PCB, power & thermal are still resilient. Compared to Jan-2025, over 80% of our sampled stocks saw an expansion on valuation and a lift for 2026 EPS estimates from consensus. Our top picks in Asia hardware coverage are Unimicron (short-term) and Delta (long-term).
We model Quanta to grow revenue/EPS at 27%/14% CAGR in 2025-27. We lifted topline forecast slightly to reflect higher AI server mix, which represents 60%-65% of total revenue in 2026-27; margins will be under pressure with buy-sell model. We maintained our target P/E at 12.5x, and apply that on 2026 EPS of NT$19.9 (vs. previous NT$19.5). Price target lifted to NT$250 (old NT$240), Underperform.

AI CAPEX & DATACENTER SCHEDULE
CONSENSUS LIFT 2026 CAPEX ESTIMATES BY 20% COMPARED TO SEP-2025, AND EXPECT CAPEX FROM MAJOR CSPS TO REACH 630B BY 2027
Is circular financing a necessity for AGI? Or is it breeding a “too big to fail” entity? Over the past quarter, OpenAI has, it would seem, entered into several significant agreements (Exhibit 1), including: 1) to deploy at least 10GW of datacenter using Nvidia systems starting from 2H26, and Nvidia will invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI progressively as the capacity is deployed 2) to purchase 6 GW of AMD GPUs (end-2026 to 2030), while AMD granting OpenAI warrants in the company’s stock (up to 160 million AMD shares, vesting in tranches). 3) to purchase 10 GW of Broadcom’s custom AI accelerators & racks (for more, please refer to: 1) NVIDIA (NVDA) and OpenAI: The King's largesse? 2) Advanced Micro Devices (AMD): OpenAI's tide lifts another boat? 3) Broadcom (AVGO): Joining the OpenAI 10GW club...
Following the initial wave of enthusiasm, investors have begun to question OpenAI's financial capacity to fulfill these commitments. According to The Information, OpenAI’s revenue in 1H25 was only $4.3B and targets a full-year revenue of US$13B and cashburn target of US$8.5B. Although the company projects annual revenue to reach $200B by 2030 and start making a profit in 2029, its purchase commitments over the next 5-7 years have a total of over one trillion dollars based on Exhibit 2.
But Bernstein’s Global software team believes the situation is far more nuanced than the news flow would suggest1 in two very important ways:
• We believe that many of these “commitments” are in fact are cancelable or the go live dates have not been locked in. They are likely more of Letters of Intent (LOI) that OpenAI can walk away from if they don’t need the capacity or have no fixed starting time.
Even for non-cancelable/ fixed term commitments the agreements layer in over time and continue for 5–6 years. For example, we understand that the ~$300B commitment with Oracle layers in, in tranches starting in Oracle’s FY27 and continuing through FY30 and each contract is at least 5 years. This means that OpenAI’s maximum commitment grow each year and if the contracts are not renewed (which we believe they will be) then the maximum annual commitment will be $50-$60B in Oracle’s FY30 or FY31 ramping up till the peak commitment is reached.
The $250B commitment with Microsoft likely contains, we believe, a portion that has fixed non-cancelable time frame and a meaningful portion that is more fungible as to when the increase in spending will occur. In other words, OpenAI will, as they did with spending their original Microsoft investment, come to Microsoft and say we need $X capacity for the next 6 years. Each time an order is placed that order becomes non-cancelable and with a 6-year term.
Thus, according to the Global Software team’s understanding: 1) OpenAI’s contractual non-cancelable commitments are far smaller than $1T (likely less than ½) with tranches of capacity roll-outs and optionality for more capacity if OpenAI desires. In fact, OpenAI may be committed to < $100B of datacenter capacity in CY30 when they expect to have $200B in revenue.
Consensus increased capex estimates again. Following 3Q25 earnings, the consensus further adjusted up the capex estimates for the four US hyperscalers (Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Google), and expect total capex to grow a CAGR of 33% during 2024–27 to ~US$536B (vs. US$435B in Sep 2025). Combining neo-cloud and Oracle (which is a mixture of CPU-based hyperscaler, AI training and now AI inference hyperscaler), the collective capex will grow at 36% CAGR to ~US$630B by 2027 (vs. ~$500B in Sep 2025). Compared to the consensus as of Sep 2025, 2026 capex forecast is adjusted up by nearly 20% (Exhibit 3).

We model the server market and high-end GPU AI server shipment to grow a 3% and 31% CAGR, respectively, in 2025-27E (Model). We adjusted up global total server shipment for 2026 by 3%, as general server shipment was stronger than our expectation, and we see a continuous uplift in ASIC server demand. We expect high-end GPU server (with 8-GPU/server and GB200/300 rack) shipment to grow 66% this year and another 46% in 2026 (vs. 35% growth in previous version), driven by a smooth transition between GB200 to GB300 racks and robust demand outlook for next year. We adjusted up the blended ASP of high-end AI servers (8-GPU server equivalent), expecting it to increase by c.10% next year, driven by a higher mix of server rack form and the introduction of the Rubin server in 4Q26. With increasing volume shipment and ASP improvement for AI server, we model the global server market to reach c.US$500B by 2026, up from our previous forecast of ~US$450 (Exhibit 7 - Exhibit 9). For 2027, we expect the higher shipment mix from Vera Rubin racks, as well as volume shipment of ASICs servers will further drive the market to reach ~US$650B (+29% YoY).
NVIDIA server rack quarterly shipment (full year shipment 30K/58K in 2025/26): Out of the12K GB200/GB300 shipments in 4Q25, we estimate that 7K is from Foxconn and c.2K each from Quanta and Wistron/Wywnn. STOCK PERFORMANCE & ESTIMATES CHANGES ACROSS AI SERVER SUPPLY CHAIN
GPU and ASIC supply chains' performance has seen a reversal recently. We create two market-cap-weighted indices of key suppliers to compare them. The GPU-server chain (NVIDIA, Quanta, etc.) rebounded strongly post-Liberation Day, aided by eased GB200 rack bottlenecks and solid earnings/guidance. The ASIC chain (Broadcom, Marvell, Wiwynn, etc.) started catching up after Broadcom’s upbeat AI outlook in December 2024, surged more in Aug 2025 on Broadcom’s 2Q25 earnings beat and its $10B+ new customer orders, and boosted again after Google’s Gemini 3.0 debut and Meta’s reported interest in deploying Google TPUs Since Sep 1, 2025, the ASIC chain has largely outperformed with a 30% gain as of 26 Nov 2025, far outpacing the GPU chain’s 2% gain in the same period. (Exhibit 26 - Exhibit 27)
Out of the 29 stocks in the AI server supply chain in Exhibit 28 and Exhibit 29, 21 experienced an increase in valuation and 8 saw a decline in terms of their P/E ratios compared to the beginning of the year. Valuation expanded in Sep-Oct 2025, encouraged by OpenAI’s multiple operation announcements (e.g. with Nvidia and AMD), as well as rates cut. However, the valuation saw a broad pullback in November as investors concerns about AI bubble. Tech hardware sectors, including PCB, optical transceiver, power, and thermal, have all witnessed such a sentiment shift.
