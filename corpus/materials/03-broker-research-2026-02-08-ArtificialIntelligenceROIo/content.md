ROI on AI: Growth Acceleration & Returns Drive Capex Revisions
CITI'S TAKE
Accelerating enterprise adoption of AI will continue to drive Cloud Service Provider revenues and backlog growth as the high returns being generated drive further CapEx investment. While investor concerns on the ability to build and finance this spend are weighing on multiples, management commentary around the degree to which demand is outstripping supply reflects the forward curve for revenue growth and ultimately the cash flow that will come from it. We continue to believe that the best investment opportunities sit where the bottlenecks are most severe: Cloud Services, Memory, Storage, Semi Cap Equipment, and, increasingly, CPUs (Figure 6, Figure 7).
CapEx Forecasts Above Consensus, Again — The four largest hyperscalers (Meta, Google, Amazon, Microsoft) all forecast 2026 capex outlooks (Figure 1) above consensus expectations, with Meta’s \$115-135B guidance landing 21%-pts. ahead of consensus growth estimates, Google’s nearly doubling capex outlook of \$175- 185B exceeding by 65%-pts., and Amazon’s outlook for \~\$200B in capex 39%-pts. above consensus. Based on capex seasonality for the group over the past several years (Figure 5), we expect most of the increase would happen in 3Q/4Q and that 1Q numbers could be more flattish due to capacity constraints in labor, power and equipment.
Revenue & Backlog Growth Accelerating — We believe the share price reactions to hyperscalers’ post-Q4 2025 prints reflect angst around seemingly capped nearterm cloud outperformance and higher-than-expected capex ramps for 2026. In our view, this reflects more of a timing mismatch as the cloud providers’ backlog/RPO performance continues to point to robust, growing demand for AI applications and services. Collectively, Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Oracle and CoreWeave’s backlog exceeded \$1T in CQ3 (Figure 3).
Returns Driving Investment — With another quarter of data (Figure 4), hyperscalers continue to show consistent cash returns on cash invested in recent years which underlies the decision to continue to raise CapEx levels. While CROCI expansion won’t be in a straight line, given the players’ central positions in the AI ecosystem and multiple monetization levers, we expect further upward revisions to outer year top-line and earnings estimates as incremental capacity comes online.

Guide to Citi Research Fundamental Research Investment Ratings:
Citi Research stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks. Risk rating takes into account both price volatility and fundamental criteria. Stocks will either have no risk rating or a High risk rating assigned.
Investment Ratings: Citi Research investment ratings are Buy, Neutral and Sell. Our ratings are a function of analyst expectations of expected total return ("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus the dividend yield for a stock within the next 12 months. The target price is based on a 12 month time horizon. The Investment rating definitions are: Buy (1) ETR of 15% or more or 25% or more for High risk stocks; and Sell (3) for negative ETR. Any covered stock not assigned a Buy or a Sell is a Neutral (2). For stocks rated Neutral (2), if an analyst believes that there are insufficient valuation drivers and/or investment catalysts to derive a positive or negative investment view, they may elect with the approval of Citi Research management not to assign a target price and, thus, not derive an ETR. Citi Research may also suspend its rating and target price and assign "Under Review" status for other exceptional circumstances (e.g. lack of information critical to the analyst's thesis, trading suspension) affecting the company and/or trading in the company's securities. In both such situations, the rating and target price will show as “—“ and “-“ respectively in the rating history price chart.
