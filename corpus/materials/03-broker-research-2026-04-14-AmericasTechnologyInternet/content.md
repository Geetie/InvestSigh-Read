US Digital Ad Q1'26 Preview: Analyzing the Industry Debates &Framing the Operating Environment
We preview Q1 2026 earnings season for the digital advertising sub-sector of our coverage universe. In terms of overall themes from our industry work, channel checks and company conversations, we would highlight that Q1 2026 seemed to be a mixed picture breaking down across a strong/stable environment for direct response advertising trends offset by some sustained muted activity across brand advertising budgets (with the latter being more mixed to negative in the face of geopolitical dynamics late in March and into early April). In terms of the direct response environment, the broader industry strength was driven by stronger auction dynamics, robust pricing per ad impression & stable end markets (across consumer/retail, travel, gaming & the app economy). In this note, we also frame recent company announcements & our takeaways from industry conversations that occurred around the IAB NewFronts & Shop Talk in late March (which highlighted how platforms are capitalizing on key shifts in advertiser trends toward AI and the blurring of the lines between commerce and advertising). Lastly, we would expect the next 3 months to be catalyst-heavy as a series of industry/company events likely highlight platform and product evolution – Cannes Lions; GOOGL: Google Cloud Next, Google Marketing Live, YouTube Brandcast; META: potential event(s) anchored around the recent Muse Spark model launch e.g. LlamaCon & Meta Conversations in prior years.
As our industry conversations evolved intra-quarter, most initial 2026 ad budget allocations have tracked roughly inline with forecasts from late 2025. One caveat in Q1 is the typical month-to-month cadence of ad spend – typical seasonal softness in Jan & early Feb; March & early April giving us a more clear picture on annual budget trends. We continued to see momentum building behind platforms/channels with scale, programmatic solutions & AI/ML-driven automation – this strength was most pronounced intra-quarter across GOOGL (Google Search), META (Family of Apps properties), retail media and programmatic DSPs inside scaled platforms & connected TV inventory. As we head into Q2 & beyond, we also highlight a series of large scaled global events in this upcoming year (FIFA World Cup; US midterm elections; etc.) that are likely to support industry growth above typical trendline. That said, the volatile/uncertain geopolitical backdrop, while not as impactful for Q1 budgets, is weighing on visibility/confidence into Q2/Q3 budget trends. Q1 Digital Ad Checks - Mixed Overall Environment (Led by Pockets of Outperformance) with Lower Visibility into Forward Trends
As detailed above, approaching the Q1 earnings season our industry channel checks and 3rd party data sources point to an overall environment that is modestly better than feared, but very “idiosyncratic” where we believe platform performance will be dictated by relative exposure to certain pockets of ad spend (by industry, ad objective, geography, size, etc.).
A few more specific themes/narratives emerged from our industry work, which we summarize below:
n Performance by Industry Vertical – Competitive intensity within Retail & eCommerce remains high which, despite typical seasonality, drove healthy ad spend trends across the funnel in Q1. Online travel showed continued momentum in Q1 ahead of a positive events calendar in 2026 (with shifting spend by channel). Finally, CPG trends were mixed in Q1 amidst an increasingly uncertain macro/consumer spending environment and with a notable focus on performance-channels (over brand).
n Brand vs. Direct Response – As mentioned above, brand advertising spend remains volatile (being the first to be cut during periods of economic volatility) with a notable uptick in uncertainty in late Q1/into Q2 amidst a volatile geopolitical landscape (offsetting optimism around a positive events backdrop in 2026). Direct response spend, while subject to some potential spend pullback (on an absolute basis), remains relatively more resilient and tied to consumer end-demand trends.
n User growth/engagement - User growth & engagement trends remain stable in the US while seeing stronger growth in international markets and overall continuing to surprise to the upside (albeit with a slight decline in engagement trends in Q1). Short-form video continues to be a driver of incremental engagement. Based on data from SensorTower, we call out Pinterest, Instagram, Snapchat and Reddit as seeing notably strong global user growth/engagement trends in Q1.
Pricing - Based on our channel checks, Q1 pricing trends across the digital advertising landscape overall reflected mixed results, declining sequentially (inline with typical seasonality) and lapping tough year-over-year comps.

Short-Form Video Remains Key Driver of Engagement Growth for Instagram & YouTube
As we’ve discussed in prior research (link | link), short-form video remains a dominant theme within digital advertising and continues to be a key driver of engagement growth (time spent, sessions per day, etc.) for several of the major US platforms. GS Factor Profile
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
M&A Rank
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
