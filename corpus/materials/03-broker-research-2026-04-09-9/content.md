1Q26 CIO Survey – AI Priority Rising, IT Budgets Less So
Despite rising priority around AI initiatives, overall IT budgets remain relatively stable at 3.7% growth, with CIOs still net cautious on the directionality of revisions. Hyperscalers still the dominant wallet share gainers in AI and Cloud, with AI Labs and Applications making incremental gains.

Modest Improvement in 2026 IT Budget Expectations, With Software and the US Leading the Gains. Our 1Q26 CIO survey, fielded between February 3rd and March 10th, points to a modestly more constructive 2026 IT budget outlook on a sequential basis. CIOs indicate spending intentions to accelerate slightly from +3.6% in 2025 to +3.7% in 2026 (+13 bps), ( Exhibit 5 ). Under the hood, however, budget trends still look increasingly K-shaped, with Software spend continuing to drive the improvement, while Hardware, Communications, and Services are expected to decelerate YoY in 2026. CIOs expect Software budgets to accelerate +28 bps YoY to +4.1% in 2026, Services budgets to modestly decelerate -9 bps YoY to +2.0% in 2026, Hardware budgets to decelerate -21 bps YoY to +1.5% in 2026, and Communications budgets to decelerate -35 bps YoY to +2.2% growth in 2026 ( Exhibit 8 ). Regionally, US expectations for IT spending growth continue to outpace their EU counterparts, US +3.9% vs EU +3.0% ( Exhibit 13 ). Notably, US CIO budget growth expectations were revised up +34 bps QoQ, while EU CIOs' growth expectations decelerated -5 bps sequentially to +3.0% growth, suggesting widening regional bifurcation in growth expectations. Turning to key CIO sentiment indicators, trends remained mixed: with 1-year up-to-down ratio (measuring the ratio of CIOs expecting to revise their budgets higher versus lower) ticking higher to 0.8x an improvement from 0.5x in 4Q25, but still reflecting a net cautious near-term outlook – as the percentage of CIOs expecting budget increases this year 24% (up from 17% in 4Q25), is outweighed by the percentage of CIOs expecting budget decreases at 30% (from 36% in 4Q25) ( Exhibit 16 ). Additionally, the long-term secular indicator reflects a positive trend, but with a modestly waning stance, as the 3-year up-to-down ratio ticked down for the second consecutive quarter to 2.7x, down from 2.9x in 4Q25 and 3.6x in 3Q25 ( Exhibit 18 ). Only 20% of the survey responses were collected post the start of the Middle East conflict (02/28), but these responses showed no deterioration in overall growth expectations ( Exhibit 6 ).

We will continue to monitor spending trends in the coming months as pricing inflation intensifies and AI proliferates further to get a clearer picture of how CIOs are responding to these two factors. Macro – Key Takeaways
We surveyed 77 US and 23 European CIOs regarding their external IT budgets and the current spending environment. Key macro takeaways from our 1Q26 survey include:
• CIO expectations for 2026 IT budgets accelerated to +3.7% growth in 1Q26, vs 3.4% as indicated in our 4Q25 reading, with levels remaining below the 10-year average of 4.1% ( Exhibit 5 ). Regionally, US CIOs continue to expect higher growth in IT budgets at +3.9%, compared to their EU counterparts (+3.0%) ( Exhibit 13 ).
The one-year up-to-down ratio ticked up to 0.8x in 1Q26, rising from 0.5x in our 4Q25 Survey. While this sequential improvement points to an improving outlook, it still reflects a net cautious near-term outlook – as the percentage of CIOs expecting budget increases this year 23% (up from 17% in 4Q25), is outweighed by the percentage of CIOs expecting budget decreases at 30% (from 36% in 4Q25) ( Exhibit 16 ). The long-term secular indicator reflects a positive trend, but with a modestly waning stance, as the 3-year up-to-down ratio ticked down for the second consecutive quarter to 2.7x, down from 2.9x in 4Q25 and 3.6x in 3Q25 ( Exhibit 19 ).
CIOs highlighted Software as the fastest growing technology industry in 2025 at +3.8% growth, followed by Communications (+2.6%), Services (+2.1%) and Hardware (+1.7%)( Exhibit 10 ). Expectations for 2026 indicate that Software spend is expected to continue to lead the pack at +4.1% growth, +32 bps vs 4Q25 reading. Expected growth for Hardware and Communications spend also accelerated +43 bps and +7bps vs 4Q25 reading to +1.5% and 2.2%, respectively. Meanwhile, expectations for Services spend decelerated modestly -2 bps vs 4Q25 reading to 2.0% growth ( Exhibit 11 ).
• Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learnings sustains positioning on the top of the priority list in 1Q26, with 17.7% of responses, increasing +133 bps in net prioritization from 4Q25. Security Software (10.7%), Digital Transformation (9.3%), ERP Appication (5.7%), and CRM Applications (5.0%) round out the top 5, inline with priorities in 4Q25 ( Exhibit 20 ).
• Security Software (net score of +12%) remains the most defensible area of spending in 1Q26, followed by AI/ML/Process Automation (net score of 10%) and Networking Equipment (+6%). If the economy were to worsen significantly, Strategy Consulting (net score -9%), CRM Applications (net score -6%) and Data Center Build out (net score -5%) represent the projects most likely to be cut (largely consistent with our prior survey results) ( Exhibit 23 ).
As workloads shift to the cloud, Microsoft and Amazon remain the clear beneficiaries. Similar to previous surveys, Microsoft and Amazon remain best positioned to gain incremental share of IT budgets, with ServiceNow screening positively as potential share gainers over the next 3 years ( Exhibit 25 , Exhibit 26 ). Conversely, Dell, Oracle, and Cisco have the highest risk of losing incremental share of IT budgets in 2026, while Dell, Cisco, and HPE screen as the largest share losers over the next three years, consistent with previous surveys.

1Q26 CIO Survey results highlight a less severe Y/Y deceleration in enterprise IT Hardware budgets in 2026 after last quarters very cautious survey. Our 1Q26 CIO Survey shows that CIOs now expect 2026 hardware spend to grow 1.5% Y/Y, up ~50bps from expectations for 1.0% Y/Y growth - a 60 quarter low (ex-COVID) in our 4Q25 CIO Survey ( Exhibit 48 ). That said, CIOs still expect 2026 hardware spending growth to decelerate 20bps Y/Y, the second largest Y/Y deceleration amongst all tech sub-sectors behind Communications. The+50bps positive revision higher to 2026 Hardware budget growth could reflect CIOs taking into account higher pricing from vendors as a result memory cost inflation, but we will continue to monitor memory pricing and CIO purchasing dynamics in the coming months to get a clearer picture on how full year spending/budgets are trending.
CIOs expect Storage and PC peripherals spending to accelerate in 2026, while PC and Server spend is expected to decelerate. Relative to our 3Q25 CIO Survey, Hardware domain experts revised 2026 spending growth expectations higher for Storage and Servers, but revised PCs lower, with 2026 (1) Storage spending growth expectations 10bps higher than 3Q25, at +3.7% Y/Y, (2) Server spending growth expectations 50bps higher than 3Q25, at +2.5% Y/Y, and (3) PC spending growth expectations 180bps lower than our 3Q25 Survey, at +1.0% Y/Y ( Exhibit 53 ).
