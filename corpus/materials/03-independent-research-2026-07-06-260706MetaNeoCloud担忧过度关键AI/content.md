Q2 closed with a chip-led melt-up,and Q3 promptly punished the winners. Wednesday's headline that Meta may commercialize its surplus AI compute as a NeoCloud-style business set off a two-day unwind across AI infrastructure names, with the market reading the move as the first real sign of a compute glut.
We disagree with that read. Meta is monetizing prior-generation H100/H200 capacity while simultaneously locking in roughly 1.6 GW of new capacity from Crusoe for the GB300/Rubin era. The compute market is splittng into tiers by GPU generation, and the newest training silicon remains scarce. Old GPUs finding a second life in inference and external compute sales tells us the asset base is being commercialized, and we think this week's selloff misprices that.
With that being said, we have been flagging the risk of a potential change in direction in our Weekly Group Calls with Institutional subscribers for the past two weeks,at least in the near term,from fund-flow and narrative perspectives. We first highlighted that major HF and LO buy flow in AI semis already completed in May, while retail leverage and options could dominate volatility from June, making the momentum much more vulnerable. We then emphasized the importance of capable open-source models like GLM 5.2 in the overall market, followed by a thorough discussion of the possibility that memory price hikes could crowd out other AI capex budgets. Despite the sharp drawdown over the past week, the market would likely remain sensitive to headline risks in the near term until we have more clarity on upcoming CSP earnings.We would continue to look for more color across the value chain.
