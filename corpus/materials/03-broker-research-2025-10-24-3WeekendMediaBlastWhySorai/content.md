By Mark Shmulik and Wenhuan Chang
AI Slop! Television was supposed to rot our brains. Video games? The cause of violent behavior. Reality TV ruined television. Why would anyone want to watch user-generated cat videos on YouTube? Social media’s infinite content feed was the bane of human existence. And TikTok dance videos were the crack to the rest of media’s cocaine run. AI Slop? With such an affectionate term coined, AI-generated content appears to be in good company.
OpenAI’s Sora has grabbed the headlines launching to the top of app store download charts and impressively holding this top spot weeks later. But Sora isn’t the only AI-generated video tool in town, Meta’s Vibes, Google’s Veo 3.1, and Grok’s Imagine are just a few of other common products in the market. Is AI slop then the future of media? And for Meta investors, is this the biggest risk to their ‘social’ media empire of engagement since TikTok? We dive into these questions in this week’s Media Blast.
SORA USHERS IN THE AI SLOP ERA
Meta stock has faced a bit of pressure since the launch of Sora nearly a month. We seem to be in a market where app store success drives perception. And with Sora’s highly successful launch following Meta’s more muted Vibes launch, concerns that a new type of media - one where you can create realistic videos using your likeliness and those of permitted friends, celebrities, and characters - was poised to peel away the most valuable resource we have from Meta’s grip - Time!
But what if we were to tell you that Sora may actually be great for Meta? To get there, you need to re-frame what Sora and AI-generated video is - Sora is CGI for UGC. Digital cameras removed the barrier studios had over video content creation, and YouTube and Instagram offered these creators a place to showcase their creations. CapCut by ByteDance (private), and eventually Edits by Meta, did the same for short-form video and suddenly enabling anyone to create seamless transitions, animations, optical illusions,
and overlap music and text. What Sora is, is a way to give creators the power of those impressive CGI effects that until now were reserved for your favorite Marvel film or HBO series.
What this also means is that Sora is a creator tool - more CapCut than TikTok, and what does a creator seek to do with their art? They need distribution, and are there any better distribution channels around than Meta, TikTok, and YouTube. This influx of novel content broadens the library that these distribution platforms content recommendation algos can choose from to deepen user engagement and time spent.
WHAT IS SORA
On Sept. 30th, OpenAI (private) released the Sora app, an AI-powered text-to-video generator capable of producing hyperrealistic videos up to one minute long from text prompts and images. The app quickly went viral and became the top downloaded app on the US Apple App Store. According to Sora head Bill Peebles, Sora reached 1 million downloads in five days after launch, even faster than ChatGPT. After its release, social media was flooded with clips made using Sora, featuring a variety of humorous and imaginative scenes.
Sora is viewed as a game-changing AI tool, making powerful production capabilities more accessible than ever. Its rapid popularity sent a buzz through the tech world with plenty of nervous Meta investors wondering if this was TikTok 2.0 with a powerful new rival in the social media space. Indeed, Sora opens to a video feed of AI-generated content. The feed itself is similar to other shortform video apps that have an algorithmically driven ‘For You’ feed with the openness to limit the videos to people you follow, to pick a mood, or to switch to a time-based feed. The “social” element here is the ability to create videos using the likeness of approved friends, celebrities, and fictional characters.

SORA THE CREATOR TOOL - ARE YOU NOT ENTERTAINED?
The two fascinating debates on Sora are (1) can they scale a video product of only AI-generated video content as a viable alternative to Meta, YouTube, and TikTok? And (2) how many of us actually want to be creators vs. simply be entertained?
Sora is a creator tool. The point, is for you to get the app, make your on AI video content, and experience the magic of AI and being a creator. While the app opens to the video feed, it’s there to inspire you and stimulate ideas of concepts you can go and create. The hope, is that once you create content, and bring your friends along to co-create content where you’re the writer, director, and star, you generate enough interesting content that you marvel at your creations, share them on the platform, and head back to the feed to find your next source of inspiration. In other words, the dream is that content creation and consumption blend together.
The structural question facing Sora first, is how many of us actually want to make stuff? We have a decent example of this 90-9-1 rule in the wild: Bytedance’s CapCut, the free, flexible video editing app. CapCut provides an easy-to-use set of features such as trimming, merging clips, adding music, filters, special effects, templates, etc., making it suitable for TikTok/Instagram content creators. While CapCut’s global download volume is impressively high at nearly ~60% of TikTok, Daily users are only 11% of TikTok’s - we all dream of being Hollywood creators, but in the end it’s just a dream.
In the meantime, let’s say you’re one of these 10% of creators. Better yet, you’re the 1% that has decided to make this your career. You’ve tinkered, toiled, and stitched together increase AI-generated videos. You NEED to get these videos in front of a bigger audience because that’s where you monetize, so where do you go? Welcome to Reels.
Social media is an archaic term that needs to be retired. Media has evolved and consumption platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube are where we go to be entertained. If you don’t like it, just remember that every new media channel through history was villainized. These platforms are not only optimized for viewing, but the algorithmically driven content recommendation engines create highly personalized, nearly infinite streams of content. When you open Instagram now, Reels is the first tab and once you’re there content just plays as it does on TikTok and YouTube Shorts.
We’ve gone backwards to old media all over again. Televisions used to just play content when you switched them on, and if you didn’t like the content you just switched the channel… seem familiar? Cable boxes and supposedly “smart” TV’s made us go seek out the content across hundreds of channels and streaming apps.
TikTok fundamentally challenged YouTube and Meta because it revolutionized the media distribution model - it took us back to zero click. TikTok also broke the mold of content feeds built around connects, back to our most basic instinct… entertain me, powered by algorithmically-driven, short-form video discovery model, which proved to be more effective due to its more broad, dynamic, engaging, and personalized content recommendations. Sora doesn’t appear to be changing the distribution model, though maybe setting content moods and genres may take hold. But we believe Sora’s biggest contribution to the media ecosystem is delivering more content volume, and yes high quality content, for the media distribution algos to choose from.

AI EMPOWERMENT > AI SLOP
A common concern we hear that Sora can result in “AI slop”— lowquality, incoherent AI generated video that don’t comply with the laws of physics and comes out looking ridiculous. There’s definitely some AI slop out there, but this is just a moment in time. All one has to do is look at the progress AI video tools have made in just a few short years - the water ripple on Sora is uncanny, one of the toughest feats for AI to pull off.
We get it, Love Island may not be for everyone, but clearly there’s enough of you out there that care whether Shakira and Harry make it. The same holds true with every new media channel - Mr. Beast created a YouTube show that offers contents cash for crazier and crazier challenges - sounds like reality TV? Well Mr. Beast has more channel subscribers than all of Netflix combined.
How about TikTok? Well here’s where Sora gets very interesting. If you Google (or ChatGPT) the most viewed videos on TikTok, a creator named Zach King sits rights at the top owning 4 of the top 10 most viewed videos on the platform. Zach’s videos are known for their mastery of complex, professional-level visual effects. Hollywood isn’t dead, it’s just moved to a different medium with a different group of creators. We’ve democratized CGI for UGC, so you too can make your own Game of Thrones. Audiences appreciate high quality content, and Sora and other AI video generation tools will likely empower the next group of creators to entertain us - the question is just where? You know our answer.

VALUATION METHODOLOGY
Meta Platforms Inc.
We value Meta on a Price Target of $900 using a 50/50 combination of 2027e EV/Sales multiple of 9x, and a DCF using a WACC of 9% and a terminal growth of 3.5%. We consider Meta primarily a digital advertising business and benchmark valuation to comparable peers in this industry set.
Alphabet Inc

RISKS
Meta Platforms Inc.
Downside risk to our target price:
• Meta is currently under investigation in multiple regulatory proceedings now mostly focused on anti-trust domestically and abroad.
• Signs that we’re reaching maturity in digital ads, increased competition as alternatives emerge with compelling ad ROIs, or further privacy related changes (e.g., Cookie depreciation) could all put press on Meta's revenue outlook resulting in compressing revenue growth and de-rating.
• Meta's metaverse aspirations could lead to margin headwinds and potentially minimal ROIC, which could negatively affect both estimates and multiples.
Alphabet Inc
Upside risk to our price target:
• Faster than expected revenue growth tied to digital advertising recovery and idiosyncratic efforts or better than expected margins tied to recent effort to slow down hiring and small scale lay-offs.
• Favorable regulatory outcomes further entrenching Google’s incumbency positions across both Search and Network.
Downside risk to our price target:
Terminal risk narratives tied to Search disintermediation, either from negative headlines or share loss (users and/or revenue) in search. If search were to suffer topline changes it could have a trickle effect across the business and have a material impact on GOOGL's ad revenues and we would expect further de-rating in the stock.
