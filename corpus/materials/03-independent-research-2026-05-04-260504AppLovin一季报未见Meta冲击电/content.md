APP Budget Trends
We recently interviewed two gaming company experts and four advertising agency experts on recent AppLovin budget trends.
1. Advertising Agency A
a. AppLovin Budget Growth
i. 1Q2025–4Q2025 YoY growth: 56% / 30.4% / 97% / 68.6%
ii. 1Q2026–2Q2026 YoY growth: 132.8% / 65.5%
iii. At year-end 2025, the agency was forecasting 2026 YoY growth of 44%; actual results have come in well above expectations.
b. Drivers of 1Q26 Outperformance
i. Gaming ads: ROAS for gaming clients improved meaningfully, particularly for high-ARPU hardcore gaming clients. 1Q26 demonstrated a notably stronger seasonal growth trend versus 1Q25.

ii. E-commerce ads: Approximately 32% of total agency budget on AppLovin in 1Q26 came from e-commerce advertisers running campaigns through the AXON Ad Manager.
iii. CTV: For the first time, the agency observed a meaningful CTV contribution. Web-based gaming advertisers and DTC advertisers began deploying CTV inventory on the web side; importantly, this traffic is not gaming ad traffic.
c. Drivers of 1Q26 Gaming Outperformance
i. There is a direct linkage to gaming advertisers beginning to adopt Apple Link-out.
1. “We estimate that since December, Apple Link-out has driven a \~5.5% incremental lift to the overall iOS gaming ad market, with the vast majority of the incremental dollars flowing to GOOG and APP, and Unity also benefiting partially.”
2. “We calculate that Link-out offsets gaming company costs by \~2% in Japan and \~9% in the US. However, advertisers do not redeploy 100% of these savings into ad spend; a portion is reinvested into game development.”
ii. Gaming real-time bidding (RTB) capabilities have also improved meaningfully.
1. AppLovin’s new AI algorithms have lowered user acquisition costs through highly effective bid shading. Whereas previously AppLovin needed to bid 50% above Unity or 30% above other bidders to win an impression, this year it is winning at premiums of just 5–7%.
2. The direct result is that AI-enabled dynamic bid prediction has compressed the ultimate cost of acquisition.
3. AXON’s CPA has declined 19% YoY, and D28 retention has improved 12.8% YoY.
d. CTV — A First-Time Datapoint
This quarter marks the first time in our two years of covering AppLovin that an agency has cited CTV as a contributor to upside. Notably, more than one agency mentioned this.

i. CTV here refers primarily to traffic from internet-connected smart TVs, used mostly to address advertisers’ web-side ad placement needs. CTV is not a standalone product; rather, CTV inventory is integrated into the AXON platform for real-time bidding, with the resulting performance lift flowing through.
ii. CTV traffic has existed for some time and can be traced back to roughly late 2024. However, the productization that maximizes inventory aggregation and improves overall fill rate has only ramped from 4Q25 into 2026.
iii. The current core customer base consists of web-based gaming advertisers and DTC advertisers, and this could pressure ad budgets at other CTV platforms.
iv. How the agency persuades advertisers to allocate to CTV:
1. “We start by showing them performance data. Within the AppLovin ecosystem, all traffic — whether CTV or other web placements — is powered by AppLovin’s own AXON data engine. So the core infrastructure capability, including AI-driven efficiency gains, benefits not only mobile but also broader PC traffic.”
2. “We are now optimizing campaigns toward web traffic acquisition, allowing advertisers to see a meaningful CAC advantage when driving users to their web properties via marketing. Compared to legacy products such as Mintegral, the cost can come in at roughly 1/5 to 1/3 of competing platforms.”
3. “After testing, advertisers have found the performance solid and have naturally scaled up web-side spend.”
4. “Beginning this year, AppLovin has rolled out AI-based interactive creative products on the web, extending its SparkLabs AI capabilities to the web. This enables advertisers to generate more cost-efficient creatives in real time, which has further lifted ultimate ad performance.”
2. Advertising Agency B
a. AppLovin Budget Growth
i. 2Q2025 QoQ +7%; 3Q2025 QoQ +10%; 4Q2025 QoQ +9–9.5%

ii. 1Q2026 QoQ +1%; 2Q2026 QoQ +5–8%
b. Reasons 1Q2026 QoQ Growth Was Below Expectations
i. AppLovin has clearly become more aggressive in direct sales, taking share from agencies. Agencies do not enjoy a strong moat versus AppLovin in gaming. In 1Q2026, AppLovin rolled out additional incentives aimed at acquiring advertisers directly.
ii. E-commerce ad growth was below expectations. Many e-commerce clients hit a ceiling during Black Friday and pulled back e-commerce ad budgets in 1Q26.
iii. AppLovin’s AI creative optimization tools have not yet shown clear improvement for e-commerce clients.
c. 2Q2026 QoQ growth has reaccelerated. The most important driver is ecommerce advertisers reaccelerating in 2Q2026.
d. AppLovin has made meaningful AI progress over the past six months, and the agency remains very constructive on the company.
i. The SparkLabs creative self-serve platform has reasonable appeal among smaller and mid-size clients. Large clients, however, are largely indifferent, as they already spend significantly on creative through other platforms or independent vendors.
ii. The Self Service product has improved continuously since 2H2025. Functionally, it still feels somewhat Excel-like compared with META and Google, but the underlying capability is genuinely improving. The agency notes strong AI vertical-fit in categories like consumer electronics and DTC.
iii. AppLovin has been testing prospecting (new-user acquisition) campaigns for SMB DTC advertisers. While slightly more expensive than the major platforms, it can help budget-constrained SMBs identify net-new users, generate opportunity cost, and convert via data-driven targeting. Hit rates in SMB DTC are reasonable and continue to improve.
iv. AppLovin is also testing creative libraries and site-building capabilities. On one side, this includes external ad creative editing and landing page builders; on the other side, it includes landing-page heatmap analytics and user conversion path analysis. These moves essentially mirror META’s and

Google’s playbooks. While AppLovin’s depth does not match these incumbents, the company is investing meaningful capital and R&D resources in this area.
3. Advertising Agency C
a. AppLovin Budget Growth and Advertiser Count
i. September: 24 advertisers; December: 45 advertisers; 3Q2025 monthly spend: \$2.38mn; 4Q2025 monthly spend: \$3.8mn.
ii. 1Q2026: 52 advertisers, in line with growth expectations. Monthly spend came in at \$4.52mn, ahead of the \$4.28mn forecast.
iii. 2Q2026: 59 advertisers expected, with monthly spend revised upward to \$5.47mn.
iv. 3Q2026: 65 advertisers expected, with monthly spend revised upward to \$6.51mn.
b. Gaming and E-Commerce Ad Trends
i. AppLovin gaming ad growth came in below expectations, with gaming acquisition costs rising rapidly.
ii. “We believe part of AppLovin’s strong e-commerce growth is coming from cannibalizing share from gaming advertisers.”
iii. “From AppLovin’s perspective, this may not matter, because their take rate from non-gaming is materially higher than gaming, so they earn more dollars overall. But I’m flagging this because the e-commerce uplift is not 100% accretive for AppLovin; some portion is cannibalizing their own gaming ad revenue.”
4. Advertising Agency D
a. AppLovin Budget Growth
i. 1Q2026 growth came in above expectations. While e-commerce declined QoQ in 1Q2026, gaming was very strong.
ii. 2Q2026 growth is not expected to decelerate from 1Q2026; gaming remains robust, and e-commerce is reaccelerating QoQ.
iii. The agency saw a CTV contribution to AppLovin for the first time.
b. The agency has not seen advertisers shifting toward META; instead, it sees more META budget rotating into AppLovin.

c. For most advertisers with gaming exposure, AppLovin has now become the third-largest ad platform. This implies advertisers must allocate more dedicated resources, and the industry will see a growing number of media buyers/optimizers focused specifically on AppLovin. New gaming companies are also adopting AppLovin as a must-have channel.
5. Gaming Company A
a. AppLovin’s budget share has continued to rise over recent quarters.
i. Current budget allocation is split into GOOG, META, and Others, at 40% / 30% / 30% respectively. Within Others, AppLovin accounts for \~40%.
ii. For each game title, META is used predominantly during the testing phase, with GOOG and Others added later. AppLovin’s share rises as games move into a more mature lifecycle phase.
iii. AppLovin allocation varies by game lifecycle stage:
1. Year 1 to 1.5 post-launch: Others typically \~20%
2. Year 1.5 to year 3: Others gradually increases to \~35%
3. New expansion or new season: Others share dips slightly
iv. As legacy games take a larger share of total bookings, the Others bucket — and AppLovin within it — continues to gain share. AppLovin has a more material impact on META than on GOOG.
b. Versus META, AppLovin shows extremely high CTR but low CVR, with overall CPM roughly in line. There was little change in this dynamic in 1Q26.
c. Apple Link-out Impact
i. Russia has fully prohibited iOS payments effective April 1, with all flows now required to route through third parties.
ii. In the US, \~10% of iOS bookings have shifted to Link-out from December through today.
iii. In China, effective March 15, the iOS payment take rate has been reduced from 30% to 25%, and from 15% to 12% for small developers.
iv. In Southeast Asia, 25–30% of iOS bookings have shifted to Link-out.
d. US Link-out adoption has been slower than expected, primarily due to high third-party costs.

i. Bypassing the Apple in-app payment still requires payment to third-party payment processors. The third-party payment take rate averages 10–15%.
ii. This affects the in-game promotional bundles game companies can offer users. If third-party payment costs decline, game companies can offer richer promotional bundles.
iii. Additionally, third-party payment requires a 3-step conversion funnel (official site → page → payment), which is far less seamless than Apple Pay’s double-click flow.
6. Gaming Company B
a. Third-Party Payment Costs
i. Combined third-party payment processing plus tax costs are close to 10%, certainly not as high as 15%. In the US, for example, sales tax adds 5–6%.
ii. Apple Pay’s 30% take rate also excludes tax, which game companies must bear separately.
iii. Third-party payment costs vary by region.
b. Following the EPIC court ruling in December, the expert’s company began testing Link-out and now estimates that 15–20% of iOS bookings have shifted to Link-out.
c. “We’ve only heard of Apple pressuring very small game companies on link-out behavior; we have not heard of any large game company facing meaningful pushback from Apple during this period.”
d. Share Shifts Within MAX
i. The expert’s company is also a MAX customer and has observed that AppLovin’s DSP share within MAX declined slightly this quarter.
ii. “This makes me even more constructive on AppLovin. AppLovin’s DSP share within MAX declining means MAX’s overall efficiency has improved and gaming ad demand has strengthened. AppLovin no longer needs to lift its own DSP share within MAX to sustain current growth rates.”
7. E-Commerce Platform Expert
a. Following the start of data collaboration with AppLovin, AppLovin’s share of total ad budget has continued to rise and has now approached TikTok’s share.
b. No meaningful CTV contribution observed.

APP E-Commerce Progress
In our note published in mid-December following Black Friday, we described AppLovin’s Black Friday performance as very strong. However, in our 4Q25 Preview, we noted that e-commerce ad spend declined post-Black Friday, falling back to roughly pre-Black Friday levels. Beginning in late February, following an ecommerce algorithm update, we have observed sustained growth in e-commerce ad budgets.
1. Advertising Agency A: Average client spend on AppLovin grew modestly QoQ in 1Q26 but began to reaccelerate QoQ in 2Q26.
a. 4Q25 had 7 e-commerce advertisers; as of mid-April, that figure stands at 17– 18.
b. While 1Q26 added many new advertisers, large 4Q25 advertisers cut budgets in 1Q26, weighing on overall e-commerce QoQ growth.
c. As newly onboarded clients ramp, QoQ growth in 2Q26 is reaccelerating.
d. E-commerce share of budget was 32% in 1Q26, rising to 34% in 2Q26.
e. Ad Manager has been meaningfully optimized, including one-click feedback, human customer service, Self Service, automated bidding, and deeply integrated data attribution. These improvements are visibly reflected in advertiser budget acceleration.
f. Beyond significant integration progress with Shopify, AppLovin has begun deeper partnerships with attribution platforms such as AppsFlyer.
2. Advertising Agency B: E-commerce progression is gradual rather than stepfunction.
a. While e-commerce declined QoQ in 1Q26, e-commerce has clearly reaccelerated QoQ in 2Q26.
i. In 1Q26, supply, e-commerce site traffic quality, and overall operations were all impacted by demand pull-forward into Black Friday and Christmas, requiring a recovery period.
ii. Many of these factors recovered in 2Q26, with both consumer purchasing power and merchant SKU supply showing clear improvement.

iii. 2Q26 represents the second-best advertiser promotional window outside of Black Friday last year.
b. E-Commerce Algorithm Improvements
i. The change in new-versus-existing customer differentiation is fundamentally about how AppLovin understands the relationship between users and advertisers (e.g., past click and purchase behaviors) and the corresponding label tagging. This tagging is not particularly difficult and can be derived from third-party data or app store signals. Clear improvement has already been seen here.
ii. The harder problem is what happens after tagging: how to precisely match label, user value assessment, bid level, and personalized (”thousand faces for thousand people”) creative. That remains the most difficult piece.
3. Advertising Agency C
a. E-Commerce Ad Progress
i. Self Service advertisers contributed roughly 10 percentage points of growth in 1Q. We grew 19% QoQ from 4Q to 1Q, which is a strong figure given that most platforms decline QoQ from 4Q to 1Q. Posting +19% is solid.
ii. Self Service contributed 10 of the 19 percentage points, which means fully managed advertisers (those onboarded prior to last September who didn’t need to wait for Self Service) grew roughly 9% QoQ. So in simple terms, our larger and more mature advertisers, if they sustain \~9% QoQ throughout the year, would deliver 40–50% full-year growth. That’s still a good number, but clearly slower than last year.
iii. This implies that any incremental upside above this needs to come from Self Service. Self Service clearly has substantial runway, as in theory it can scale very rapidly across both advertiser count and per-advertiser spend.
b. E-Commerce Algorithm Improvements
i. “I’ve heard about it. It’s hard for me to assess directly. We did raise our numbers. The reasoning was broader. I’m primarily watching Self Service advertisers, and the platform genuinely keeps getting better. That’s how they continue to grow per-advertiser spend and attract new advertisers. I haven’t broken it out in the numbers, but yes, it’s a contributing factor.”

4. Advertising Agency D
a. During the Black Friday peak, e-commerce ad budgets reached roughly 2x pre-Black Friday levels.
b. Spend declined steadily post-Black Friday, falling back to pre-Black Friday levels by January through the first half of February.
c. As the e-commerce algorithm has improved, advertisers can now meaningfully target and acquire new users, addressing the prior core pain point of being unable to differentiate new from existing customers. This has driven sustained e-commerce ad budget growth.
d. Current e-commerce ad budgets are now approaching Black Friday peak levels.
5. E-Commerce Platform Expert
a. The Platform’s Partnership History with AppLovin
i. The partnership began in 2024, and the company was among the first batch of testing customers for e-commerce ads. Prior to e-commerce ads, the company had been a long-standing AppLovin App Download Campaign customer.
ii. E-commerce ad testing began in 2Q25, but without formal data collaboration in place and given internal data security concerns, largescale spend did not occur.
iii. True large-scale e-commerce ad spend began during Black Friday in 4Q25.
iv. 1Q26 marked the shift to a refined operations phase, with systematic improvement in AppLovin e-commerce ad efficiency.
b. AppLovin’s Budget Share at the Expert’s Company Over Time
i. 1Q25–2Q25: AppLovin budget share was 6–8%, primarily App Download Campaigns. During the testing period, e-commerce ads accounted for \~20– 25% of AppLovin spend.
ii. 3Q25–4Q25: AppLovin budget share rose to 9–11%, with e-commerce ads reaching 50% of AppLovin spend. At the Black Friday peak, e-commerce reached 65% of AppLovin spend.
iii. Post-Black Friday peak decline phase: e-commerce ads accounted for \~60– 62% of AppLovin spend.

iv. January–February 2026: AppLovin budget share at 11%; e-commerce ad mix stable at 55–60%.
v. April 2026: AppLovin budget share at 12–13%, approaching TikTok’s share; e-commerce mix at 60–65%.
c. The company began materially scaling e-commerce ad budget on AppLovin only after launching formal data collaboration.
i. The company went through a lengthy negotiation with AppLovin during Black Friday, alongside a legal-led data security review.
1. The company was concerned about potential data leakage. If, for example, SKU preference data for specific users were leaked to competing e-commerce platforms, the company could face direct user churn risk.
2. The company viewed AppLovin’s e-commerce data security handling as less mature than META, GOOG, and TikTok, requiring a longer legal review process.
3. By early December, the expert believed negotiations were near completion, and the company maintained the position that any data leakage incident would result in immediate termination of the ecommerce data collaboration.
ii. After clearing legal review, the company formally began data collaboration with AppLovin.
1. Pre-Black Friday December: began formally returning shopping behavior data to AppLovin, but limited to a small number of categories and brands.
2. Throughout 2026: gradually expanded to additional brands and categories and broadened the user age cohorts.
iii. Returned data includes:
1. User label data and user behavior counts (clicks, add-to-cart, browsing).
2. Purchase data and repurchase data.
3. The company does not return purchase behavior for users who entered the platform through META or TikTok; only users directly attributable to AppLovin are returned.

4. AppLovin participates deeply in the data collaboration, helping the company optimize its LTV models and providing Email/Push reactivation recommendations.
d. Recent E-Commerce Algorithm Improvements
i. Algorithm improvements have indeed been observed.
ii. AppLovin’s user journey tracking capability has improved meaningfully, evolving from focusing only on Install/Purchase to incorporating purchase value and LTV.
iii. SKU recommendation accuracy has also improved meaningfully, supporting advertisers in mining repurchase value from existing customers.
iv. Following data integration with AppLovin, the new-versus-existing customer differentiation problem has been substantially solved.
1. The primary tool used is New User Optimization. Many SMB advertisers are unfamiliar with how to deploy this tool.
2. Advertisers must split campaigns and self-manage the new-customer mix.
e. Self Service Product Progress
i. The expert views the following functionalities as highly valuable:
1. Automated creative rotation: product cards, images, and videos rotate automatically, saving optimizer workload.
2. Automated event switching: automatically selects between Install, Purchase, and user conversion value as the optimization event.
3. Automated reporting output: replaces manual analysis by media buyers (ROI analysis, revenue value analysis).
ii. Although the company has its own automated buying products, AppLovin’s Self Service feels more native and seamless within the AppLovin buying environment than the company’s in-house product.
iii. Junior media buyers may be displaced, but full automation remains unrealistic.
f. SparkLabs Improvements

i. Creative generated by SparkLabs delivers ROI 10–15 percentage points higher than creative generated by the company’s in-house ad middleplatform.
ii. SparkLabs handles both video and image creative; video output is solid.
iii. SparkLabs skews more toward immersive, interactive creative and is wellsuited to gaming users, while Gemini and Seedance are more generalpurpose.
g. We Discussed With the Expert Why Many E-Commerce Companies Cut AppLovin Budget in 1Q26 and Reaccelerated in 2Q26
i. Large advertisers crowded out smaller ones during Black Friday:
1. AppLovin’s traffic allocation during Black Friday skewed toward large advertisers.
2. CPC and CPM rising 30–40% is fully normal.
3. Large advertisers have more sophisticated long-term operational capability and can offer larger discounts to attract users.
4. Large advertisers have stronger data capabilities to differentiate new from existing customers.
ii. January is a structural low point for e-commerce advertising; both ad demand and ROI decline. SMB advertisers’ Black Friday performance often falls short of expectations, leading to further budget cuts.
iii. Following e-commerce algorithm improvements in late February, budgets should reaccelerate.
h. CTV Advertising
i. CTV accounts for less than 5% of the company’s AppLovin spend.
ii. The mix has consistently stayed below 5%, reaching 5% during Black Friday.
iii. No clear CTV functionality changes were observed in 1Q26.
i. On the Common SMB Advertiser Complaint About AppLovin’s Lack of BD Support
i. AppLovin firmly believes AI can handle all media buying and that a large BD organization is unnecessary. This may prove correct in the long run but is very challenging in the short term.

ii. For example, many advertisers cannot differentiate new from existing customers precisely because there are no BD reps to educate them. Even today, many advertisers do not know which tool to use to differentiate new versus existing customers.
META’s Entry Into Gaming and Implications for AppLovin
In our META Preview, we noted that we had not observed any impact on AppLovin from META Audience Network, nor any signals of META preparing to launch non-IDFA bidding.
1. Advertising Agency A
a. The sample size is small. Only very small gaming clients have shown interest in Audience Network, and no meaningful budget growth has been observed.
2. Advertising Agency B
a. Clients do not directly ask whether Audience Network is good. Since 2024, META has been pushing the Advantage+ suite, and the core philosophy is that advertisers no longer select specific ad placements.
b. Advertisers simply tell the platform their target audience profile, geo, and desired reach, and the platform delivers the goal within 7, 15, or even 30 days.
c. During delivery, the platform does not disclose how much spend goes to Feed, Story, or Reels; it only reports overall reach performance.
d. Through this packaging, META has effectively activated Audience Network inventory for some time and has resolved the prior client complaint that “the volume bought wasn’t sufficient; quality was good but supply was constrained, making it hard to meet user growth and conversion KPIs.”
e. Audience Network has also expanded its set of partners (analogous to GOOG’s Display Network or industry DSP platforms).
f. Beginning in 4Q25, there has been some positive feedback. Some advertisers with tighter budgets but a need to maintain volume actively pursue cost optimization. For example, where a normal bid of \$2/click previously cleared, in tight-budget periods, advertisers may cap at \$0.80 or even \$0.50, which

generally cannot win core Facebook or Instagram inventory and is forced into Audience Network.
3. Advertising Agency C
a. Audience Network has not really started yet. 1Q26 mix is 2.1%, the same as 4Q25. That said, META is clearly investing more aggressively. META, via CloudX, is pushing hard to control more inventory in the gaming ecosystem. They will see some success, although early traction may be slow because many publishers want to work with CloudX and META but don’t want to be first movers; they want others to test first.
b. Gaming advertisers are seeing pricing on AppLovin rise materially, so they are pushing more dollars toward META. Combining these factors, I expect we will see growth in Facebook Audience Network. I haven’t formalized a forecast, but META is doing many things and I expect some results. By year-end, it could be 2.3% or potentially 3%.
c. It’s hard to characterize this as ambitious or merely incremental. It depends heavily on CloudX’s success in securing inventory control. But yes, META is putting in more effort. It could become a new incremental growth lever for META, particularly given the pace of META’s ad improvements. They are essentially in an AI arms race with AppLovin to determine who can produce the most relevant ads, and I would bet on META. While AppLovin has more control over the gaming ecosystem, META is better at targeting. So yes, I expect this to become a growth lever for META later this year.
4. Gaming Company B
a. The expert has heard that META may push Audience Network in 2H, with billing options based on ROAS or MAIA. However, based on conversations, the expert does not believe META will move to non-IDFA bidding.
b. The base is currently very small and will not be a focus area in 1H.
5. Our Take
That said, we do view META as having added substantial headcount to its competitive efforts against AppLovin and as having materially elevated the priority of this initiative. We expect more DSP features competing with AppLovin to launch over the next year. While this may not necessarily impact AppLovin directly, it does meaningfully complicate the narrative.

AppLovin 1Q26 Quarterly Outlook
1. E-Commerce Progress
a. We estimate 1Q26 e-commerce declined 11% QoQ, driven by:
i. 1Q26 being a seasonally soft e-commerce quarter
ii. SMB advertisers having a poor Black Friday experience and likely meaningfully cutting 1Q26 budgets
b. That said, we believe AppLovin e-commerce has shown clear improvement starting in late February. We estimate 2Q26 e-commerce will grow 23% QoQ, exceeding 4Q25 e-commerce ad revenue.
c. Following the rollout of Self Service and the resolution of e-commerce algorithm issues, new-versus-existing customer differentiation, and interplatform data collaboration, we believe AppLovin e-commerce will continue to improve.
2. Gaming Progress
a. We view Apple Store link-out as a long-term tailwind for AppLovin, Unity, META, and GOOG. In 1Q26, 10–15% of gaming ad budget has already implemented Apple Store link-out.
b. We do not believe AppLovin was negatively impacted by META gaming ads in 1Q26. We continue to see gaming advertisers shifting budget from META to AppLovin.
3. New Growth Vector: CTV
This quarter is the first time we have heard agencies attribute upside to CTV contribution, and from more than one agency.
4. Revenue Beat Expectation
We expect AppLovin’s 1Q26 revenue beat to exceed the historical average beat magnitude. The company’s 2Q26 outlook and its commentary on the long-term ecommerce growth trajectory will strengthen market confidence in AppLovin’s ecommerce ads narrative.
5. Sentiment Shift
Last quarter, we noted that we expected AppLovin’s 4Q25 in-quarter results to be modest but its forward guidance to be strong. However, given negative results from RDDT, RBLX, and TTWO, we became significantly more concerned about

AppLovin’s narrative at the time. AppLovin appeared to be on the wrong side of the AI trade. This quarter, we believe that dynamic has been partially alleviated.
6. META Competition Risk
The competitive issue with META remains difficult to resolve in the near term and is likely to grow more complex over time. As long as META views this initiative as important and continues to invest, AppLovin will be pulled into META-related discussions every quarter.
