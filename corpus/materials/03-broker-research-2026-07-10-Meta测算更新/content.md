Media reports (link) suggest Meta plans to 1) double its datacenter capacity to 
14GW in 2027, and 2) accelerate mass production of its in-house chip "Iris" to 
starting in September, i.e. in two months. While the knee-jerk reaction on the shares 
has been to the downside this morning, we think that is overdone for the following 
reasons.
Adding 7GW of capacity in 2027 implies AI-related capex of ~$245bn, assuming 
each GW of capacity costs ~$35bn. Some of that capex spend likely will be spread 
over the 2026-2027 period, but, at the very least, could imply 2027 capex at north 
of $200bn. Although Street consensus for 2027 capex is well below -- at ~$160-
$165bn, our prior expectations were in the ~$185bn range, and we believe the 
buyside was much higher, at around $220bn.
Meta has also entered into a joint-venture -- Hyperion -- that could add ~1.5GW of 
capacity in 2027, on a longer-term plan that includes building ~5GW of capacity. 
This is an off-balance sheet transaction, where Meta's portion of the outlay, which 
is more modest, likely has already been invested. If we exclude Hyperion, Meta 
likely may be adding only 5.5GW of incremental capacity in 2027, which, at $35bn 
per GW, would imply capex of ~$190-$195bn.
A simplifying assumption in our math above is the capex per GW. The $35bn 
estimate is based on Coreweave's costs of adding datacenter capacity purely using 
Nvidia chips, which we know come with significant gross margins built in. Hence 
why Meta intends to mass produce the "Iris" chips, part of Meta's four-generation 
project for Meta Training and Inference Accelerators (MTIA) that it is designing inhouse to reduce dependence on costlier 3P silicon. Since these chips represent 
~60-65% of total datacenter costs, a 50% reduction in prices versus Nvidia's GPUs 
-- which may not be unreasonable given Nvidia's gross margins that are ~75% -- 
could result in ~30-35% lower datacenter costs. Adding 5.5GW of capacity in 2027 
using a 50/50 mix of Iris chips and GPUs, implies an average cost of ~$30bn/GW, 
and could represent a more modest outlay of ~$165bn, well within the range that 
the buyside is already assuming.

Meta looking to reduce exposure to Nvidia
Reuters reported that Meta plans to begin manufacturing its in-house AI chip, Iris, 
in September, while also targeting a doubling of computing infrastructure from 
7GW in 2026 to 14GW in 2027. That headline understandably triggered investor 
concern that 2027 capex estimates need to move materially higher, particularly as 
the market has become increasingly sensitive to any incremental AI infrastructure 
spend. That said, we think the immediate reaction misses three important offsets:
n A meaningful portion of incremental capacity likely comes through offbalance-sheet or partner-funded structures;
n MTIA progress should reduce Meta’s long-term dependence on merchant 
GPUs and lower the blended cost per GW;
n Higher capacity meaningfully increases the potential third-party cloud 
revenue opportunity, which could reframe AI capex from a pure cost-center 
debate into a high-margin revenue optionality debate.

Street capex estimates may appear low, particularly if investors apply a 7GW 
incremental capacity number to Meta’s 2027 buildout. However, we think the right 
starting point is not 7GW x full on-balance-sheet cost per GW. Meta’s Hyperion/
Blue Owl structure already demonstrates a willingness to reduce balance-sheet

would be closer to ~5.5GW vs. 7.0GW. At ~$35bn/GW, that implies roughly ~
$190bn of gross incremental infrastructure cost that is not wildly disconnected 
from buyside expectations which we believe were already in the ~$220bn range.
The second offset is chip mix. Reuters reported that Iris is part of the broader MTIA 
roadmap and that the chip is intended to augment, not replace, Meta’s large Nvidia/
AMD GPU footprint. This custom silicon should however help Meta lower compute 
costs and reduce dependence on external chip suppliers. As a result the cost per 
GW should not remain static if Meta shifts a larger share of incremental workloads 
from Nvidia GPUs to internally optimized silicon. While we are not underwriting a 
step-function reduction in capex from MTIA near term, we think the mix shift 
creates a credible path to lower blended cost per unit of compute, especially for 
inference-heavy workloads where Meta can optimize silicon against its own 
recommendation, ranking, and content-generation use cases. Our $35bn/GW 
estimate is based on Coreweave's costs of adding datacenter capacity purely using 
Nvidia chips, which come with significant gross margins built in. Since servers

are ~75% -- could result in ~30-35% lower datacenter costs. Adding 5.5GW of 
capacity in 2027 using a 50/50 mix of Iris chips and GPUs, implies an average cost 
of ~$30bn/GW, and could represent a more modest outlay of ~$165bn, well within 
the range that the buyside is already assuming.
