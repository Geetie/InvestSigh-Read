What APP and U Had to Say About AI Disruption
We lay out key takeaways from APP and U's commentary at earnings, as the companies addressed the AI disruption bear case head on. We came away incrementally confident in their ability to lean into emerging opportunities and adopt these new tools going forward.
Key Takeaways
APP and U argued AI presents more opportunity than disruption, countering recent AI disruption fears
We came away more confident in their ability to navigate any changes in industry dynamics, while leaning into emerging opportunities
We lay out why, if AI increases the supply of games/ad creative, discovery and marketing should also become more valuable, benefiting ad leaders like APP
We also address why we believe game engine leaders like U look well positioned to adopt and benefit from emerging AI tools like world models
APP and U Pushed Back on the Recent AI Disruption Narrative, Highlighting Opportunities Rather than Risks... Over the past several weeks, we have seen a sharp derating in video game (and video game adjacent) stocks. We believe the main driver of this weakness has been the fear of potential disruption from world models and other new AI tools, which we discussed in World Models Are Here...So What Do They Mean for U, RBLX, TTWO and APP?. Similar to our takeaways from RBLX/ TTWO (see here), we were encouraged by APP and U's commentary at 4Q results last week, as the companies outlined how genAI could create greater opportunities and more manageable risks than recent price action suggests.
...And We See Mobile Ad Networks Benefiting and Game Engines Adapting: In our view, AI has different implications across ad networks and game engines. We believe ad networks are not only insulated from AI disruption (i.e. they are already using cutting-edge AI to target ads) but ad leaders like APP could actually benefit if AI increases competition in the game ecosystem, driving greater reliance on user acquisition and performance marketing. For game engines, we continue to believe a hybrid approach to game creations is more likely than broad AI displacement. Game development platforms like U have already begun integrating new AI tools into existing workflows, including the ability to build entire games using natural language prompts. With that in mind, we came away from APP and U's results incrementally confident that AI could be an accelerator, not a disrupter.

2) AI May Be Best Positioned to Improve Today's Game Engines Rather than Replace Them: While APP has no direct exposure to game engines and development, those are core to U's value proposition (and the strength of its ad business over the long term). We heard a consistent theme from U's management team at earnings: new AI tools like world models are best positioned to augment and improve existing tools/processes, rather than replace them. The company also made a clear distinction between generating content and running a game. They argued that a playable, monetizable, cross-platform experience requires the deterministic execution, integration, and tooling that U provides.
