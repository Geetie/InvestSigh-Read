AI Supply Chain: TSMC outlook, ASIC updates, OCP implication for AI Semis
2026 looks like being another strong year for AI semis, but a bottleneck could come from niche memory and/or server racks, not TSMC’s capacity.
TSMC commented “AI demand is stronger than we thought 3 months ago”: At its recent earnings call, TSMC said it continues to work hard to "narrow the gap" between demand and supply (both CoWoS and wafer front-end capacity). On its opportunity to serve China's market, although there could be some restrictions on foreign AI GPUs from serving China's AI market, management is confident that a 5- year revenue CAGR of mid-40% or higher can be achieved, even if opportunities from China are limited. We keep our CoWoS capacity demand and supply models unchanged in this report, since TSMC only needs 6 months' lead time to expand CoWoS capacity. For wafer front-end (4nm and 3nm), TSMC’s capacity is tight, but it seems AI semi will have a higher priority than crypto mining ASIC and Android smartphone SoC, so we keep our 4nm/3nm wafer capacity the same. At NVIDIA's recent NDR (link), the CEO also mentioned that semiconductor capacity is not the limiting factor it once was (see below for more details).
OCP takeaways: AI Power, Storage, and Optics
• AI clusters are moving to 100k GPU+. This anchors ethernet-first designs and liquid cooling as the default for new AI racks. Aspeed's (OW) BMC is not only on servers, but on multiple devices including cooling. We recently also initiated on Innoscience at EW given future HVDC 800V demand.
• AI storage demand remains strong. Seagate mentioned HDD will keep 95% capacity online, reducing rebuilds/truck rolls and extending driver life, which matters in large/remote data centers (DCs). Meanwhile, Meta said QLC NAND would be preferred in their DCs given lower costs, while TLC NAND seeks to balance power and cost. We reiterate OW on NAND module maker Phison.
• On optics, Alibaba (covered by Gary Yu) mentioned pluggable remains preferred for TCO and flexibility; LPO/KRO are gaining traction. NPO/CPO will still likely happen in 2028 as manufacturing capability matures. We are OW on FOCI and Himax, and expect the semi supply chain to ramp up scaleout CPO in 2026, with small volume of scale-up CPO in late 2027.

Key Takeaways from 2025 OCP
AI infrastructure standardization and scale: The Open Compute Project (OCP) introduced Open Datacenter for AI and Open Cluster Design for AI – to define deployable, procurement-grade blueprints spanning HVDC power interface, CDUs/liquid cooling, rack mechanics, and telemetry. For context, industry additions are 16 zettaflops this year with a path to 160 GW by 2030; scalable units are at \~40 MW; and clusters are moving to 100k GPU+ in the long run. This anchors ethernet-first designs and liquid cooling as the default for new AI racks. We also noticed BMC (Aspeed) is not only on servers, but also on multiple devices including cooling.
Platforms, networking and optics: Meta’s Yosemite V5 ( 3U, up to six servers, ORV3- aligned, CXL options) emphasizes modularity and reusability, while its 2U QLC server targets extreme capacity per watt and \$/TB (up to \~50PB/rack) for heavy workloads. NVIDIA’s Spectrum 6 is pushing AI-optimized Ethernet to cut GPU, claiming \~95% bandwidth efficiency and strong MoE gains. On optics, Alibaba mentioned pluggable remains preferred for TCO and flexibility; LPO (linear pluggable optics) are gaining traction; NPO/CPO (near packaged optics/co-packaged optics) will still likely happen in 2028, as manufacturing capability and interoperability mature.

Hyperscalers' Capex Remains Strong into 2026
