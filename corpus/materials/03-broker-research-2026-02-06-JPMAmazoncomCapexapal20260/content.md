Capexapalooza
Another mega-cap Internet earnings, another big capex step up for 2026, as Amazon completes the trifecta. \$200B in capex has some shock value to it and it's bigger than Google's \$175B-\$185B and Meta's \$115B-\$135B. But in terms of growth from the 4Q run-rate, Amazon's capex is up 30% while Google increases 66% and Meta 58%. We'd also note that around \$45B or so is related to Stores. But make no mistake, the \~\$70B step-up this year is driven largely by AWS and AI, and that's actually a good thing. Think back to the 4Q24 call a year ago when Andy said, "We don't procure it unless we see significant signals of demand. And so, when AWS is expanding its capex, particularly in what we think is one of these once-in-alifetime type of business opportunities like AI represents, I think it's actually quite a good sign medium-to-long term for the AWS business." The pick-up in demand is evident in 4Q current results, with 4Q AWS growth accelerating to 24% and backlog increasing 22% Q/Q to \$244B, with the increase largely driven by the OpenAI deal. But like for Google and Meta, financials take a big hit—FCF in particular—and Amazon is not currently sharing any guardrails around the investment spending. Amazon currently has \$57B in net cash, between Google at \$80B and Meta at \$23B. But it is also a substantially lower margin business which generated \$11B in 2025 FCF, compared to Google's \$73B and Meta's \$44B. As a result, Amazon FCF will almost certainly be negative this year, and likely by a substantial margin as we project negative FCF of -\$36B. Amazon last burned cash during the Covid logistics buildout in 2021 and 2022, before seeing a significant rebound in 2023 and 2024 on rationalization and regionalization of the network footprint. Accordingly, as Amazon did many years ago, we believe the company is willing to take some near-term profit pain to drive significant long-term growth opportunities. We maintain our Overweight rating and lower our PT to \$265 based on 29x 2027E GAAP EPS of \$9.04.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
Amazon.com (Overweight; Price Target: \$265.00)
Investment Thesis
We believe Amazon is well positioned as the market leader in e-commerce and public cloud, for which the secular shifts remain early – US e-commerce represents \~20% of adjusted retail sales, and we estimate only \~10% of IT spend is in the cloud today. We believe Amazon’s flexibility in pushing first-party vs. third-party inventory and the Prime membership serve as major advantages in its Stores business, and its multi-year head start in the cloud has led to \~31% AWS global market share. We believe AMZN is on track for multi-year margin expansion & FCF ramp, with AMZN targeting MSD%+ N. America operating margins. High-growing AWS and Advertising revenue streams are AMZN’s most profitable businesses, further supporting margin expansion & FCF generation.
Valuation
We reduce our December 2026 PT from \$305 to \$265 based on \~29x our 2027E GAAP EPS of \$9.04. This is a premium to GOOGL & META trading at \~26x & \~22x given AMZN’s faster bottom-line growth profile.
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Downside risks include: 1) Pace of AWS reacceleration & normalized growth trajectory; 2) State of US consumer, with macro pressures impacting spending levels; 3) AMZN’s margin path is not always linear, with AMZN historically investing in growth; 4) GenAI monetization is early & visibility is limited; 5) Heavy competition across Stores & Cloud; 6) Increased regulatory scrutiny could become a multi-year overhang; & 7) Less traditional valuation support vs mega-cap peers.
