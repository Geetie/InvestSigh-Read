Where should we start? This is probably one of the most extreme weeks we have seen in our career history, if not the most extreme, by the pace and magnitude of drawdown in momentum. In hindsight, there were multiple narrative shifts we could point to, including open-source model competition, new methods in model distillation, increasing memory cost burden on capex, etc. However, if we focus on frontier model development and industry ARR growth, fundamentals haven’t seen a meaningful downturn. Therefore, we have kept pointing towards positioning as the main source of vulnerability. This argument is probably validated this week, after brutal deleveraging at certain star AI funds. You probably have read a lot on that matter, and we won’t elaborate. Still, we do believe this drawdown creates attractive entry opportunities for many names with visible growth and profitability outlook.
Switching gears to new narratives, the verdict on the heart of hyperscaler earnings season came down on a single axis: AI ROI specificity. Microsoft disclosed 30m+ paid M365 Copilot seats with roughly 10m net adds in a single quarter — double the prior pace — and got rewarded; we think that re-rating is only beginning. Meta grew 28% to the high end of guidance but raised CapEx to \$130–145bn and OPEX to \$165–169bn without putting numbers on the AI revenue increment, and was punished for exactly the gap our preview framed: does AI need to recreate another META? AWS settled the argument by simply delivering — 36.7% growth, the fastest in 18 quarters and a fifth straight acceleration, with backlog up 36% QoQ to \$496bn.
The detail worth sitting with is AWS raising its 2026 CapEx guide from \~\$200bn to \~\$220bn mainly because of memory costs — in the same week memory equities went through a historic momentum unwind. The largest memory buyers on the planet are lifting CapEx because of memory prices while telling you they still cannot meet 2027 demand; that is not consistent with the top of a pricing cycle. The bigger question — whether xAI can actually plug in the \~8GW of compute it has ordered for 2027 — is the subject of our deep dive: the demand is locked into tier-1 OEM production schedules, but nobody on the supply side underwrites more than 5GW. Preview & Review | AWS 2Q26: Accelerated Materially as Expected, and Should Accelerate Again in Q3; CapEx Raised Again
AWS grew 36.7% YoY to \$42.2bn — the fastest in 18 quarters and a fifth consecutive acceleration — with backlog up 36% QoQ to \$496bn and 2026 CapEx lifted to \~\$220bn on memory costs. Our preview called the shape of the quarter: heavy capacity additions landing late in Q2 with the fuller revenue contribution in Q3, alongside accelerating Graviton shipments and high-single to low-double-digit effective GPU price increases. We continue to expect further acceleration in 3Q26.
Preview & Review | META 2Q26: AI Monetization Needs More Specificity
Revenue grew 28% to the high end of guidance. Still, CapEx and OPEX guides moved up again with no concrete answer on the AI revenue increment — the exact risk our preview centered on, with \~6ppt of annual margin drag ahead and the question of whether AI can recreate another META still open. The preview’s eight expert calls had flagged a solid ad base but slow GPU-cloud monetization; next quarter, Meta One and Business Agent contributions are what would repair the story.
Preview | AMD 2Q26: Datacenter CPUs and GPUs Jointly Underpin 2027 Earnings Growth
We expect revenue of \$11.47bn and EPS of \$1.69, above guidance and consensus, with Datacenter at \$6.9bn split between \$2.7bn of GPU and \$4.2bn of server CPU. AI inference and agentic workloads are pulling CPU demand alongside the GPU ramp, supporting earnings growth into 2027.
Preview | SPCX 2Q26: The xAI Compute Ramp, CapEx and the Lock-Up Calendar

The quarter itself has limited surprise room — we estimate \$6.89bn revenue and EPS of -\$0.10, with the only real accounting question being the Anthropic discount rate — while Q3 leased-compute revenue is set to rise \~220% QoQ on already-signed leases. The events that matter are the FY26 capex disclosure on 8/4 and the \~8/6 unlock of up to 911.5m shares; valuation still keys off xAI capacity scaling from 1.5GW today to \~5GW by end-2027, underpinning FY27 revenue of \$83.4bn.
Review | INTC 2Q26: The CapEx Raise Signals Confidence; Results and the Q3 Guide Crush Expectations
Revenue of \$16.1b and EPS of \$0.42 came in well above expectations; the 3Q guide of \$15.8–16.8b beat both consensus and our numbers, and Q4 remains supplyconstrained. Server CPU demand is the engine — DCAI revenue grew 59% YoY to \$6.3b — and the CapEx raise reads as a marked step-up in management confidence.
Review | Nokia 2Q26: Strong Orders Cannot Outrun Near-Term Supply and Restructuring; Chandler Campus Deal Reinforces the Coherent Lite Logic
AI & Cloud orders of €2.8bn beat expectations, but the company left its revenue guidance unchanged, flagged supply constraints for the next 12 months, and leans on 4Q for FY26 operating profit. This setup keeps near-term pressure on the multiple. The Chandler campus acquisition strengthens the Coherent Lite thesis.
Preview & Review | Kioxia FY26Q1: Strong ASP Growth and an Aggressive Buyback
The headline missed consensus (revenue ¥1.77tn, OP ¥1.27tn vs ¥1.84tn/¥1.37tn) on shipment deferrals, a litigation provision and SBC, but the F2Q guide implies \~+30% ASP and SSD & Storage revenue grew 90% QoQ / 390% YoY to 66% of the mix — AI is now visibly driving NAND. The ¥800bn buyback authorization (\~1.8x this year’s planned capex, \~13.5% annualized FCF yield) sets the shareholder-return benchmark for the memory group. As our preview argued into the print, the unwind’s pricing fears look overdone while LTAs trade milder pricing for earnings visibility — management reiterated CY27 demand above supply.
