Advanced Micro Devices Inc. (AMD): Upgrade to Buy as Agentic AI drives server CPU tailwinds; we see datacenter GPU upside in 2027 & beyond
We upgrade AMD to Buy with a 12-month price target of \$450 (27% upside) given tailwinds to its server CPU business we expect from agentic AI, as well as upside in datacenter GPUs in 2027 and beyond. First, we expect proliferation of agentic AI in enterprise and consumer workloads to act as a medium-term tailwind to the server CPU TAM, benefiting AMD. We expect AMD to be an outsized beneficiary of enterprise agentic AI adoption, where we see x86 having staying power as agents interact with enterprises’ exiting x86 infrastructure. Second, we see upside to AMD’s datacenter GPU revenue in 2027 and beyond. We are most bullish on the 6 GW deployment opportunity at Meta, and we expect stable share at Microsoft - although we await more visibility into OpenAI’s deployments. All in, our 2027/28 EPS estimates are \~20% above the Street (VA), and we expect stock price outperformance to be driven by positive estimate revisions. We continue to prefer Buy-rated Broadcom and Nvidia on a relative basis, but we believe AMD presents a compelling opportunity for exposure to agentic AI adoption via server CPUs. Our view is summarized by the following key points:
Structural tailwinds from agentic AI. As framed in our report on agentic AI, given the shift toward more inference workloads, we expect the server CPU TAM to grow at an accelerated rate over the next five years, with the x86 architecture benefiting from enterprise AI agents, and AMD continuing to gain share in the server CPU market. Our 2027/28 server CPU revenue estimates of \$21.1bn/\$27.4bn are 24%/38% above Street estimates.
We see upside to Datacenter GPU revenue estimates. We take a prudent stance on AMD’s Datacenter GPU revenue opportunity, and we are bullish on the 6GW deployment opportunity at Meta. However, we take a measured approach to modeling Microsoft (assuming stable share in accelerators) and OpenAI (we model \~2GW deployment throughout 2030). Our 2027/28 Datacenter GPU revenue estimates are \$42.5bn/\$56.3bn, 28%/26% above Street estimates.
n Cyclical downtick of traditional business is mostly priced in. We believe memory-driven demand destruction in PCs, late-cycle dynamics in gaming, and a cyclical upturn in embedded are well understood by the market.
Estimate changes: We increase our average revenue/EPS estimates by 10%/14% respectively driven by higher Server CPU revenues, higher gross margins, and more operating leverage. Our updated 2027/28 EPS estimates are \$13.50/\$18.00 (\~20% above the Street).

We see Agentic AI driving structural tailwinds for AMD’s server CPU business
With the advent of agentic AI driving a shift toward more inference workloads, we believe server CPU usage is likely to increase meaningfully relative to recent years. Conceptually, agentic-based systems are likely to drive increased CPU cycles as CPUs handle more thinking, data routing, and tool calls - while orchestrating workloads across an entire network. As we highlight in our note (here), we expect tokens related to agenticAI workloads to represent 90% of overall tokens by 2030, with enterprise agentic AI tokens representing \~50%%, and consumer agentic AI tokens representing 40%.
Between x86 and ARM based CPUs, we expect x86 based servers to retain a majority share in enterprise agentic AI to drive better interoperability between AI agents and existing enterprise infrastructure, which is primarily based on x86 architecture. For consumer AI agents, we directionally expect a skew toward ARM-based CPUs, especially as it pertains to hyperscaler internal workloads such as Google Search, Amazon’s e-commerce business, Instagram, YouTube, etc.

We highlight five factors that summarize our bullish view on AMD’s server CPU franchise:
Higher CPU attach rate in AI servers: As highlighted above, we have observed the GPU:CPU attach rate move from 8:1 to 2:1 in the latest rack-level configurations, and we expect this trend to continue in the same direction as industry transitions to using CPU-only racks which complement GPU/ASIC racks.
Strong growth in AMD accelerators: We expect strong growth in AMD’s Datacenter GPU shipments beginning in 2H26, and expect AMD’s AI racks to have a higher attach rate of AMD CPUs. We model AMD’s GPU units growing 66%/98% YoY in 2026/27.
Modernization of enteprise datacenters: As AI proliferates across the enterprise, we have seen companies refresh their CPU fleets to optimize physical space and power. As agentic AI takes hold, we expect this trend of upgrading existing CPU fleets to continue.
n Higher core count driving higher ASPs: We expect agentic AI workloads to drive higher core count utilization and performance requirements - driving upside to server CPU units and ASPs as the industry transitions to new-generation offerings.
n Continued share gains for AMD: We continue to expect AMD to gain incremental share in the server CPU market, especially in the enterprise where we see an outsized benefit for x86-based CPUs relative to ARM - driven by better interoperability with existing x86-based enterprise infrastructure
All in, we expect Server CPU TAM to grow at an accelerated pace over the next 3-5 years, x86 as an architecture to be a key beneficiary especially for enterprise AI agents, and AMD to continue to gain share in x86 and overall Server CPU market. Our 2027/28 server CPU revenue estimates of \$21.1bn/\$27.4bn are 24%/38% above Street (VA) estimates.

Cyclical dynamics in traditional business (Client, Gaming, Embedded) are well understood, and we believe are mostly priced in
We believe the memory-price-driven demand destruction in PCs, and tailwinds from enterprise PC refresh cycle subsiding are well understood near-term headwinds by investors which are largely priced in the stock. We believe any further deterioration in PC fundamentals in 2027 should have a relatively limited impact on company fundamentals due to strong expected growth in the datacenter segment. For gaming, we believe the industry is in the later stages of a console cycle, and this segment is expected to be less than 5% of company revenue in 2027 and beyond, per our estimates. For embedded, we believe we are in early stages of an upcycle, and a recovery in this segment should benefit revenues and margins for the company.
For context, we expect other three segments combined (Client, Gaming and Embedded) to represent <25% of company revenue in 2027 and beyond.

GS Factor Profile
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
M&A Rank
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
