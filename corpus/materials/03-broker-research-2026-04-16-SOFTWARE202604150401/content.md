Watt's New With The Neoclouds? CoreWeave vs. Nebius: Scaled Leadership + Visibility vs. Diversity + Optionality
We view CRWV as the scale + speed leader w/ stronger visibility from disclosures to backlog to 8GW+ power & proven DDTL financings. NBIS comes w/ valuable optionality from greater spot exposure and broader potential diversity across customer concentration, funding sources & data center strategy.
Key Takeaways
Higher Exposure to Spot Market – Nebius. Nebius could benefit near-term due to strong GPU pricing. CoreWeave has more visibility, backlog & committed contracts
Data Center Strategy: Both use co-lo for faster time to market. Nebius existing owned site helping TCO. Both likely to increasingly build/own in future
Larger Scale & Execution on Power – CoreWeave. CoreWeave currently has 5x Nebius' Active Power and greater NT & LT power targets. Nebius scaling rapidly too
Capital Structure / Financing: CoreWeave w/ clearer debt-based financing strategy vs. Nebius more diverse funding sources but also more uncertainty
Customer Concentration & GTM: CRWV focused on scale + largest AI spenders; NBIS replicating, but selling AI Cloud to broader Enterprise base remains priority
Plenty of Similarities...Both CoreWeave and Nebius are rapidly growing assets, positioned in the same attractive AI infrastructure market, providing the GPU cloud compute, networking and storage enabling training and inference workloads. Both have a stack of AI software to enhance the performance of and complement GPU rentals, both are externally validated (highly ranked), and both contracted large deals with Microsoft and Meta. Both enjoy close supplier, investor, and customer relationships with NVIDIA. Both companies benefit from the incredible demand for AI compute and the corresponding supply constraints. We do not see this market as a zero sum game and both companies are in a great position to benefit. However, while CRWV and NBIS are similar in many ways, understanding the significant differences across these businesses may be the key to discover alpha as market dynamics change over time.

1. Data Center Sites & Strategy – Mostly Co-Location Across CoreWeave and Nebius, Some Existing Nebius Owned Infrastructure A Positive, Both Building New Owned Sites. CoreWeave and Nebius are both pursuing hybrid infrastructure strategies, blending co-location, build-to-suit arrangements, and owned development; however the mix of the existing footprint and the trajectory of that mix differ. CoreWeave's existing live infrastructure relies on co-location sites, with a broad third-party data center footprint. Nevertheless, CoreWeave is no longer purely an outsourced model – it is incorporating self-build and joint venture development to improve delivery certainty, gain more direct control over power and physical infrastructure, support the next phase of hyperscale AI factory expansion and open the doors to different asset backed financing opportunities.
Nebius is pursuing a similar hybrid model, but with an existing owned, live data center facility in Finland and a more explicit long-term tilt toward owned

2. Power Progress – CoreWeave Ahead, Nebius Close Behind. Both CoreWeave and Nebius have made tremendous progress contracting power – CoreWeave sits at >3.1 GW of contracted power and Nebius at >2 GW as of the end of 2025. CoreWeave guided to an incremental 5 GW of contracted power by 2030, bringing its target to 8 GW plus by 2030 (see CoreWeave: Deepening the Alliance - CoreWeave & NVIDIA Expand Partnership (27 Jan 2026)), while Nebius is targeting 3 GW by end of 2026 and 5 GW total by 2030 (see Nebius Group NV: Strengthening Strategic Ties - Nebius Announces Partnership With NVIDIA (11 Mar 2026)). From an Active Power perspective, CoreWeave has five times the Active Power compared to Nebius at the end of 2025, at 850 MW vs. 170 MW. We expect CoreWeave can deliver ~1 GW of active power annually over the next several years, while we model ~600 MW of net new active power annually for Nebius over this time period. The difference mostly lies in historical scale and execution, with CoreWeave already bringing online 5x the active power to-date, demonstrating proven ability to a greater extent than the still-more-nascent story at Nebius.

3. Capital Structure, Financing & Capex - CoreWeave Has a Clearer Debt-Based Financing Strategy vs. Nebius More Diverse Options but More Uncertainty. In order for CoreWeave and Nebius to grow, the companies must spend billions on capex; in order to spend billions on capex, the companies need significant amounts of capital, with an ability to raise funds on an ongoing basis. Capital intensity is high, but the shape and maturity of their financing models are quite different. CoreWeave has built a relatively predictable financing pattern around asset-level debt through the delayed-draw term loan (DDTL) facility structure, complemented by corporate debt, convertibles, OEM financing, and equity (see CoreWeave: Building the GPU Economy; Initiate at Equal-weight (22 Apr 2025)). CoreWeave is a pioneer with regard to the DDTL structure in the Neocloud space, and effectively lowered its cost of capital over the years in subsequent rounds. CoreWeave has not only raised very large amounts of funding, but has also progressively improved pricing and broadened the investor base willing to finance GPU-backed AI infrastructure. However, this leads to significant debt balances for CoreWeave – our estimate of CoreWeave's debt to more than double from $21B in 2025 to $46B in 2026 and

4. Market Segmentation, Key Customers & Concentration: CoreWeave Primarily Focused on Scale, Nebius Primarily Focused on Selling AI Cloud to Broader Enterprise Customer Base. CoreWeave targets frontier AI labs, hyperscalers, AI-native startups, and enterprises that need large-scale training and inference infrastructure. CoreWeave has secured some of the most visible and strategically important customer relationships in the sector, demonstrated clear mind-share and market-share strength, reinforcing self proclaimed and external validation of CoreWeave's leadership position. Even so, the business remains highly concentrated today; ~2/3rds of revenue today comes from Microsoft, and we estimate ~2/3rds of 2026 ending ARR comes from a combined Microsoft, OpenAI & Meta.
Nebius is following a similar playbook built around a handful of very large, attractive credit, anchor customers supplemented by a growing long tail of AI startups and enterprise users. Nebius' backlog is highly concentrated today, but the quality of concentration is unusually strong because it is tied to some of the largest and bestcapitalized customers in AI infrastructure. Further, Nebius expects it will be able to bridge the gap from the $4.0B - $4.5B in ARR from Microsoft and Meta by end of 2026 (MSe) to its $7 -$9B ARR target from a broader customer base. In that sense, Nebius’s current concentration, including the 'backstop' portion of the Meta deal, helps secure prepayments, validate demand at scale, and should help sell to a broader base of customers in the medium-term. If Nebius is successful in selling to a broader base of customers, the market likely rewards shares, given lower concentration and potential for higher margins.

5. Software Stack - Contributing to Better Performance, But Early Days for Separate Monetization. Both companies are clearly trying to differentiate as fullstack AI infrastructure platforms rather than commodity GPU lessors, and software is becoming an increasingly important part of that differentiation. CoreWeave’s stack is more mature today in some ways, especially in infrastructure orchestration, observability, workload management, and storage. It has built a platform designed to manage large-scale training and inference clusters efficiently, with tools spanning Kubernetes, Slurm-based environments, storage acceleration, and fleet-level lifecycle operations. CoreWeave's Storage business surpassed $100MM in ARR (as of 3Q25) and its expanded partnership with NVIDIA extends direct software monetization efforts with NVIDIA offering CoreWeave software to global CSPs and Enterprises.
Nebius is also highly focused on software and selling its AI Cloud to a broad set of Enterprise customers. Nebius appears more focused on delivering a tightly integrated, secure AI cloud environment with strong virtualization, tenant isolation, workload orchestration, and production inference tooling layered directly on top of its infrastructure. It is still incredibly early with regard to software, but CoreWeave seems more advanced today in large-scale infrastructure operations software, with an incredible focus on performance, while Nebius looks focused on building a more differentiated application-facing and inference-oriented layer over time, geared to attracting enterprise customers. Both go well beyond bare metal GPU rentals; CoreWeave looks ahead on software maturity and platform breadth (some capabilities through M&A), whereas Nebius looks increasingly interesting as a vertically integrated AI cloud offering, with evidence in its success selling to a broader Enterprise customer base.

6. Profitability – Coming from Different Starting Points, Heading Toward Similar imita
Margin Levels? The profitability setup is nuanced because both companies are scaling rapidly, such that the true underlying economics are masked by rapid growth. However, CoreWeave operates at a much larger revenue base and has demonstrated strong GAAP operating margin profitability already (though margins are moving lower this year due to rapid growth and timing mis-match of expenses and revenue), but its reported profitability remains pressured and below targets by the realities of scaling an extremely capital-intensive platform at very high speed. Depreciation, interest expense, and infrastructure costs are all rising ahead of the full maturation of newly deployed capacity, which means near-term margins can compress even as the underlying business scales rapidly (see CoreWeave: Valuation Hot, Expecting Some Liquid Cooling; Raising PT to Reflect Business Momentum (31 Jul 2025)).
Nebius is not only much earlier in absolute revenue scale, but also started from day one with 100's of employees and an opex base from Yandex (see Nebius Group NV: Differentiated Neocloud, Full Valuation; Initiate at Equal-weight (15 Jan 2026)) and zero in revenue. Further, Nebius' other assets - consolidated in its financials (including AVride) are operating at a meaningful loss and weighing on margins. Still, Nebius may ultimately benefit from a greater owned-infrastructure mix, tight hardware-software integration and a higher margin customer base (more likely to attached managed services and less likely to bring over software tooling to run on lower margin bare metal). Relative to CoreWeave, Nebius is not yet as proven on scaled earnings power, but it may have a more compelling margin potential if it can successfully convert its new capacity, owned-site strategy, broader Enterprise customer go-to-market and software layer into structurally better economics.
