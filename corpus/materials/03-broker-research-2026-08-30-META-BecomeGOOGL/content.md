Could META Now Become GOOGL of 2H:25?
We detail our take on META’s teen engagement settlement and, as an analogy to GOOGL in ’25, wonder if the META product pipeline is about to flow and META’s multiple is about to expand.
Meta Litigation Clearing Event…Last week (Wednesday), META agreed to pay up to ~$17bn over 10 years to settle social media addiction claims, while also agreeing to changes for teens using FB/IG. These include 1) a 2-hour daily limit across FB/IG for U18 users, removable with parental permission, 2) default blocking during school (8am–3pm) and overnight hours (midnight–6am), and 3) an opt-in to non-algorithmic feeds for U18 users, among other changes. Payments are made up of a guaranteed $12.7bn to a coalition of states over 10 years (with a $10bn legal expense accrual this quarter) plus an additional $5.3bn contingent on YouTube/ TikTok reaching similar agreements whereby they also pay $5.3bn and those apps agree to a one-hour time limit.
…and Potential Larger Long-term Headwind to YouTube than Meta…While, in our view, it will be challenging to push GOOGL to follow a 1 hour limit (and pay $5.3bn) quickly, we believe youth engagement ceilings like these would actually be a larger headwind to YouTube than Meta given average time spent by teens on FB/IG is well below two hours per day (with IG usage less than 1 hour) and third party data shows that average time spent per user per day on YouTube in the US is ~73 minutes…and we believe youth adoption of YouTube is higher than FB/IG. We also believe revenue from teens represents only ~1% of META revenue.
…Are Youth Digital Regulation Winds About to Start Blowing Harder? Bigger picture, we are focused on whether a ruling like this (which includes YouTube, not a social network) could potentially accelerate a push for firmer regulation of teen and youth digital usage. Investors should watch the tea leaves here as this could have impacts on many platforms including SNAP, RBLX, SPOT and others. RBLX is covered by Matthew Cost, and SPOT is covered by Sean Diffley.
GOOGL Last Year? The time frame over which leading tech companies launch innovative new offerings can be impacted by many factors…including, in our view, externalities such as substantial lawsuits. Look no further than September of ’25 when the GOOGL DoJ ruling was followed by a slew of successful new product and model launches, including Gemini 3 and broader rollout of search tools including AI Mode/AI Overviews.…which ultimately led GOOGL’s multiple to expand by ~40% from 9/1/25 to 12/31/25.
