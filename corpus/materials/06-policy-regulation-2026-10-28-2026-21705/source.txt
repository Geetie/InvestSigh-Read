SUMMARY: The OCC, as part of its continuing effort to reduce paperwork 
and respondent burden, invites comment on a new information collection, 
as required by the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995 (PRA). In accordance 
with the requirements of the PRA, the OCC may not conduct or sponsor, 
and the respondent is not required to respond to an information 
collection unless it displays a currently valid Office of Management 
and Budget (OMB) control number. The OCC is proposing a new information 
collection titled ``Reporting Forms and Instructions for Permitted 
Payment Stablecoin Issuers Subject to the Jurisdiction of the Office of 
the Comptroller of the Currency,'' that would include weekly and 
quarterly reporting forms that must be completed by permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers. The OCC is 
seeking a new OMB control number for this information collection. The 
OCC also is giving notice that it has sent the collection to OMB for 
review.

    Description: The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for 
U.S. Stablecoins Act (12 U.S.C. 5901 et seq.) (GENIUS Act or the Act) 
was enacted on July 18, 2025. The GENIUS Act establishes a regulatory 
framework for payment stablecoin activities. On March 2, 2026, the 
Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) issued a proposed rule 
that would implement requirements of the Act with respect to the 
issuance of payment stablecoins and certain related activities by 
entities subject to the OCC's jurisdiction (the proposed rule).\1\ On

June 12, 2026, the OCC proposed weekly and quarterly reporting forms 
(Form PS-01 and Form PS-02, respectively) that permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers subject to

OCC issued a final rule implementing the requirements of the GENIUS Act 
with respect to the issuance of payment stablecoins and certain related 
activities by entities subject to the OCC's jurisdiction (the final 
rule), published elsewhere in this issue of the Federal Register.\3\

    These reporting forms would help ensure compliance with the final 
rule, facilitate OCC supervision of permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers, and would promote 
transparency with respect to the financial condition of permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers. 
Twelve CFR 15.14 of the final rule requires a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer to submit two reports to the OCC. Section 15.14(h) 
requires a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to submit a weekly 
confidential reporting form to the OCC for each payment stablecoin it 
issues, and section 15.14(i) requires a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer to submit a quarterly reporting form to the OCC. Section 
15.31(b)(2) of the final rule requires a foreign payment stablecoin 
issuer registered with the OCC to produce the reports required of a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer under section 15.14. With this 
issuance, the OCC is publishing a revised set of the required weekly 
and quarterly reporting forms for public comment. The revised reporting 
forms, and their instructions, are available at <a href="http://www.occ.gov">www.occ.gov</a>. The 
proposed forms are published as text documents. However, the OCC 
expects to use eXtensible Markup Language (commonly known as ``XML'') 
or another format for permitted payment stablecoin issuers to submit 
reported data to enhance usability of the data. The final rule will be 
effective on January 18, 2027. This information collection would take 
effect as of the January 25, 2027, report date for OCC Reporting Form 
PS-01, Payment Stablecoin Activity and Reserve Weekly Reporting, and 
the March 31, 2027, report date for OCC Reporting Form PS-02, Permitted 
Payment Stablecoin Issuer and Foreign Payment Stablecoin Issuer Reports 
of Condition and Income.

General Comments Regarding Forms PS-01 and PS-02

    On June 12, 2026, the OCC proposed a weekly reporting form, Form 
PS-01, and a quarterly reporting form, Form PS-02, that permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers, 
subject to the OCC's jurisdiction, would be required to file with the 
OCC. The notice accompanying the forms invited comment on Forms PS-01 
and PS-02 generally as well as certain technical aspects of the forms. 
The comment period closed on August 11, 2026, and the OCC received 
approximately 20 comments in total. The comments focused on numerous 
aspects of the proposed forms as well as the overall reporting 
framework. Below is a discussion of the comments the OCC received 
concerning the overall reporting framework and technical comments that 
apply to both Form PS-01 and Form PS-02. Comments specific to Form PS-
01 and Form PS-02 are discussed below.
    One commenter expressed support for a reporting framework that is 
targeted, risk-based, and aligned with the GENIUS Act and encouraged 
the OCC to tailor the reporting obligations to capture information most 
important to assessing compliance with the GENIUS Act's core 
requirements, while avoiding imposing duplicative or impracticable 
obligations. In response to this feedback, the OCC has revised the 
reporting forms to minimize duplication, reduce the overall reporting, 
and ensure that the information being collected from permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers is essential 
to the supervision of those entities. Upon implementation, the OCC will 
continue to assess the reporting forms to ensure that the data being 
reported serves a justifiable supervisory purpose and provides the OCC 
with information relevant to the supervision of permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers.
    Several commenters expressed support for the issuance of a 
reporting framework jointly adopted by the primary Federal payment 
stablecoin regulators. Generally, the commenters stated that jointly 
adopted reporting forms would improve consistency of reporting, reduce 
burden, and align with the statutory mandate in the GENIUS Act for 
Federal payment stablecoin regulators. One commenter stated that the 
primary Federal payment stablecoin regulators should coordinate closely 
to ensure that reporting requirements for all federal qualified payment 
stablecoin issuers are substantially similar. Another commenter stated 
that harmonization is key to ensuring that permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers compete on a level compliance field, while also enabling the 
Federal payment stablecoin regulators to evaluate the same core 
information through consistent, comparable reporting metrics. Another 
commenter stated that a review of the reporting forms and instructions 
published by the OCC and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation 
(``FDIC'') found mostly identical definitions but that there were a few 
notable differences between the OCC's and the FDIC's respective 
forms.\5\ The commenter claimed that differences in definitions, 
labels, or calculation relationships can result in inconsistencies and 
lack of interoperability between datasets, which makes it not only 
difficult to compare and analyze, but also limits the Federal payment 
stablecoin regulators' ability to share tools and platforms for 
collection and use. The commenter further stated that harmonizing the 
reporting requirements and terminology will make it easier and less 
expensive for the industry to implement. Another commenter stated that 
the OCC should coordinate with the other primary Federal payment 
stablecoin regulators so that common fields have the same meaning, 
which would improve comparability, reduce reconciliation risk, and 
minimize duplicative reporting burdens for permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers subject to multiple Federal reporting or supervisory 
expectations.

primary Federal payment stablecoin regulators. Further, the OCC has

joint reporting forms. The GENIUS Act vests the primary Federal payment

re-proposal of the reporting forms until after the final rule is

March 31, 2027, will be due no later than April 30, 2027.\6\ The OCC is

    The OCC also received comments concerning the aspects of the 
proposal related to the reporting requirements for foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers. One commenter expressed the view that the OCC 
should not default to a ``one-size-fits-all'' reporting regime for both 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin 
issuers and urged the OCC to tailor the information required of a 
foreign payment stablecoin issuers to what is reasonably necessary for 
the OCC to assess a foreign payment stablecoin issuer's U.S. payment 
stablecoin activities and its compliance with the GENIUS Act. The 
commenter explained that an appropriately tailored and flexible 
reporting regime for foreign payment stablecoin issuers will further 
cross-border collaboration in digital financial innovation using 
payment stablecoins. The same commenter further stated that the OCC 
should consider which data items in Forms PS-01 and PS-02 are truly 
necessary to supervise a foreign payment stablecoin issuer's U.S. 
operations and should exempt foreign payment stablecoin issuers from 
unnecessary data items. As an example, the commenter stated in Form PS-
01, instead of asking for a foreign payment stablecoin issuer's top 
five counterparties and top 100 exchanges worldwide, those items could 
be limited to the top five U.S. counterparties and the top 100 
exchanges that make the foreign payment stablecoin issuer's payment 
stablecoins available in the U.S.
    With regard to quarterly reporting for foreign payment stablecoin 
issuers, the commenter expressed the view that foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers could provide a simplified version of Form PS-02's 
income statement that does not require a detailed breakdown of income 
streams and expenses for different types of reserve assets in proposed 
Schedule A, Items 1 and 2 and for other income sources and other 
expenses in proposed Schedule A, Items 4 and 5. The commenter explained 
that a simplified approach to ``other'' income and expenses in Items 4 
and 5 would appropriately reflect the fact that foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers may have somewhat different activities, income 
sources, and expenses than domestic permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers, while still providing the OCC and the public with sufficient 
information on the foreign payment stablecoin issuer's financial 
performance. The same commenter also recommended that the OCC permit 
foreign payment stablecoin issuers to elect to satisfy their reporting 
obligations with reasonably equivalent reports prepared for their home 
country regulators.
    The commenter also recommended that the OCC permit foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers to submit their weekly and quarterly reporting forms 
in accordance with the International Financial Reporting Standards 
(``IFRS'') to avoid imposing unnecessary compliance burdens on foreign 
payment stablecoin issuers. The commenter explained that because 
foreign payment stablecoin issuers are organized or domiciled outside 
the United States, many foreign payment stablecoin issuers will prepare 
their home country reports and audited financial statements according 
to IFRS.
    The OCC declines to tailor the reporting forms for foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers or allow foreign payment stablecoin issuers to 
satisfy their reporting obligations through the submission of reports 
generated for compliance in their home jurisdiction. Under the final 
rule, foreign payment stablecoin issuers are required to file the same 
reports as permitted payment stablecoin issuers.\13\ Following 
implementation of the final rule, the OCC will assess the reporting 
forms and consider whether the reporting forms, or some aspect of the 
forms, should be tailored for foreign payment stablecoin issuers. 
Issuer-specific and jurisdiction specific-issues related to the 
reporting forms may also be addressed as a condition of licensing or 
under comparability determinations or reciprocity agreements created by 
the Secretary of the Treasury, under section 18 of the GENIUS Act (12 
U.S.C. 5916). The OCC notes that a foreign payment stablecoin issuer 
may request, and the OCC may provide, an exemption from a reporting 
requirement.\14\ Thus, the final rule provides an avenue for reducing 
burden on a case-by-case basis.

    The OCC also received feedback related to the filing of amended 
forms. Specifically, a commenter stated that the OCC should replace the 
proposed five calendar-day deadline by which payment stablecoin issuers 
would be required to produce amended filings with a more flexible 
deadline. The commenter stated that in many cases, five days is 
unlikely to be enough time to identify, reconcile, verify, and report 
an error. The commenter added that historical revisions may require 
significant resources to identify affected periods, reconstruct and 
reconcile data, and to prepare and file an accurate restatement.
    In response to this comment, the OCC has amended the proposed 
instructions for Forms PS-01 and PS-02 to remove the requirement that 
amended filings be made within five calendar days of discovery. The OCC 
made this revision to the instructions to provide permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers with increased flexibility to address omissions or 
significant errors. However, the removal of the five-calendar-day 
deadline for filing amended reports is not intended to suggest that 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers are relieved of their obligation 
to correct forms containing omissions or significant errors in a timely 
manner. A permitted payment stablecoin issuer should contact their 
supervisory team if it is unclear about its obligations to address 
omissions or significant errors in its reporting forms in an 
appropriate timeframe.
    The OCC also received comments and feedback concerning technical 
elements of the reporting forms. The comments largely recommended that 
the OCC clarify certain aspects of the reporting forms and 
instructions. One commenter requested that the OCC confirm how bridged 
and wrapped representations should be treated.\16\ The commenter 
recommended that representations the permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer did not create should not count toward their outstanding 
issuance value and should be disclosed separately. The same commenter 
also suggested the OCC confirm how restricted balances interact with 
reported outstanding supply, so that the reported figure can be 
compared with observable network supply.

    \16\ ``Wrapping'' is a process where a crypto-asset is provided 
to a wrapped token provider, the provider controls or 
programmatically locks up the crypto-asset in a way that it cannot 
be transferred, lent, pledged, rehypothecated, or otherwise used for 
any reason, then mints a new crypto-asset in the form of a 
redeemable wrapped token. The redeemable wrapped token confers to 
the holder the right to ``un-lock'' the original crypto-asset from 
the wrapped token provider and is designed so that the number of 
redeemable wrapped tokens outstanding equals the underlying crypto-
assets wrapped. Wrapped tokens are primarily used to enhance 
interoperability between blockchains by creating a redeemable 
wrapped token that is issued on a different blockchain than the 
original crypto-asset, effectively allowing holders to transact 
crypto-assets on blockchains they are not natively minted on.
    ``Bridging'' is the process of moving a crypto-asset from one 
blockchain to another or creating a redeemable wrapped token on a 
different blockchain than the underlying crypto-assets. Crypto-asset 
issuers that can mint and burn their crypto-asset on multiple 
blockchains may offer the ability to natively ``bridge'' crypto-
assets. They do this by accepting their crypto-asset issued on one 
blockchain, burning, or permanently removing it from circulation, 
and simultaneously minting it on another blockchain. The new crypto-
asset has been permanently transferred to the second blockchain 
while the overall supply of crypto-assets issued by the provider 
remains the same. In contrast, a wrapped token provider may provide 
``bridging'' services. However, as described above, the resulting 
crypto-asset is a redeemable wrapped token. It does not burn or 
alter the original crypto-asset in any way.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The OCC does not believe any clarification concerning the treatment 
of bridged or wrapped stablecoins is required. First, the definition of 
payment stablecoin, in part, states that a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer has an obligation to redeem the payment stablecoin for a fixed 
amount of monetary value.\17\ A permitted payment stablecoin issuer 
would not have an obligation to redeem bridged or wrapped stablecoins. 
With respect to restricted stablecoins, the OCC, again, does not 
believe any additional clarity is necessary. Whether a restricted 
stablecoin meets the definitions of a payment stablecoin may depend on 
the facts and circumstances of that payment stablecoin. Permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers will determine whether payment stablecoins 
that have been minted meet the definition of a payment stablecoin and 
whether they are issued to be considered part of outstanding issuance 
value. If a restricted payment stablecoin remains a liability of the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer because the payment stablecoin may 
be redeemed in the future, then the payment stablecoin is part of the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's outstanding issuance value and a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer must maintain reserves backing the 
outstanding stablecoin and the associated liability.

Proposed Weekly Reporting Form PS-01

    The proposed weekly reporting forms would, among other things, help 
ensure compliance with the reserve asset requirements in proposed 
sections 15.11 and 15.31(a)(3). Proposed Schedule A would collect 
information on top stablecoin holders and transactors and counterparty 
concentration information. Proposed Schedule B would collect 
information on day-over-day changes in outstanding payment stablecoins 
and information redemptions. Proposed Schedule C would collect 
information about a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's aggregate 
composition of reserve assets for each payment stablecoin it issues. 
Proposed Schedules D, E, F, G, and H would collect disaggregated 
information about specific classes of reserve assets (e.g., U.S. 
Treasury securities on proposed Schedule E). Permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers would only be 
required to report information regarding reserve assets held pursuant 
to the GENIUS Act on Proposed Schedules C, D, E, F, G, and H. Permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers are 
not required to report information on these schedules regarding assets 
they own that are not held as reserve assets or transactions in which 
they have engaged unrelated to the maintenance of reserve assets. For 
example, permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment 
stablecoin issuers should not report U.S. Treasury securities they own 
on Schedule E if those U.S. Treasury securities do not comprise the 
reserve assets used to back outstanding stablecoin issuance pursuant to 
the GENIUS Act.
    As a part of the notice, the OCC invited comments on what items 
should be modified or removed and whether any items should be added. 
The OCC received feedback on numerous aspects of proposed Form PS-01.
    One commenter expressed support for the collection and stated that 
the weekly reporting asks for the right information at the right 
cadence, and the schedule structure introduced in the proposed 
rulemaking is sensible. Another commenter stated that the proposed 
schedules collect information that is both available to permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and is relevant to the oversight of 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers. In contrast, another commenter 
stated that the proposed weekly reporting cadence has no identified 
analogue in the ordinary prudential reporting the OCC requires of a 
national bank in sound condition. The commenter also questioned why the 
Call Report and the monthly, independently examined reserve report 
leave a supervisory gap requiring a universal weekly filing. The 
commenter also expressed support for adopting monthly reporting instead 
of a weekly reporting cadence.
    The OCC believes that requiring a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer to provide a weekly confidential set of data for each payment 
stablecoin it issues will allow the agency to understand the permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer's operations and the risks unique to its 
business model. Monthly and quarterly reports do not provide a frequent 
enough reporting cadence to oversee risks and changes, given the short 
maturity of permitted payment stablecoin issuers' reserve assets. This 
regular data reporting will also allow the OCC to tailor its 
examinations to be risk-based, which will reduce the burden of 
examinations. Further, weekly reporting will allow the agency to 
identify and respond more quickly to emerging novel and financial 
stability risks. The weekly reporting will remain confidential because 
its primary purpose is to inform the OCC's supervision of permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and the stablecoin market.
    Several commenters provided feedback about the challenges 
associated with providing reliable and accurate data on a weekly basis 
on the proposed timelines. One commenter claimed that Form PS-01 
effectively establishes a daily reporting obligation across all eight 
schedules that largely duplicates the GENIUS Act's monthly attestation 
requirement. The commenter further claimed that collecting and 
furnishing this data on a daily basis for consolidation into a weekly 
report creates significant operational demands that, in some cases, may 
not be possible or may need additional time to verify. The commenter 
highlighted three categories as particularly problematic to comply 
with: (i) third-party data; (ii) instrument-level reserve detail; and 
(iii) operational timing. The commenter further claimed that providing 
this data in near-real time also minimizes the ability to conduct data 
validation and reconciliation. The commenter further added that 
reporting should allow sufficient time to reconcile and evidence 
reserve balances and payment stablecoins in circulation, rather than 
require publication of unsupported figures. The commenter also proposed 
for permitted payment stablecoin issuers in full compliance with the 
attestation requirements, that the OCC substitute the weekly forms by 
supplementing the attestation with a randomly selected monthly surprise 
observation date. The commenter stated that this is a standard that has 
been successfully used by the New York Department of Financial Services 
to ensure payment stablecoin issuers maintain identifiable reserves at 
all times.
    Another commenter stated that requiring permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers to become a routine reporting agent for third-party 
trading activity is a distinct market surveillance function.

[[Page 66922]]

The commenter added that if the OCC concludes that issuer-based market 
surveillance is both necessary and authorized, the requirement should 
be proposed in regulatory text through coordinated notice-and-comment 
rulemaking. The commenter added that the process should identify the 
risk each item addresses, the decisions it will inform, the party best 
positioned to supply it, and a common methodology and accuracy 
standard. The commenter further added that the Tuesday-to-Wednesday 
cycle leaves approximately seventeen hours to collect final data from 
multiple providers, reconcile it, approve it, and submit it. The 
commenter claimed that preliminary files frequently change after 
settlement or valuation adjustments, which results in a forced choice 
between provisional reporting and routine amendments. Two other 
commenters added that requiring permitted payment stablecoin issuers to 
file Form PS-01 within less than 24 hours creates a significant 
recurring operational demand, while also reducing a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer's ability to validate data. Another commenter 
recommended that the OCC eliminate the secondary market pricing line 
items in Form PS-01 and the OCC obtain real-time pricing data instead 
of requiring permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report this 
information.
    As discussed in greater detail below, the OCC is revising Form PS-
01 and its accompanying schedules to reduce the amount of market data 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers are required to report. For 
example, the OCC has removed Items 3 and 4 from proposed Schedule A, 
which sought data related to trading volume of exchanges. The OCC is 
eliminating these reporting categories in response to feedback received 
concerning the operational and validation challenges associated with 
the collection of third-party data.
    In response to these comments concerning the limited amount of time 
between the close of the reporting period and the reporting deadline, 
the OCC has elected to maintain the Wednesday, 5:00 p.m. (Eastern) 
filing deadline for Form PS-01, but revise the reporting period in the 
proposed instructions for Form PS-01 from ``12:00:00 a.m. (Eastern) 
each Wednesday to 11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern) the following Tuesday'' to 
``12:00:00 a.m. (Eastern) each Tuesday to 11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern) the 
following Monday.'' This change is being implemented to provide 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers with an additional 24 hours to 
prepare and submit Form PS-01.
    A commenter recommended that the OCC align Form PS-01 reporting 
with information maintained in a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's 
books and records as of its ordinary close of business. The commenter 
observed that the instructions require daily balance items as of 
11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern) and daily activity based on transactions 
occurring from 12:00:00 a.m. through 11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern), including 
weekends and holidays. The commenter explained that those times may not 
align with a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's ordinary accounting 
and posting processes and could require reconstruction of activity 
beyond its existing books and records. Another commenter added that 
most blockchain systems currently use a Coordinated Universal Time (or 
``UTC'') -based reference and shifting to Eastern time zone business 
hours would add operational complexity without any commensurate 
supervisory benefit.
    The OCC declines to modify the instructions for Form PS-01 to allow 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers to utilize their preferred close 
of business. The OCC believes maintaining the 12:00:00 a.m. to 11:59:59 
p.m. (Eastern) reporting period promotes consistency and comparability 
across the reported data. For these reasons, the OCC is maintaining the 
proposed reporting period. Following implementation of the final rule, 
the OCC will monitor the reporting process to determine if changes to 
the reporting instructions are warranted.
    The OCC also received a range of recommendations and feedback on 
the instructions for Form PS-01. Several commenters stated that 
reporting with reference only to name and ticker cannot reliably 
identify which specific token implementation a filing relates to. One 
commenter recommended that the OCC add a standardized digital token 
identifier (``DTI'') field, consistent with ISO 24165. Several 
commenters also recommended that the OCC require reporting entities to

not required to obtain a DTI for purposes of reporting Form PS-01. 
Similarly, if a blockchain does not have an assigned DLI, a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer should leave the field blank.
    Another commenter recommended that the OCC revise the instructions 
for Form PS-01 to allow for submission of the form on the next business 
day if the Wednesday deadline falls on a federal holiday. The commenter 
noted that the proposed instructions for Form PS-02 state, ``If the 
reporting deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday, the form may 
be submitted on the first business day following the weekend or federal 
holiday.'' However, the instructions for Form PS-01, which is due every 
Wednesday, do not contain a similar provision.
    In response to this comment, the OCC has amended the proposed

    The OCC also received several comments proposing reduced reporting

    In response to these comments, the OCC considered the potential

Proposed Form PS-01--Schedules A Through G

    Proposed Form PS-01 contained eight schedules--Schedules A through 
H. As discussed below, the OCC has consolidated proposed Schedule A and 
Schedule B from revised Form PS-01. As a result, revised Form PS-01 now 
contains seven schedules--Schedule A through G. Below is a discussion 
of the proposed schedules, comments the OCC received related to the 
proposed schedules, and the changes the OCC is incorporating into 
revised Schedules A through G.

Schedule A--General

    Proposed Schedule A would collect information on the largest 
holders of a stablecoin issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer 
or foreign payment stablecoin issuer (listed by wallet address), 
exchanges facilitating trading of that stablecoin, the trading volume 
of the stablecoin, and the top counterparties of a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer.
    The agency sought feedback on the collection of information in 
proposed Schedule A. Specifically, the OCC asked if this is the right 
type of information for the agency to collect to monitor primary and 
secondary market dynamics, including during times of stress. The OCC 
also asked if there are adjustments that should be made to make this 
information more valuable or less burdensome. For example, should the 
agency collect a fewer number of wallet addresses or exchanges and what 
would be the potential advantages or disadvantages of doing so. 
Finally, the agency asked if there is other information the agency does 
not propose to collect that would give better insight into primary and 
secondary market dynamics. In response to these prompts, the OCC 
received numerous comments related to proposed Schedule A and the 
information the OCC proposed to be collected.
    One commenter explained that because exchange and venue addresses 
will constitute a substantial share of the addresses holding the 
largest balances, Item 1 will to a significant extent restate, in an 
anonymous alphanumeric unit, information that Item 3 collects in a 
named and directly usable one. The commenter also claimed that Items 1 
and 2 of Schedule A together produce as many as 1,400 address-level 
observations in each weekly filing. The commenter also observed that 
FDIC PS-01a collects only the ten largest wallet addresses and 
blockchains that exceed 2 percent of total outstanding issuance. The 
commenter explained that the supervisory content of a concentration 
item lies in the positions large enough to bear on concentration; a 
position below a stated threshold does not inform that assessment, and 
its collection is burden without corresponding utility. The commenter 
further added that a threshold also makes the size of the collection a 
function of the market rather than of an arbitrary row count: where 
holdings are genuinely dispersed, few rows are reported, which is 
itself the supervisory answer.
    Several commenters also provided feedback on proposed Schedule A's 
collection of the ``Top 100 payment stablecoin wallet addresses by 
trading volume.'' One commenter stated that the instructions should 
specify whether the table is intended to capture activity involving 
centralized exchanges, decentralized exchanges, or both, and that the 
OCC should explain how an issuer should report activity attributable to 
a centralized exchange, where customer trading generally occurs off-
chain on the exchange's internal ledger and therefore may not be 
visible at the individual wallet-address level on the relevant 
blockchain. Another commenter noted that footnote 1 to the instructions 
states that the OCC proposes to define ``trading volume'' as the 
aggregate number of payment stablecoins issued by a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer that were 
purchased or sold on exchanges during a specified period of time. The 
commenter stated that this item requests information that a reporting 
issuer does not generate and has no means of obtaining from any source 
within its control. Schedule A, Items 3 and 4 already collect trading 
volume at the exchange level, from the entities that hold the execution 
data and that can be identified by legal name, and item 3 supplies the 
venue population used by the Schedule B pricing items. The commenter 
requested that the OCC remove Schedule A, Item 2 and retain Items 3 and 
4. Another commenter encouraged the OCC to remove the reporting of 
third-party data and not expect issuers to certify secondary-market 
data beyond what they can reasonably obtain and validate. Similarly, 
another commenter stated that the OCC should remove requirements for a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer to report information that is 
derived from information held by exchanges, such as items 3 and 4 from 
Form PS-01, Schedule A regarding exchange trading volume or item 2 from 
Form PS-01, Schedule B regarding secondary market pricing. Another 
commenter observed that proposed Schedule A would require reporting on 
the daily trading volume across top exchanges and other daily volume 
figures. However, according to the commenter, it is unclear whether 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers would be required to report the 
daily average for the week or the daily average for each day of the 
reporting week. If the former, the commenter recommended

[[Page 66924]]

that the instructions clarify this approach. If the latter, the 
commenter recommended updating the reporting template to present how 
daily volumes should be reported for each day of the reporting week.
    One commenter stated that Form PS-01 should supplement wallet-
address reporting with information regarding major direct redemption 
counterparties, custodians, exchanges, omnibus accounts, affiliates, 
and beneficial ownership concentrations where known. The commenter 
explained that wallet-address concentrations may not necessarily 
correspond to economic concentrations. The commenter added that a 
single wallet address may represent an exchange, custodian, omnibus 
account, liquidity pool, affiliate, bridge, or other intermediary 
relationship serving numerous customers, while economically significant 
holders may maintain positions across multiple wallet addresses and 
blockchains. As a result, wallet-level data may not fully identify the 
entities capable of generating significant redemption activity or 
transmitting stress through primary and secondary markets. Another 
commenter recommended retaining a shorter address list for 
investigative purposes and adding an aggregated distribution view for 
each stablecoin on each network. The commenter claimed that this would 
be both more informative and less burdensome.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should ensure that any 
collection of large-holder information by wallet address is subject to 
the highest levels of confidentiality. The commenter stated that robust 
privacy protections are essential given the unique transparency of 
public blockchains, where wallet addresses can be linked, directly or 
indirectly, to identifiable persons through blockchain analytics or 
off-chain data sources. Another commenter stated that the OCC should 
modify its requirement to report the top 100 stablecoin wallet 
addresses in Form PS-01 to instead require reporting of wallets holding 
the greater of $10 million or 1% of outstanding supply for any given 
stablecoin. According to the commenter, absent supply-based tailoring, 
the current top 100 wallet reporting requirement could produce 
unhelpful results for payment stablecoins with small issuance volumes. 
Another commenter stated that the address and the holder are distinct 
units, and that the difference bears directly on whether the item 
delivers the information the notice describes. The commenter explained 
that a single person may control any number of addresses, and nothing 
in the collection would disclose that those addresses are commonly 
controlled. Conversely, a single address may hold payment stablecoins 
on behalf of a large number of unrelated persons. The commenter claimed 
that for a payment stablecoin in general circulation, the addresses 
holding the largest balances are predominantly infrastructure 
addresses: omnibus accounts operated by centralized exchanges, bridge 
contracts, and liquidity pool addresses associated with automated 
market makers. Another commenter stated that the OCC should replace the 
proposed ``largest holders by wallet address'' field with reporting on 
the permitted payment stablecoin issuer's largest direct customers, 
minters, redeemers, and other direct counterparties within the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's own perimeter. The commenter 
added that a wallet address does not necessarily correspond to a single 
holder, customer, beneficial owner, or risk exposure, and issuers often 
will not have reliable visibility into beneficial ownership across 
wallets at exchanges, custodians, intermediaries, decentralized venues, 
or in secondary-market transfers.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should consider whether 
additional venue-level information is necessary to identify 
concentrated pricing dislocations, liquidity migration, market 
fragmentation, or emerging stress at particular trading venues. The 
commenter explained that trading volume alone is not a sufficient 
measure of market quality because elevated volume may reflect healthy 
liquidity, arbitrage activity, liquidity migration, market stress, or 
forced selling. The commenter noted that the OCC recognized that 
exchange-specific activity is relevant by requiring reporting on the 
top 100 exchanges by trading volume and by calculating key pricing 
metrics, including minimum price, maximum price, estimated secondary-
market price, peg deviation, and bid/ask spread, using data from the 
top 15 exchanges. However, according to the commenter, many of these 
secondary-market indicators are reported only as aggregated measures 
across those exchanges. The commenter added that venue-level 
information may provide greater visibility into whether adverse market 
conditions are isolated to a specific exchange or reflect broader 
deterioration in stablecoin market liquidity and resilience.

Consolidation of Proposed Schedule A and Schedule B

    In response to the above comments concerning proposed Schedule A, 
the OCC has eliminated certain items in Schedule A from revised Form 
PS-01. The OCC believes the removal of these items in proposed Schedule 
A serves the dual purpose of reducing the burden associated with 
reporting Form PS-01 and ensuring that the information obtained from 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers is relevant to the supervision of 
those entities. As discussed further below, Item 1 (Top 100 payment 
stablecoin wallet addresses by number of payment stablecoins) and Item 
2 (Top 100 payment stablecoin wallet addresses by trading volume) from 
proposed Schedule A have been moved to revised Schedule A, with 
modifications, and Item 5 (Top five counterparties by exposure) from 
proposed Schedule A has been moved to revised Schedule B. Item 3 (Top 
100 exchanges by trading volume) and Item 4 (Daily total trading volume 
of all exchanges) from proposed Schedule A have been removed from Form 
PS-01 entirely. These categories of information were eliminated because 
they overlap with other categories of information that the OCC is 
proposing to collect, have limited supervisory utility, and would be 
unnecessarily burdensome to collect.

Proposed Schedule B--Issuance and Redemption

    Proposed Schedule B would collect information on the issuance and 
redemption of stablecoins over the reporting period, as well as 
information on secondary market price and trading activity.
    The OCC received numerous comments concerning proposed Schedule B 
and the information the OCC proposed to be collected. One commenter 
strongly urged the OCC to revisit Schedule B's secondary market price 
and trading activity requirements to ensure issuers are not required to 
report data they cannot reasonably generate or verify themselves. 
Another commenter expressed support for anchoring Schedule B to 
primary-market issuance and redemption data, which is information a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer controls and can reliably report. 
The commenter also claimed that this information is the most probative 
measure of reserve, liquidity, and redemption risk. The commenter 
further stated that permitted payment stablecoin issuers should not be 
required to certify secondary-market activity beyond information they 
can

[[Page 66925]]

reasonably obtain, standardize, and validate. The commenter also 
expressed support for treating every transaction in which a customer 
provides fiat and receives stablecoins as issuance, and every 
transaction in which a customer provides stablecoins and receives fiat 
as redemption, regardless of whether the transaction involves the 
technical minting or burning of tokens. The commenter also expressed 
support for separate treasury-stablecoin line items as a market-
integrity control but stated that those fields should be reported 
quarterly rather than weekly.
    One commenter recommended incorporating additional distributional 
and stress-sensitive measures, including pending, rejected, and 
partially fulfilled redemption requests; major redemption 
counterparties; median and tail redemption times; redemptions exceeding 
contractual timelines; and redemption activity by direct customers, 
intermediaries, affiliates, and unaffiliated parties. The commenter 
explained that these metrics would help the OCC determine whether 
redemption stress is concentrated among particular counterparties, 
channels, or market structures.
    Another commenter stated that the concentration of the reporting 
permitted payment stablecoins issuer's issuance and redemption traffic 
among the counterparties through which it passes, a measure closely 
connected to redemption pressure, is absent from Schedule B. The 
commenter claimed that every permitted payment stablecoin issuer knows 
this with precision from its own transaction records. Another commenter 
stated that Form PS-01 should distinguish direct redemptions from 
redemptions submitted through intermediaries, as well as redemptions by 
affiliates, market makers, and other significant counterparties. The 
commenter claimed that this disaggregation would help the OCC better 
understand how redemption pressure reaches the issuer and whether 
reliance on intermediaries, exchanges, or arbitrageurs could affect 
redemption activity during periods of market stress.
    Several commenters requested clarification of certain aspects of 
Schedule B. One commenter stated that columns under Item 1.a 
(Blockchain name) include several reporting fields with ambiguous 
descriptions that would benefit from clarification. Specifically, the 
commenter explained that the distinction between Column C (Payment 
stablecoins redeemed) and Column D (Payment stablecoins removed from 
circulation) is not clear. The commenter also stated that the 
definition of time-locked stablecoins in Column F (Time-locked) also 
has room for various interpretations.
    Another commenter requested that the OCC: (i) specify that Item 2.e 
(Secondary-market bid/ask spread) is computed across centralized venues 
only, or in the alternative specify a defined proxy methodology for 
automated market maker venues; (ii) specify the computation methodology 
for items 2.c (Estimated secondary-market price) and 2.d (Estimated 
secondary-market peg deviation) across mixed venue populations; and 
(iii) confirm that a reporting issuer may report Items 2.a through 2.e 
from a named third-party market data source.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should also clarify how 
nonrepresentative trades are treated when calculating minimum price, 
maximum price, Volume-Weighted Average Price (``VWAP''), peg deviation, 
and bid/ask spread metrics. The commenter explained that small, stale, 
infrequent, outlier, wash, or affiliated trades may distort reported 
secondary-market conditions if not treated consistently. The commenter 
added that without clear standards governing the treatment of such 
transactions, reported pricing metrics may not accurately reflect 
prevailing market conditions or issuer-specific stress.
    Another commenter observed that proposed Schedule B would require 
reporting on ``Outstanding issuance value at beginning of day'' and 
``Outstanding issuance value at end of day'' and recommended that the 
reporting template be updated to show how the beginning of day and end 
of day balances would be reported for each day of the reporting week.
    Another commenter encouraged the OCC to adopt limitations on trade 
reporting to ensure permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign 
payment stablecoin issuers report only information that provides 
meaningful supervisory value. The commenter suggested excluding small 
transactions and those that do not reflect prevailing market terms, 
such as trades lacking sufficient volume, frequency, or recency. The 
commenter claimed that this approach would better focus Schedule B 
reporting on the information most relevant to monitoring issuer 
liquidity, potential redemption stress, and secondary-market price 
stability.

Revised Schedule A

    As discussed above, the OCC has consolidated certain items from 
proposed Schedule A with proposed Schedule B. As a result, the 
consolidated Schedule A and Schedule B has been re-classified as 
revised Schedule A. The OCC has also implemented numerous changes to 
revised Schedule A to reduce the reporting burden, address comments, 
clarify categories of information and their corresponding instructions, 
and improve the presentation and technical accuracy of certain 
calculations.
    Item 1.a (Digital Ledger Identifier (DLI)) has been amended to 
allow a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to report, if available, 
the DLI for the blockchain network.
    Revisions were made to proposed Columns A through I to clarify the 
calculation of daily changes in outstanding issuance value. 
Calculations on daily changes in outstanding issuance value are 
preserved, and simplified, in revised Columns A through F, while also 
preserving supporting information in revised Columns K and L that were 
reported in proposed Columns E through H.
    Item 1.a of revised Schedule A has been amended to reflect the 
addition of Columns H (Payment stablecoins redeemed through non-reserve 
assets), I (Other payment stablecoin redemption), J (Payment 
stablecoins held in issuer-controlled wallets), and K (Temporarily 
nonredeemable payment stablecoins).
    Column A now requests the blockchain's name instead of the 
outstanding issuance value at the beginning of the day, which has been 
moved to Column B.
    Revised Column B has been further amended to clarify that 
``outstanding issuance value'' is meant as that term is defined in 
section 15.2.
    Revised Column C now requests information on the number of payment 
stablecoins issued during a calendar day. The instructions now clarify 
that ``payment stablecoins'' is meant as that term is defined in 
section 15.2. The amended instructions for Column C also remove the 
reference to ``on each specific blockchain'' from the first sentence 
because it is redundant based on the reporting structure of revised 
Schedule A. The instructions for Column C also remove the direction to 
omit Treasury stablecoins because the instructions now clarify that 
payment stablecoins reported in this column must meet the definition of 
a payment stablecoin in section 15.2.
    Revised Column D now requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to include information about the payment stablecoins redeemed during a 
calendar day. The instructions have been amended to further clarify 
that ``redemption'' is meant as that term is defined in section 
15.12(a)(2). Specifically, redemption is deemed to

[[Page 66926]]

occur when a permitted payment stablecoin issuer has fully processed 
and initiated the outgoing transfer of funds to the customer or the 
customer's financial institution as directed by the customer, or, if 
requested by the customer, credited funds to the customer's account 
with the permitted payment stablecoin issuer. Revised Column D includes 
information that would have been reported in proposed Columns C and D 
of proposed Schedule B.
    Revised Column E has been amended by replacing the requirement to 
report payment stablecoins removed from circulation with a requirement 
to report other changes to the outstanding issuance value. The 
instructions have been clarified to explain that permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers should use this column to report the total par value 
of payment stablecoins added to or removed from the outstanding 
issuance value balance unrelated to payment stablecoin issuance or

changes in outstanding issuance value that would not be captured in 
revised Columns B and C.
    Revised Column F now collects the total outstanding issuance value 
at the end of each calendar day (i.e., as of 11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern)). 
As the instructions explain, this field will automatically be populated 
based on the inputs in Columns B, C, D, and E.
    Columns G through I were added to report similar information to 
proposed Columns C and D, with additional clarity in instructions and a 
new data item in Column I needed to reconcile the detail information on 
daily redemptions to total daily redemptions.
    Revised Column G requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to 
report the daily total of payment stablecoins redeemed where the source 
of funds is directly from reserve assets and the payment stablecoin is 
simultaneously burned.
    Revised Column H now requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to report the daily total value of all payment stablecoins redeemed 
where the source of funds are not reserve assets and the payment 
stablecoin remains outstanding and is not burned.
    Revised Column I requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to 
report the total payment stablecoins that were redeemed, but not 
reported in either Column G (Payment stablecoins redeemed through 
reserve assets) or Column H (Payment stablecoins redeemed through non-
reserve assets).
    Column J requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
all payment stablecoins held in issuer-controlled wallets, commonly 
known as ``Treasury,'' ``corporate held,'' or ``minted but not issued'' 
stablecoins.
    Column K requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
all temporarily nonredeemable payment stablecoins, including, but not 
limited to temporary access restricted and time-locked stablecoins. The 
instructions define ``temporary access-restricted payment stablecoins'' 
to mean those payment stablecoins that are currently held in wallets or 
smart contracts subject to temporary freezes or administrative blocks 
(e.g., blacklist functions). The instructions further define ``time-
locked payment stablecoins'' to mean payment stablecoin on each 
specified blockchain that are held in issuer-imposed smart contracts or

prevent transfer or liquidation until a specific future timestamp or 
block height. The instructions also explain that a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer should also report here any other payment stablecoin 
that is temporarily nonredeemable where the payment stablecoin was 
fully funded by a customer when issued and has not been redeemed. 
Column K now combines Columns E (Temporary access-restricted) and 
Column F (Time-locked) from proposed Schedule B.
    Column L requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
the end-of-day total of payment stablecoins that have been issued but 
not redeemed and are irretrievably lost or inaccessible and not subject 
to redemption by contractual or other means.
    Item 1.b (Total payment stablecoins) of revised Schedule A has been 
amended to replace ``Blockchain name'' with ``Digital Ledger 
Identifier.'' As discussed above, this change was implemented to 
improve the accuracy of the information the OCC collects about the

    Item 2 (Secondary market pricing) of revised Schedule A covers 
secondary market pricing data. Items 2.a through 2.e replace the phrase 
``listed on Schedule A.3'' with ``on which the payment stablecoin is

eliminating proposed Item 3 from proposed Schedule A (Top 100 exchanges 
by trading volume) from Form PS-01.
    The instructions for Item 2.e (Secondary-market bid/ask spread) 
also now clarifies that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should 
exclude from the bid-ask spread calculation, any exchange for which 
end-of-day bid or ask prices are unavailable or for which a bid-ask 
spread is not applicable or cannot be reasonably calculated based on 
the exchange's market structure.
    Item 3 (Liquidity measures) of revised Schedule A requires 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report information related to 
liquidity measures. Item 3.a has been changed to ``Total payment 
stablecoins purchased not yet issued'' from ``Total payment stablecoins 
purchased not yet minted.'' The instructions further clarify that the 
OCC is seeking information about the aggregate count of payment 
stablecoins for which valid purchase orders have been received and 
funds cleared, but for which the corresponding on-chain issuance--not

implemented to clarify that the OCC is seeking information about

issued.
    Item 3.c (Average redemption time) has been amended to clarify that 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers should comply with section 
15.12(a) and (b) for purposes of determining confirmation of a 
redemption request and when a transfer to a payment stablecoin holder 
is initiated.
    Item 3.d has been changed to ``Total redemption dollar amount 
exceeding two business days'' from ``Total redemption dollar amount

15.12(c)(1), which requires a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's

    Item 5 (Top 20 redemptions) was added to revised Schedule A and 
requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report additional 
information related to redemptions in addition to Items 4.a through 
4.c. Specifically, Item 5 requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to report the 20 wallet addresses with the largest cumulative amount of 
redemption transactions successfully processed each reporting day. The 
addresses and the accompanying transaction amounts will be reported 
under Item 5.a. This information is a modification of the data 
requested in proposed Schedule A, Item 2 (Top 100 payment stablecoin 
wallet addresses by trading volume). The OCC is requesting this 
information in response to comments concerning burden, whether the 
issuer has access to or control over third parties and to better align 
with supervisory needs. Permitted payment stablecoin issuers will have 
ready access to verifiable information on redemptions, compared to 
transaction

[[Page 66927]]

activity by wallet addresses that may have no relationship to the 
issuer. The number of addresses was reduced from 100 to 20 to reduce 
the reporting burden. The OCC believes it is important to collect this 
information because redemptions are directly related to a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer's liquidity risk and will allow the OCC to

    Finally, the OCC has incorporated Item 1 (Top 100 payment 
stablecoin wallet addresses by number of payment stablecoins) from 
proposed Schedule A into revised Schedule A as Item 6. However, instead

stablecoins, Item 6 asks permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report

concerning concentration risk being collected is relevant to the 
supervision of permitted payment stablecoin issuers. A few commenters 
recommended implementing a size threshold to avoid forcing permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers to report wallet addresses with payment 
stablecoin holdings that are inconsequential and thus lack relevance to 
the supervision of a permitted payment stablecoin issuer. The OCC is 
also implementing this change to indirectly cap the total number of 
wallets that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer would need to report 
at 50.

Schedule C--Reserve Assets

Schedule E--U.S. Treasury Securities

    Proposed Schedule E would collect information on U.S. Treasury 
securities held as reserve assets. This information would include the 
Committee on Uniform Securities Identification Procedures (``CUSIP'') 
numbers for U.S. Treasury securities, their fair value, remaining 
maturity, coupon rate, effective interest rate, and information about 
the custodian holding the securities, as well as information, such as 
counterparty and collateral haircut, on any U.S. Treasury securities 
encumbered by outstanding repurchase agreements.
    The OCC received several comments related to proposed Schedule E. 
One commenter expressed support for confidential supervisory reporting 
of issuer-maintained Treasury-security data. However, the commenter 
claimed that the proposed sub-24-hour reporting turnaround, combined 
with a requirement to produce daily individual security positions, 
counterparty names, and collateral breakdowns, presents a significant 
operational challenge. The commenter further claimed that standard 
treasury and custody platforms are built to record aggregate holdings, 
fund-level balances, and total counterparty exposures and not the 
instrument-level and counterparty-level detail proposed Form PS-01 
seeks on a continuous daily basis. The commenter added that meeting 
this requirement would necessitate purpose-built data extraction and 
reconciliation workflows that go well beyond the capabilities of 
existing back-office infrastructure. The commenter also stated that the 
OCC should define maturity fields on a remaining-maturity basis 
consistent with the GENIUS Act's 93-days-or-less standard for Treasury 
bills, notes, and bonds. The commenter added that the reporting form 
should not impose, or imply, a separate weighted-average-maturity cap 
or other maturity constraint that narrows the statutory reserve asset 
basket.
    Another commenter observed that proposed Schedule E would collect 
information on U.S. Treasury securities held as reserve assets, 
including the CUSIP numbers for individual U.S. Treasury securities. 
The commenter stated that CUSIP information for U.S. Treasury 
securities is more granular than it is for money market mutual funds 
and would impose substantial burden on permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers. The commenter further recommended that the OCC consider 
collecting aggregated U.S. Treasury security information (e.g., 
bucketed by maturity) that would provide the necessary supervisory 
information without the associated burden of CUSIP-level reporting.
    First, as discussed above, the OCC has modified the reporting 
instructions for Form PS-01 to provide for an additional 24 hours to 
prepare and file Form PS-01. Second, due to permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers' sensitivity to interest rate risk, it is essential 
that the OCC have timely and accurate data concerning a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate risk profile. The information 
proposed to be collected will provide the OCC with the necessary data 
to ascertain a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate 
risk. Finally, the OCC is retaining CUSIP-level reporting because 
Treasuries likely represent the largest source of interest rate risk 
for permitted payment stablecoin issuers. Given this, the OCC believes 
CUSIP-level reporting is necessary to accurately account for a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate risk.

Revised Schedule F

    As discussed above, the OCC has consolidated proposed Schedule A 
and Schedule B in Form PS-01. As a result, proposed Schedule G has been 
re-classified as the revised Schedule F.
    The OCC has added a new Item 1, Money Market Mutual Funds, with 
accompanying instructions that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer is 
required to report information related to money market mutual funds 
held as reserve assets. As result of this addition, Item 1 in proposed 
Schedule G (Fund name) has been reclassified as Item 1.a in revised 
Schedule F. Under Item 1.a., the OCC has made several amendments to the 
instructions for Columns A, C, D, and E and added a new Column J. Under 
proposed Schedule G, Column A would require a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer to report the percentage ownership of a money market 
mutual fund. A commenter explained that it could be operationally 
challenging to determine the ownership percentage of a fund due to 
certain informational challenges, including the mismatch between the 
reporting cadence for money market mutual funds and the weekly 
reporting requirements. In response to this comment, the OCC has 
modified this information collection to only require a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer to indicate if the permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers owns more than 50% of the money market mutual fund. 
The OCC believes that these changes to the information collection will 
alleviate some of the operational concerns commenters raised while also 
ensuring that the OCC has timely information concerning significant 
reserve asset concentrations.
    The OCC is modifying Column C to collect the LEI of the money 
market mutual fund instead of its ticker. The OCC is requesting 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers report the LEI of its money market 
mutual funds instead of tickers to increase the accuracy of the 
information reported and minimize the potential for confusion that 
could arise through the use of ticker symbols.
    The OCC is making clarifying modifications to Column D (Weighted 
average maturity (WAM)). Specifically, the OCC is amending the 
instructions for Column D to clarify that WAM is defined as the dollar-
weighted average time remaining, measured in days, based on the 
percentage of each security's market value in the money market mutual 
fund. This change was implemented to clarify that the OCC is seeking 
the weighted average of the underlying securities and not the money 
market mutual fund. The instructions further clarify that for floating 
rate securities in the money market mutual fund, the maturity shall be 
deemed one day. Finally, the instructions clarify that if the WAM is 
unavailable as of the reporting date, a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer should report the most recent WAM available. Similarly, the OCC 
is modifying the instructions for Column E (Weighted average life 
(WAL)) to clarify that, if the WAL is unavailable as of the reporting 
date, a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should report the most 
recent WAL available.
    Finally, the OCC is adding Column J to revised Schedule F to 
collect information related to whether the reported money market fund 
is tokenized.

Description of Revised Schedule F

    Revised Schedule G would collect information on money market mutual

custodians.

Schedule H--Other Instruments

would act as a catch-all collection information on any reserve assets 
otherwise not covered by the other schedules, for example for any 
physical currency held by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer.
    The OCC received one comment related to proposed Schedule H. 
Specifically, one commenter stated that the instructions should treat 
Schedule H as an exception-based reporting field, and not an invitation 
to create new reserve categories or commingle alternative crypto-assets 
or other non-GENIUS Act assets with the payment stablecoin reserve.

catch-all collection of information to ensure that all reserve assets 
are reported. The inclusion of the schedule is not an invitation to 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers to use impermissible assets as 
reserve assets. The GENIUS Act and the final rule are clear about the 
universe of permissible reserve assets.\25\

Quarterly Reporting Form PS-02

    The proposed quarterly reports of financial condition for permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers 
required by sections 15.14(i) and 15.31(b)(2) mirrors the quarterly 
statements of financial condition that national banks and Federal 
savings associations provide to the Federal banking agencies through 
their quarterly Call Reports.\26\ The information required to be 
reported quarterly will be streamlined substantially relative to the 
Call Reports, in light of the comparatively simple business model of a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer. Standardizing these reporting 
requirements will enhance the OCC's ability to supervise permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers and 
provide clarity as to the information a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer must report. Some OCC-
regulated institutions may already be subject to other reporting 
obligations, notably the Call Report.

    Proposed Schedule A would collect an income statement from the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin

    As a part of the notice, the OCC invited comments on what items 
should be modified or removed, as well as whether any items should be 
added. A few commenters expressed their views on a range of issues 
related to proposed Form PS-02, its instructions, the overall reporting 
structure, and reporting requirements.
    Several commenters provided feedback on the requirement to file 
Form PS-02 and others proposed alternative reporting structures. One 
commenter stated that the OCC should allow permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers to satisfy their quarterly reporting obligations through a 
single report. The commenter stated that OCC-regulated institutions 
that file a Call Report should be exempt from Form PS-02 reporting. The 
commenter further stated that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer 
should be allowed to submit only the Call Report and Form PS-01 to 
satisfy its OCC reporting obligations. According to the commenter, this 
would avoid redundant reporting while preserving the OCC's supervisory 
visibility into a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's financial 
condition. Another commenter stated that the OCC should instead work 
through the Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council, or 
FFIEC, to create a standalone payment stablecoin schedule within the 
Call Report for institutions that already file Call Reports. The 
commenter added that the Call Report schedules should remain the single 
source of institution-level information on income, financial condition, 
off-balance sheet items, and capital. For Federal nonbank permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers, the 
commenter added that the OCC could require a streamlined quarterly Form 
PS-02 containing the stablecoin-specific items in the payment 
stablecoin Call Report schedule.
    Other commenters advocated for Form PS-02 submissions to satisfy 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers quarterly reporting obligations. 
Several commenters stated that OCC-supervised banks should be exempted 
from the obligation to submit Call Reports if their business primarily 
involves issuing payment stablecoins and conducting activities 
incidental to that issuance and redemption and if they comply with the 
proposed quarterly reporting obligations via Form PS-02. One commenter 
explained that the two forms of reporting would be largely duplicative. 
The commenter further stated that to the extent the OCC or other 
commenters identify relevant information gaps between Form PS-02 and 
Call Report fields, the OCC could add additional schedules to Form PS-
02 or expand the scope of Schedule E. The commenter added that since 
the OCC proposes to make information provided via Form PS-02 publicly 
available, the public would not be disadvantaged if banks reporting via 
Form PS-02 are exempted from requirements to submit Call Reports.
    Several commenters stated that OCC-regulated permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers should not be 
required to file both the proposed stablecoin reporting forms and 
duplicative Call Report items covering the same stablecoin data. One 
commenter stated that best solution is a single, uniform, standardized 
stablecoin dataset, distinct from the Call Report that provides 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer-specific detail.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should extract the data 
points it needs from the existing machine-readable Call Report and only 
require entities to report data that is not already required to be 
reported in the Call Report. The commenter claimed that this approach 
will not only reduce reporting burden, but it will also likely improve 
the integrity of the data reported because data is required to be 
prepared once and duplication introduces the chance for errors.
    Several commenters recommended that permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers be required to file both Form PS-02 and the Call Report. One 
commenter explained that requiring separate reporting would help 
distinguish reporting related to GENIUS Act-authorized payment 
stablecoin issuer activities from non-payment stablecoin issuer 
activities, promoting transparency and allowing market

[[Page 66932]]

participants, regulators, and other stakeholders to consistently 
compare information across issuers, regardless of charter type or 
organizational structure. The commenter added that clear linking 
instructions would further reduce reporting burden and ensure 
consistency across reporting requirements. Another commenter stated 
that the OCC should require additional reporting, such as the Call 
Report, for all issuers that are authorized to engage in digital assets 
service provider or other trust charter activities. The commenter also 
stated that the OCC should not limit disclosure by these entities to 
the information in the streamlined quarterly report and these 
institutions should be required to submit a full Call Report. Another 
commenter stated that the quarterly reporting framework should remain 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer-specific and should not rely on 
Call Reports or parent-entity reporting as a substitute for stablecoin 
data. The commenter stated that Call Reports do not capture the 
reserve, redemption, liquidity, token-supply, custody, technology, 
affiliate, and operational-backstop information necessary for GENIUS 
Act supervision. The commenter added that capital and operational-
backstop fields should be calibrated to permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer continuity and orderly wind-down needs, including non-
discretionary operating expenses or tailored unwind costs, rather than 
hard-wiring a 12-month total-expense measure into the reporting form.
    The OCC believes that requiring a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer to provide a quarterly report of condition will allow the agency 
and the public to understand a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's 
financial condition, operations, and the risks unique to its business 
model. This regular data reporting will also allow the OCC to tailor 
its examinations to be risk-based, which will reduce the burden of 
examinations. Further, the OCC acknowledges that there will be some 
overlap between Form PS-02 and the Call Report. However, these two 
reports are important and should remain because they serve two 
different purposes. Call Reports convey information to the public about 
the financial condition of a bank and Form PS-02 will convey 
information to the public about the financial condition of a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer. Following implementation of the final rule, 
the OCC will assess the quarterly reporting framework and consider if 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer should be relieved of some 
reporting obligations.
    Several commenters provided feedback concerning the public 
disclosure of Form PS-02 as well as the process for designating 
confidentiality. One commenter expressed support for publishing 
quarterly financial-condition information at an aggregate, decision-
useful level so the public can understand an issuer's financial 
condition. Another commenter stated that the OCC should establish a 
strong presumption that Form PS-02 information will be publicly 
disclosed. The commenter explained that Form PS-02 will collect 
information that is key to evaluating whether a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer can maintain sufficient reserves, meet redemption 
obligations, manage operational and affiliate risks, and remain 
financially sound. Another commenter stated that the OCC should narrow 
the proposed confidential-treatment process. The commenter observed 
that the instructions allow a reporting entity to seek confidential 
treatment for ``some or all'' portions of Form PS-02 upon a showing 
that disclosure would likely cause substantial competitive harm. The 
commenter claimed that an issuer-by-issuer process could produce 
inconsistent disclosure outcomes, reduce comparability across permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers, and create unnecessary administrative 
burden for both reporting entities and the OCC. The commenter further 
added that confidential treatment should be limited to narrow, 
predefined categories where disclosure would present specific 
cybersecurity, operational, liquidity, counterparty, or market-
stability risks. Another commenter stated that the OCC should preserve 
a clear distinction between confidential supervisory reporting and 
public disclosures. The commenter stated that weekly reporting should 
remain confidential and supervisory-only, particularly for 
counterparty-specific, named-institution, custodian, wallet-level, 
exchange, and secondary-market information. The commenter added that 
public disclosure should instead be limited to aggregate, decision-
useful quarterly information that helps payment stablecoin holders and 
market participants understand an issuer's financial condition without 
revealing information that could enable front-running, destabilize 
banking or custody relationships, or transmit stress between permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and their reserve counterparties.
    The OCC's presumption is that each quarterly Form PS-02 for each 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer will be publicly available. It is 
vitally important that the public has access to timely and reliable 
data concerning the financial condition of a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer. The availability of Form PS-02 is further consistent 
with the GENIUS Act's requirement that permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers publish their reserve composition monthly.\28\ However, the OCC 
is maintaining the ability to grant a request for confidentiality when 
the facts and circumstances warrant such accommodation. The OCC expects 
to use this authority sparingly.

management has ownership of a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's 
financial reporting function. Similarly, the OCC believes that 
requiring all members of the board of directors and senior management 
to attest to the correctness of quarterly reports will help ensure that 
the board of directors and senior management are engaged in the 
oversight of the permitted payment stablecoin issuers' financial 
reporting function. Upon implementation, the OCC will continue to

Item 10.
    Item 6 is the sum of Items 2 through 5 of revised Schedule D.
    Item 7 requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
capital elements which is the total of Item 1 (Total equity capital)

    Under the Operational Backstop section of revised Schedule D, the 
OCC is moving Item 7 (12-months total expenses) from proposed Schedule 
D to Item 11 in revised Schedule D. The new Item 11 contains two 
clarifying revisions. First, ``months'' and the citation ``12 CFR 
15.41(b)'' in the first sentence have been revised to ``months' '' and 
``12 CFR 15.41(a)(2),'' respectively. The OCC is also changing the 
heading of Item 11 from ``12-month total expenses'' to ``Operational 
backstop requirement'' for clarity. The OCC has also updated the 
instructions to Schedule D to state that one-time expenses that are 
wholly unrelated to the continuing operation of the permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer may be excluded from the calculation of the previous 
12 months' total expenses in Schedule D, Item 11 if the permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer has received prior written approval from the 
OCC pursuant to 12 CFR 15.41(b)(1)(iii).
    The OCC is also changing the heading of Item 8 from ``Composition 
of operational backstop'' to ``Operational backstop assets'' for 
clarity. Also, under Item 8, the OCC is also adding Item 8.e, which is 
simply a total of Items 8.a through 8.d.
    The OCC is also adding a new category of information, Operational 
backstop debt, as Item 9. Item 9 requires permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers to report the total amount of operational backstop debt that 
meets the requirements outlined in 12 CFR 15.40(c). The instructions 
further explain that the reported debt cannot exceed 50 percent of the 
amount reported in revised Schedule D, Item 11 (Operational backstop 
requirement).
    The OCC is also adding a new Item 10, which requires permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers to report the operational backstop assets 
funded by equity. This figure is the total of Schedule D, Item 8.e 
(Total operational backstop assets) less Schedule D, Item 9 
(Operational backstop debt).
    Finally, the OCC is adding a new Item 12, which requires permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers to report the minimum capital amount that 
the permitted payment stablecoin issuer must hold based on the 
requirements in 12 CFR 15.41(a), as of the report date.

Schedule E--Memorandum

    Proposed Schedule E would collect additional information about a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's or a foreign payment stablecoin 
issuer's operations, including issuer type, other reserve asset income 
and expenses, other income and expenses, other assets and liabilities, 
number of branded stablecoins issued, non-U.S. dollar assets and 
liabilities, average reserve assets, stablecoin issuances, redemption, 
and burn data, blockchains, redemption metrics, and custody activities.
    As a part of the notice, the OCC invited comment on proposed 
Schedule E. Several commenters recommended that the OCC require the 
token-level DTI instead of, or in addition to, the blockchain name in 
Item 14.a of proposed Schedule E, so that per-ledger issuance and 
redemption figures tie to a specific, externally verifiable token 
implementation rather than a non-standardized market label.
    As previously discussed, the OCC has revised the reporting forms, 
including revised Schedule E, to collect DTI information where 
appropriate.

Revised Schedule E

    (b) The accuracy of the OCC's estimate of the burden of the 
collection of information.
    The OCC received several comments concerning the burden estimates 
in the notice. Several commenters claimed that the burden estimates 
predate the reporting forms and were not derived from the forms and 
instructions. The commenters also questioned the estimate of one hour 
weekly to prepare the Form PS-01 due to volume and granularity of the 
data to be collected and recommended that the OCC estimate burden based 
on the proposed forms.
    In contrast, several other commenters expressed the view that the 
proposed reporting is either a byproduct of data permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers already produce internally or that after the initial 
setup of the reporting function, the ongoing reporting burden is 
minimal.
    In response to these comments, the OCC has elected to review its 
burden estimates associated with the ongoing compliance reporting 
burden. Upon review, the OCC has determined that an upward revision to 
the estimate of weekly ongoing compliance reporting burden for Form PS-
01 from one hour to two hours is appropriate. The increase in the 
estimate of the weekly ongoing compliance reporting burden reflects the 
OCC's acknowledgement that the agency is seeking a substantial amount 
of information and that this information takes time to compile, 
validate, and submit. The OCC considered allocating additional time to 
the preparation and submission of Form PS-01 but ultimately concluded 
that the revision to two hours was appropriate because the OCC's 
expectation is that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer will collect 
many, if not all, of the data points sought by Form PS-01 in real time. 
For example, permitted payment stablecoin issuers are required to 
constantly maintain reserve assets backing their payment stablecoins at 
a ratio of at least 1:1. In order to ensure continuous compliance with 
this requirement, a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should have 
internal controls to monitor in real-time the total outstanding 
issuance of its payment stablecoins and its total reserve assets. The 
maintenance of these fundamental controls should allow a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer to readily produce reporting about the 
issuance and redemption of its payment stablecoins and its reserve 
asset composition. This data can be incorporated directly into Form PS-
01 or at least inform the figures that ultimate populate Form PS-01. 
Similarly, the OCC expects that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer 
would have internal control to monitor on-chain activity and this on-
chain surveillance could be utilized to populate certain transaction 
and market data required by Form PS-01. In sum, Form PS-01 seeks a 
substantial amount of data, but the OCC expects permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers to have internal control systems capable of 
generating much of the information sought through the form.
    In the final rule, the OCC estimates a total annual compliance 
burden associated with the submission of Form PS-01 of 4,056 hours. 
This represents an increase of 2,548 hours from the estimate in the 
June 12, 2026, notice.\35\

    (d) Ways to minimize the burden of the collection on respondents,

    In response to the request for feedback on ways to minimize the 
burden of the collection on respondents, the agency received several 
recommendations on ways to minimize burden. One commenter presented 
several recommendations including specifying a structured, machine-
readable filing format for both forms, using the XBRL standard, 
adopting a common ontology across all agencies and reporting regimes 
containing defined terms and definitions related to blockchain and 
digital assets, and harmonizing the OCC's and the FDIC's reporting 
structures. Another commenter stated that the single most consequential 
burden minimization measure would be the specification of a structured, 
machine-readable format. Another commenter recommended utilizing 
standardized machine-readable fields rather than recurring narrative 
responses,
    As discussed above, the OCC will ensure that the reporting forms 
are consistent with the FDTA and the agencies' joint rule.
    Similarly, another commenter recommended that the Federal 
stablecoin regulators jointly adopt reporting forms. The commenter 
stated that this would improve consistency, reduce burden, and align 
with the statutory mandate of the GENIUS Act.
    The OCC has coordinated closely with the other primary Federal 
payment stablecoin regulators. Further, the OCC has taken seriously its 
obligation to coordinate with other agencies, as appropriate, and does 
not believe that it is necessary to do so through a joint rule or joint 
reporting forms. The OCC will continue to coordinate with other Federal 
payment stablecoin regulators as appropriate.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should revise Form PS-01 so 
that Schedules A, D, E, G, and H only require weekly data, rather than 
requiring end-of-day data for each calendar day in the reporting week. 
Alternatively, the commenter stated that the OCC could require daily 
data capture and reporting only upon notice from the OCC. The commenter 
stated that a weekly snapshot would reduce the reporting burden on 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers without eliminating supervisory 
information and would also reduce the time required for the OCC to 
review Form PS-01 submissions.
    As discussed above, the OCC is amending the instructions for Form 
PS-01 to reduce the weekly reporting burden on smaller issuers to allow 
those issuers that are not currently subject to a formal enforcement 
proceeding or order; have an outstanding issuance value, together with 
any affiliated permitted payment stablecoin issuers, of less than $1 
billion; and are in compliance with all of the reserve requirements set 
forth in section 15.11 and the reporting requirements in section 15.14, 
to satisfy their obligations for revised Schedules D through G by only 
submitting the required data for the last day of the reporting period 
(i.e., each Monday) for each payment stablecoin it issues. The OCC 
believes this approach serves the goals of reducing the reporting 
burden for smaller issuers while also ensuring that the OCC receives 
the information necessary to supervise permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers. In addition, once reporting has commenced, the OCC will 
continue to assess the information collection and consider if further 
revisions are warranted.
    Another commenter explained that the daily reporting requirement 
multiplies extraction, validation, reconciliation, exception 
management, and data-governance work across each affected schedule. 
According to the commenter, for Schedules B (Issuance and Redemption), 
C (Reserve Assets), and F (Reverse Repurchase Agreements), this burden 
is well justified by the supervisory value of monitoring data that 
closely correlates with risks to permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
or individual stablecoins. However, the commenter claimed that several 
of Form PS-01's schedules are unlikely to have meaningful daily 
fluctuations. Specifically, proposed Schedule A (General) primarily 
tracks information on the largest wallets and most active exchanges. 
The commenter explained that proposed Schedule A's data fields will 
undoubtedly change daily because of ordinary settlement flows, 
operational transfers, or short-lived trading activity, but these 
changes are unlikely to provide insight into sustained concentrations 
or material trends as compared to weekly snapshots and aggregates. 
Similarly, the values in proposed Schedule D (Cash Balances) are likely 
to exhibit daily noise as a result of normal operations without 
corresponding supervisory value.
    The commenter further explained that other Form PS-01 schedules are 
more appropriate for weekly reporting precisely because they contain 
numerous fields that will not change significantly from day to day. 
Specifically, proposed Schedule E (U.S. Treasury Securities), proposed 
Schedule G (Money Market Mutual Funds), and proposed Schedule H (Other 
Instruments) are substantially composed of static and slow-changing 
information. CUSIPs, coupon rates, maturity dates, custodians, and 
other security-identification fields do not ordinarily change each day, 
and so will be of little value for daily tracking in Schedule E. The 
commenter added that the same is true for many of the money market 
mutual fund fields in proposed Schedule G and the fields in proposed 
Schedule H. Weekly reporting of fair value and amortized cost of 
Treasuries, WAM, and ownership concentrations of money market mutual 
funds, or fair value and WAM of other instruments in proposed Schedule 
H should provide sufficient information for the OCC to permit effective 
supervision.
    The revisions to Form PS-01 reflect that the OCC considered these 
comments. For example, the OCC

[[Page 66939]]

eliminated certain items from proposed Schedule A and refined the 
information collected related to wallets to focus on data that is 
relevant to the supervision of permitted payment stablecoin issuers. In 
addition, once reporting has commenced, the OCC will assess the 
information collection and consider further revisions are warranted. 
For example, the OCC may consider modifying some data fields in Form 
PS-01 from daily reporting to weekly reporting if they are found to be 
static.
