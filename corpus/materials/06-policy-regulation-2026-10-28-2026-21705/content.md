## 摘要（发布方原文）
The OCC, as part of its continuing effort to reduce paperwork and respondent burden, invites comment on a new information collection, as required by the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995 (PRA). In accordance with the requirements of the PRA, the OCC may not conduct or sponsor, and the respondent is not required to respond to an information collection unless it displays a currently valid Office of Management and Budget (OMB) control number. The OCC is proposing a new information collection titled "Reporting Forms and Instructions for Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers Subject to the Jurisdiction of the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency," that would include weekly and quarterly reporting forms that must be completed by permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers. The OCC is seeking a new OMB control number for this information collection. The OCC also is giving notice that it has sent the collection to OMB for review.

## 正文 1｜Item 1.
Item 1.a (Digital Ledger Identifier (DLI)) has been amended to 
allow a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to report, if available, 
the DLI for the blockchain network.
    Revisions were made to proposed Columns A through I to clarify the 
calculation of daily changes in outstanding issuance value. 
Calculations on daily changes in outstanding issuance value are 
preserved, and simplified, in revised Columns A through F, while also 
preserving supporting information in revised Columns K and L that were 
reported in proposed Columns E through H.

## 正文 2｜Item 1.
Item 1.a of revised Schedule A has been amended to reflect the 
addition of Columns H (Payment stablecoins redeemed through non-reserve 
assets), I (Other payment stablecoin redemption), J (Payment 
stablecoins held in issuer-controlled wallets), and K (Temporarily 
nonredeemable payment stablecoins).
    Column A now requests the blockchain's name instead of the 
outstanding issuance value at the beginning of the day, which has been 
moved to Column B.
    Revised Column B has been further amended to clarify that 
``outstanding issuance value'' is meant as that term is defined in 
section 15.2.
    Revised Column C now requests information on the number of payment 
stablecoins issued during a calendar day. The instructions now clarify 
that ``payment stablecoins'' is meant as that term is defined in 
section 15.2. The amended instructions for Column C also remove the 
reference to ``on each specific blockchain'' from the first sentence 
because it is redundant based on the reporting structure of revised 
Schedule A. The instructions for Column C also remove the direction to 
omit Treasury stablecoins because the instructions now clarify that 
payment stablecoins reported in this column must meet the definition of 
a payment stablecoin in section 15.2.
    Revised Column D now requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to include information about the payment stablecoins redeemed during a 
calendar day. The instructions have been amended to further clarify 
that ``redemption'' is meant as that term is defined in section 
15.12(a)(2). Specifically, redemption is deemed to

[[Page 66926]]

occur when a permitted payment stablecoin issuer has fully processed 
and initiated the outgoing transfer of funds to the customer or the 
customer's financial institution as directed by the customer, or, if 
requested by the customer, credited funds to the customer's account 
with the permitted payment stablecoin issuer. Revised Column D includes 
information that would have been reported in proposed Columns C and D 
of proposed Schedule B.
    Revised Column E has been amended by replacing the requirement to 
report payment stablecoins removed from circulation with a requirement 
to report other changes to the outstanding issuance value. The 
instructions have been clarified to explain that permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers should use this column to report the total par value 
of payment stablecoins added to or removed from the outstanding 
issuance value balance unrelated to payment stablecoin issuance or 
redemption. Revised column E is necessary to accurately calculate daily 
changes in outstanding issuance value that would not be captured in 
revised Columns B and C.
    Revised Column F now collects the total outstanding issuance value 
at the end of each calendar day (i.e., as of 11:59:59 p.m. (Eastern)). 
As the instructions explain, this field will automatically be populated 
based on the inputs in Columns B, C, D, and E.
    Columns G through I were added to report similar information to 
proposed Columns C and D, with additional clarity in instructions and a 
new data item in Column I needed to reconcile the detail information on 
daily redemptions to total daily redemptions.
    Revised Column G requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to 
report the daily total of payment stablecoins redeemed where the source 
of funds is directly from reserve assets and the payment stablecoin is 
simultaneously burned.
    Revised Column H now requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to report the daily total value of all payment stablecoins redeemed 
where the source of funds are not reserve assets and the payment 
stablecoin remains outstanding and is not burned.
    Revised Column I requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to 
report the total payment stablecoins that were redeemed, but not 
reported in either Column G (Payment stablecoins redeemed through 
reserve assets) or Column H (Payment stablecoins redeemed through non-
reserve assets).
    Column J requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
all payment stablecoins held in issuer-controlled wallets, commonly 
known as ``Treasury,'' ``corporate held,'' or ``minted but not issued'' 
stablecoins.
    Column K requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
all temporarily nonredeemable payment stablecoins, including, but not 
limited to temporary access restricted and time-locked stablecoins. The 
instructions define ``temporary access-restricted payment stablecoins'' 
to mean those payment stablecoins that are currently held in wallets or 
smart contracts subject to temporary freezes or administrative blocks 
(e.g., blacklist functions). The instructions further define ``time-
locked payment stablecoins'' to mean payment stablecoin on each 
specified blockchain that are held in issuer-imposed smart contracts or 
vesting schedules that are solely controlled by the issuer, and that 
prevent transfer or liquidation until a specific future timestamp or 
block height. The instructions also explain that a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer should also report here any other payment stablecoin 
that is temporarily nonredeemable where the payment stablecoin was 
fully funded by a customer when issued and has not been redeemed. 
Column K now combines Columns E (Temporary access-restricted) and 
Column F (Time-locked) from proposed Schedule B.
    Column L requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
the end-of-day total of payment stablecoins that have been issued but 
not redeemed and are irretrievably lost or inaccessible and not subject 
to redemption by contractual or other means.

## 正文 3｜Item 1.
Item 1.b (Total payment stablecoins) of revised Schedule A has been 
amended to replace ``Blockchain name'' with ``Digital Ledger 
Identifier.'' As discussed above, this change was implemented to 
improve the accuracy of the information the OCC collects about the 
blockchain a payment stablecoin operates on.
    Item 2 (Secondary market pricing) of revised Schedule A covers 
secondary market pricing data. Items 2.a through 2.e replace the phrase 
``listed on Schedule A.3'' with ``on which the payment stablecoin is 
traded.'' This change was implemented to reflect that the OCC is 
eliminating proposed Item 3 from proposed Schedule A (Top 100 exchanges 
by trading volume) from Form PS-01.
    The instructions for Item 2.e (Secondary-market bid/ask spread) 
also now clarifies that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should 
exclude from the bid-ask spread calculation, any exchange for which 
end-of-day bid or ask prices are unavailable or for which a bid-ask 
spread is not applicable or cannot be reasonably calculated based on 
the exchange's market structure.
    Item 3 (Liquidity measures) of revised Schedule A requires 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report information related to 
liquidity measures. Item 3.a has been changed to ``Total payment 
stablecoins purchased not yet issued'' from ``Total payment stablecoins 
purchased not yet minted.'' The instructions further clarify that the 
OCC is seeking information about the aggregate count of payment 
stablecoins for which valid purchase orders have been received and 
funds cleared, but for which the corresponding on-chain issuance--not 
minting--transaction has not been finalized. These changes were 
implemented to clarify that the OCC is seeking information about 
payment stablecoins that have been purchased--minted or not--but not 
issued.

## 正文 4｜Item 3.
Item 3.c (Average redemption time) has been amended to clarify that 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers should comply with section 
15.12(a) and (b) for purposes of determining confirmation of a 
redemption request and when a transfer to a payment stablecoin holder 
is initiated.

## 正文 5｜Item 3.
Item 3.d has been changed to ``Total redemption dollar amount 
exceeding two business days'' from ``Total redemption dollar amount 
exceeding 48 hours.'' This change was implemented to align with section 
15.12(c)(1), which requires a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's 
redemption policy to state that timely redemption may not exceed two 
business days following the date of the requested redemption.
    Item 5 (Top 20 redemptions) was added to revised Schedule A and 
requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report additional 
information related to redemptions in addition to Items 4.a through 
4.c. Specifically, Item 5 requires permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
to report the 20 wallet addresses with the largest cumulative amount of 
redemption transactions successfully processed each reporting day. The 
addresses and the accompanying transaction amounts will be reported 
under Item 5.a. This information is a modification of the data 
requested in proposed Schedule A, Item 2 (Top 100 payment stablecoin 
wallet addresses by trading volume). The OCC is requesting this 
information in response to comments concerning burden, whether the 
issuer has access to or control over third parties and to better align 
with supervisory needs. Permitted payment stablecoin issuers will have 
ready access to verifiable information on redemptions, compared to 
transaction

[[Page 66927]]

activity by wallet addresses that may have no relationship to the 
issuer. The number of addresses was reduced from 100 to 20 to reduce 
the reporting burden. The OCC believes it is important to collect this 
information because redemptions are directly related to a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer's liquidity risk and will allow the OCC to 
monitor and assess trends and concentration in redemption activity in 
normal and stress environments.
    Finally, the OCC has incorporated Item 1 (Top 100 payment 
stablecoin wallet addresses by number of payment stablecoins) from 
proposed Schedule A into revised Schedule A as Item 6. However, instead 
of seeking the top 100 payment stablecoin wallets by number of payment 
stablecoins, Item 6 asks permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report 
all wallets that hold more than two percent of the total outstanding 
payment stablecoins as of the end of each day. Item 6.a now requires 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers to report the addresses and 
corresponding payment stablecoin holdings as of the end of each day. 
The OCC is implementing this change to ensure that the information 
concerning concentration risk being collected is relevant to the 
supervision of permitted payment stablecoin issuers. A few commenters 
recommended implementing a size threshold to avoid forcing permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers to report wallet addresses with payment 
stablecoin holdings that are inconsequential and thus lack relevance to 
the supervision of a permitted payment stablecoin issuer. The OCC is 
also implementing this change to indirectly cap the total number of 
wallets that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer would need to report 
at 50.

Description of Revised Schedule A

    In summary, revised Schedule A collects similar information on the 
issuance and redemption of stablecoins over the reporting period, 
information on secondary market price and trading activity, liquidity 
measures, and concentration metrics as proposed Schedule B.

Schedule C--Reserve Assets

    Proposed Schedule C would collect information on the reserve assets 
used to back the outstanding issuance of a stablecoin issued by a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer. This information would include a 
list of all reserve assets by type, as well as information on each 
class of reserve assets, including fair value and amortized cost. 
Schedule C would collect, among other information, the balance of 
assets within each asset category that are in tokenized form.
    The OCC received numerous comments concerning proposed Schedule C 
and the information proposed to be collected. One commenter stated that 
reserve reporting should improve supervisory visibility without 
narrowing the statutory reserve criteria. The commenter added that fair 
value is the appropriate uniform measure for reserve sufficiency 
because it best reflects whether reserve assets support the at-all-
times 1:1 backing requirement.\21\ The commenter further added that 
reporting fields should not impose a de facto weighted-average-maturity 
cap or create special limits for off-the-run Treasury securities.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \21\ See 12 U.S.C. 5903(a)(1)(A).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Another commenter observed that FDIC PS-01a would collect a weekly 
minimum, maximum and average reserve asset coverage ratio (i.e., 
reserve assets against outstanding issuance), but this ratio does not 
appear on Form PS-01, although it is derivable from data both full 
forms already collect. The commenter claimed that collecting this 
information would enhance the utility of the information collected at 
negligible additional burden and would place the most direct indicator 
of reserve adequacy on the face of the form.
    In the notice, the OCC invited comments on whether additional 
information about tokenized assets should be included elsewhere in the 
reporting form, for example, specific items on tokenized deposits or 
tokenized money market funds. One commenter stated that the reporting 
fields should not treat tokenized forms of otherwise eligible reserve 
assets as separate asset classes and the final instructions should make 
clear that tokenized forms of otherwise eligible reserve assets are 
reportable within the corresponding underlying category. Another 
commenter stated that the OCC should adopt a technology-neutral 
approach with respect to reporting reserve assets. The commenter 
claimed that reporting reserve assets should not depend upon the 
technology or recordkeeping used to record those assets; if a tokenized 
U.S. Treasury security and a non-tokenized U.S. Treasury security are 
both held as reserve assets and represent the same claim on the U.S. 
government, their distinction for reserve adequacy purposes appears 
immaterial.

Revised Schedule B

    As discussed above, the OCC has consolidated proposed Schedule A 
and Schedule B in Form PS-01. As a result, proposed Schedule C has been 
re-classified as the revised Schedule B.
    Items 1 and 1.a through 1.f remain unchanged from proposed Schedule 
B. The OCC has revised Item 1.g (Total reserve assets) by adding 
``before considering liabilities on repurchase agreements'' to the end 
of the last sentence. The OCC added this language to clarify that the

available to support the outstanding payment stablecoins net of

## 正文 6｜Item 1.
Item 1.h (Outstanding repurchase agreement liabilities) adds a new 
information collection for outstanding repurchase agreement 
liabilities. This item was moved from proposed Schedule D, Item 1.c, 
Column E (Repurchase Agreement Liabilities) to ensure accurate 
calculation of Column B and provide additional clarity on Reserve asset 
value. As proposed, Column B would not have resulted in accurate 
calculations because the underlying formula relied upon gross reserve 
assets, not reserve assets net of repurchase agreement liabilities.

the regulatory definition of Reserve asset value in section 15.2.\22\ 
Moving this item from proposed Schedule D improves clarity by 
consolidating all components of Reserve asset value on the same

amount of repurchase agreement liabilities related to money received 
from repurchase agreements that are held as reserve assets.

reserve assets. Thus, the OCC has included this category of information 
in revised Schedule B.

Schedule D--Cash Balances Schedule D

    Proposed Schedule D would collect information on cash balances held 
as reserve assets. This information would include items on the location 
and size of insured and uninsured deposits maintained at depository 
institutions, as well as any balances maintained at a Federal Reserve 
Bank. Proposed Schedule D would also collect aggregate information on 
repurchase agreement transactions and associated liabilities. Proposed 
Schedule D includes line items for cash balances held at U.S. 
depository institutions and foreign depository institutions.
    In the notice, the OCC invited comment on the proper handling of 
U.S. and foreign cash balances, as well as whether there should be 
other distinctions among cash balances serving as payment stablecoin 
reserves. One commenter expressed support for reporting cash reserves 
by location and size, including balances maintained at Federal Reserve 
Banks and at U.S. and foreign depository institutions, provided that 
this information remains confidential and supervisory-only. The 
commenter added that the reporting instructions should reflect the 
GENIUS Act's express recognition that reserve assets may include demand 
deposits at foreign branches, agents, and correspondent banks of U.S. 
insured depository institutions under Sec.  4(a)(1)(A)(ii).\23\ The 
commenter added that the reporting form should capture lawful reserve 
activity; it should not be designed in a way that presupposes or 
effectively imposes a U.S.-only reserve-location requirement that is 
left explicitly open in the GENIUS Act.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \23\ See 12 U.S.C. 5903(a)(1)(A)(ii).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The OCC disagrees that the instructions for proposed Schedule D 
require revisions to explicitly state which cash balances are 
permissible reserve assets. The GENIUS Act and the final rule identify 
the suite of reserve assets, including cash balances, that a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer may hold.
    Another commenter stated that proposed Schedule D only requests 
aggregate deposits in U.S. and foreign depository institutions. The 
commenter further claimed that uninsured deposits represent the highest 
credit risk in reserve assets.
    In response to this comment, as discussed below, the OCC has 
incorporated institution-level reporting into the revised schedule.

Item 1.a of revised Schedule C also includes a new column requesting

financial institution. This change is being incorporated in response to 
a comment concerning the risk associated with uninsured cash deposits. 
This reporting will provide the OCC with relevant detail about the

Schedule E--U.S. Treasury Securities

    Proposed Schedule E would collect information on U.S. Treasury 
securities held as reserve assets. This information would include the 
Committee on Uniform Securities Identification Procedures (``CUSIP'') 
numbers for U.S. Treasury securities, their fair value, remaining 
maturity, coupon rate, effective interest rate, and information about 
the custodian holding the securities, as well as information, such as 
counterparty and collateral haircut, on any U.S. Treasury securities 
encumbered by outstanding repurchase agreements.
    The OCC received several comments related to proposed Schedule E. 
One commenter expressed support for confidential supervisory reporting 
of issuer-maintained Treasury-security data. However, the commenter 
claimed that the proposed sub-24-hour reporting turnaround, combined 
with a requirement to produce daily individual security positions, 
counterparty names, and collateral breakdowns, presents a significant 
operational challenge. The commenter further claimed that standard 
treasury and custody platforms are built to record aggregate holdings, 
fund-level balances, and total counterparty exposures and not the 
instrument-level and counterparty-level detail proposed Form PS-01 
seeks on a continuous daily basis. The commenter added that meeting 
this requirement would necessitate purpose-built data extraction and 
reconciliation workflows that go well beyond the capabilities of 
existing back-office infrastructure. The commenter also stated that the 
OCC should define maturity fields on a remaining-maturity basis 
consistent with the GENIUS Act's 93-days-or-less standard for Treasury 
bills, notes, and bonds. The commenter added that the reporting form 
should not impose, or imply, a separate weighted-average-maturity cap 
or other maturity constraint that narrows the statutory reserve asset 
basket.
    Another commenter observed that proposed Schedule E would collect 
information on U.S. Treasury securities held as reserve assets, 
including the CUSIP numbers for individual U.S. Treasury securities. 
The commenter stated that CUSIP information for U.S. Treasury 
securities is more granular than it is for money market mutual funds 
and would impose substantial burden on permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers. The commenter further recommended that the OCC consider 
collecting aggregated U.S. Treasury security information (e.g., 
bucketed by maturity) that would provide the necessary supervisory 
information without the associated burden of CUSIP-level reporting.
    First, as discussed above, the OCC has modified the reporting 
instructions for Form PS-01 to provide for an additional 24 hours to 
prepare and file Form PS-01. Second, due to permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers' sensitivity to interest rate risk, it is essential 
that the OCC have timely and accurate data concerning a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate risk profile. The information 
proposed to be collected will provide the OCC with the necessary data 
to ascertain a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate 
risk. Finally, the OCC is retaining CUSIP-level reporting because 
Treasuries likely represent the largest source of interest rate risk 
for permitted payment stablecoin issuers. Given this, the OCC believes 
CUSIP-level reporting is necessary to accurately account for a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's interest rate risk.

Schedule G--Money Market Mutual Funds

    Proposed Schedule G would collect information on money market 
mutual funds held as reserve assets, including fund name and sponsor, 
CUSIP, net asset value (``NAV''), and effective interest rate, WAM, WAL 
as well as information on custodians.
    The OCC received several comments related to proposed Schedule G. 
One commenter expressed support for confidential supervisory reporting 
of issuer-maintained money market fund data--including fund name, 
sponsor, CUSIP, NAV, and custodian. However, the commenter added that 
requiring a daily effective interest rate is not

[[Page 66930]]

feasible because interest on money market fund holdings accrues and is 
reported on a monthly cycle, making daily rate calculation 
operationally inconsistent with how these instruments are administered 
and recorded. The commenter stated that the instructions should clarify 
that eligible reserve assets in this category are limited to Government 
money market funds that maintain a stable $1.00 NAV and whose 
underlying assets remain within the GENIUS Act's reserve-asset 
perimeter and prime and municipal money market funds should not be 
treated as eligible reserve assets. The commenter also recommended that 
the OCC require a single, clearly defined maturity measure rather than 
both WAM and WAL. The commenter stated that given the short-duration, 
high-liquidity nature of eligible Government money market funds, 
requiring both measures would add a duplicative reporting burden 
without materially improving supervisory insight.
    The OCC declines to make changes to WAL and WAM reporting, as both 
measures are required to be calculated and reported by money market 
mutual funds.\24\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \24\ See 17 CFR 270.2a-7(d).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Another commenter stated that the percentage ownership field for 
money market mutual funds in proposed Schedule G should be replaced 
with a field measuring either the fair value or NAV-based value of the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer's holdings in the fund. The 
commenter added that calculating the permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer's percentage ownership of the fund will require external data 
that may not be available to a permitted payment stablecoin issuer on 
the required reporting cadence, and so completing the form may require 
a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to engage with external vendors 
to source necessary information.
    As discussed below, the OCC is revising the schedule to only 
require a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to indicate if the 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer holds more than 50% of a money

    The OCC has added a new Item 1, Money Market Mutual Funds, with

Schedule F. Under Item 1.a., the OCC has made several amendments to the 
instructions for Columns A, C, D, and E and added a new Column J. Under 
proposed Schedule G, Column A would require a permitted payment 
stablecoin issuer to report the percentage ownership of a money market 
mutual fund. A commenter explained that it could be operationally 
challenging to determine the ownership percentage of a fund due to 
certain informational challenges, including the mismatch between the 
reporting cadence for money market mutual funds and the weekly 
reporting requirements. In response to this comment, the OCC has 
modified this information collection to only require a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer to indicate if the permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers owns more than 50% of the money market mutual fund. 
The OCC believes that these changes to the information collection will 
alleviate some of the operational concerns commenters raised while also 
ensuring that the OCC has timely information concerning significant 
reserve asset concentrations.
    The OCC is modifying Column C to collect the LEI of the money 
market mutual fund instead of its ticker. The OCC is requesting 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers report the LEI of its money market 
mutual funds instead of tickers to increase the accuracy of the 
information reported and minimize the potential for confusion that 
could arise through the use of ticker symbols.
    The OCC is making clarifying modifications to Column D (Weighted 
average maturity (WAM)). Specifically, the OCC is amending the 
instructions for Column D to clarify that WAM is defined as the dollar-
weighted average time remaining, measured in days, based on the 
percentage of each security's market value in the money market mutual 
fund. This change was implemented to clarify that the OCC is seeking 
the weighted average of the underlying securities and not the money 
market mutual fund. The instructions further clarify that for floating 
rate securities in the money market mutual fund, the maturity shall be 
deemed one day. Finally, the instructions clarify that if the WAM is 
unavailable as of the reporting date, a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer should report the most recent WAM available. Similarly, the OCC 
is modifying the instructions for Column E (Weighted average life 
(WAL)) to clarify that, if the WAL is unavailable as of the reporting 
date, a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should report the most 
recent WAL available.
    Finally, the OCC is adding Column J to revised Schedule F to 
collect information related to whether the reported money market fund 
is tokenized.

Quarterly Reporting Form PS-02

    The proposed quarterly reports of financial condition for permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers 
required by sections 15.14(i) and 15.31(b)(2) mirrors the quarterly 
statements of financial condition that national banks and Federal 
savings associations provide to the Federal banking agencies through 
their quarterly Call Reports.\26\ The information required to be 
reported quarterly will be streamlined substantially relative to the 
Call Reports, in light of the comparatively simple business model of a 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer. Standardizing these reporting 
requirements will enhance the OCC's ability to supervise permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers and 
provide clarity as to the information a permitted payment stablecoin 
issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer must report. Some OCC-
regulated institutions may already be subject to other reporting 
obligations, notably the Call Report.

commenter further stated that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer

satisfy its OCC reporting obligations. According to the commenter, this 
would avoid redundant reporting while preserving the OCC's supervisory 
visibility into a permitted payment stablecoin issuer's financial 
condition. Another commenter stated that the OCC should instead work 
through the Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council, or 
FFIEC, to create a standalone payment stablecoin schedule within the 
Call Report for institutions that already file Call Reports. The 
commenter added that the Call Report schedules should remain the single 
source of institution-level information on income, financial condition, 
off-balance sheet items, and capital. For Federal nonbank permitted 
payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers, the 
commenter added that the OCC could require a streamlined quarterly Form 
PS-02 containing the stablecoin-specific items in the payment 
stablecoin Call Report schedule.
    Other commenters advocated for Form PS-02 submissions to satisfy 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers quarterly reporting obligations. 
Several commenters stated that OCC-supervised banks should be exempted 
from the obligation to submit Call Reports if their business primarily 
involves issuing payment stablecoins and conducting activities 
incidental to that issuance and redemption and if they comply with the 
proposed quarterly reporting obligations via Form PS-02. One commenter 
explained that the two forms of reporting would be largely duplicative. 
The commenter further stated that to the extent the OCC or other 
commenters identify relevant information gaps between Form PS-02 and 
Call Report fields, the OCC could add additional schedules to Form PS-

the OCC proposes to make information provided via Form PS-02 publicly 
available, the public would not be disadvantaged if banks reporting via 
Form PS-02 are exempted from requirements to submit Call Reports.

stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers should not be 
required to file both the proposed stablecoin reporting forms and 
duplicative Call Report items covering the same stablecoin data. One 
commenter stated that best solution is a single, uniform, standardized 
stablecoin dataset, distinct from the Call Report that provides 
permitted payment stablecoin issuer-specific detail.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should extract the data 
points it needs from the existing machine-readable Call Report and only 
require entities to report data that is not already required to be 
reported in the Call Report. The commenter claimed that this approach 
will not only reduce reporting burden, but it will also likely improve 
the integrity of the data reported because data is required to be 
prepared once and duplication introduces the chance for errors.
    Several commenters recommended that permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers be required to file both Form PS-02 and the Call Report. One 
commenter explained that requiring separate reporting would help 
distinguish reporting related to GENIUS Act-authorized payment 
stablecoin issuer activities from non-payment stablecoin issuer 
activities, promoting transparency and allowing market

participants, regulators, and other stakeholders to consistently

Report, for all issuers that are authorized to engage in digital assets 
service provider or other trust charter activities. The commenter also 
stated that the OCC should not limit disclosure by these entities to

    (b) The accuracy of the OCC's estimate of the burden of the 
collection of information.
    The OCC received several comments concerning the burden estimates 
in the notice. Several commenters claimed that the burden estimates 
predate the reporting forms and were not derived from the forms and 
instructions. The commenters also questioned the estimate of one hour 
weekly to prepare the Form PS-01 due to volume and granularity of the 
data to be collected and recommended that the OCC estimate burden based 
on the proposed forms.
    In contrast, several other commenters expressed the view that the 
proposed reporting is either a byproduct of data permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers already produce internally or that after the initial 
setup of the reporting function, the ongoing reporting burden is 
minimal.
    In response to these comments, the OCC has elected to review its 
burden estimates associated with the ongoing compliance reporting 
burden. Upon review, the OCC has determined that an upward revision to 
the estimate of weekly ongoing compliance reporting burden for Form PS-
01 from one hour to two hours is appropriate. The increase in the 
estimate of the weekly ongoing compliance reporting burden reflects the 
OCC's acknowledgement that the agency is seeking a substantial amount 
of information and that this information takes time to compile, 
validate, and submit. The OCC considered allocating additional time to 
the preparation and submission of Form PS-01 but ultimately concluded 
that the revision to two hours was appropriate because the OCC's 
expectation is that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer will collect 
many, if not all, of the data points sought by Form PS-01 in real time. 
For example, permitted payment stablecoin issuers are required to 
constantly maintain reserve assets backing their payment stablecoins at 
a ratio of at least 1:1. In order to ensure continuous compliance with 
this requirement, a permitted payment stablecoin issuer should have 
internal controls to monitor in real-time the total outstanding 
issuance of its payment stablecoins and its total reserve assets. The 
maintenance of these fundamental controls should allow a permitted 
payment stablecoin issuer to readily produce reporting about the 
issuance and redemption of its payment stablecoins and its reserve 
asset composition. This data can be incorporated directly into Form PS-
01 or at least inform the figures that ultimate populate Form PS-01. 
Similarly, the OCC expects that a permitted payment stablecoin issuer 
would have internal control to monitor on-chain activity and this on-
chain surveillance could be utilized to populate certain transaction 
and market data required by Form PS-01. In sum, Form PS-01 seeks a 
substantial amount of data, but the OCC expects permitted payment 
stablecoin issuers to have internal control systems capable of 
generating much of the information sought through the form.
    In the final rule, the OCC estimates a total annual compliance 
burden associated with the submission of Form PS-01 of 4,056 hours. 
This represents an increase of 2,548 hours from the estimate in the 
June 12, 2026, notice.\35\

    The OCC has coordinated closely with the other primary Federal

payment stablecoin regulators as appropriate.
    Another commenter stated that the OCC should revise Form PS-01 so 
that Schedules A, D, E, G, and H only require weekly data, rather than 
requiring end-of-day data for each calendar day in the reporting week. 
Alternatively, the commenter stated that the OCC could require daily 
data capture and reporting only upon notice from the OCC. The commenter 
stated that a weekly snapshot would reduce the reporting burden on 
permitted payment stablecoin issuers without eliminating supervisory 
information and would also reduce the time required for the OCC to 
review Form PS-01 submissions.
    As discussed above, the OCC is amending the instructions for Form 
PS-01 to reduce the weekly reporting burden on smaller issuers to allow 
those issuers that are not currently subject to a formal enforcement 
proceeding or order; have an outstanding issuance value, together with 
any affiliated permitted payment stablecoin issuers, of less than $1 
billion; and are in compliance with all of the reserve requirements set 
forth in section 15.11 and the reporting requirements in section 15.14, 
to satisfy their obligations for revised Schedules D through G by only 
submitting the required data for the last day of the reporting period 
(i.e., each Monday) for each payment stablecoin it issues. The OCC 
believes this approach serves the goals of reducing the reporting 
burden for smaller issuers while also ensuring that the OCC receives 
the information necessary to supervise permitted payment stablecoin 
issuers. In addition, once reporting has commenced, the OCC will 
continue to assess the information collection and consider if further 
revisions are warranted.
    Another commenter explained that the daily reporting requirement 
multiplies extraction, validation, reconciliation, exception 
management, and data-governance work across each affected schedule. 
According to the commenter, for Schedules B (Issuance and Redemption), 
C (Reserve Assets), and F (Reverse Repurchase Agreements), this burden 
is well justified by the supervisory value of monitoring data that 
closely correlates with risks to permitted payment stablecoin issuers 
or individual stablecoins. However, the commenter claimed that several 
of Form PS-01's schedules are unlikely to have meaningful daily 
fluctuations. Specifically, proposed Schedule A (General) primarily 
tracks information on the largest wallets and most active exchanges. 
The commenter explained that proposed Schedule A's data fields will 
undoubtedly change daily because of ordinary settlement flows, 
operational transfers, or short-lived trading activity, but these 
changes are unlikely to provide insight into sustained concentrations 
or material trends as compared to weekly snapshots and aggregates. 
Similarly, the values in proposed Schedule D (Cash Balances) are likely 
to exhibit daily noise as a result of normal operations without 
corresponding supervisory value.
    The commenter further explained that other Form PS-01 schedules are 
more appropriate for weekly reporting precisely because they contain 
numerous fields that will not change significantly from day to day. 
Specifically, proposed Schedule E (U.S. Treasury Securities), proposed 
Schedule G (Money Market Mutual Funds), and proposed Schedule H (Other 
Instruments) are substantially composed of static and slow-changing 
information. CUSIPs, coupon rates, maturity dates, custodians, and 
other security-identification fields do not ordinarily change each day, 
and so will be of little value for daily tracking in Schedule E. The 
commenter added that the same is true for many of the money market 
mutual fund fields in proposed Schedule G and the fields in proposed 
Schedule H. Weekly reporting of fair value and amortized cost of 
Treasuries, WAM, and ownership concentrations of money market mutual 
funds, or fair value and WAM of other instruments in proposed Schedule 
H should provide sufficient information for the OCC to permit effective 
supervision.
