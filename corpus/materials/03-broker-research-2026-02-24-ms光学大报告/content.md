Into the Spotlight: Optical Market Opportunities
The optical market is set to reach $65bn+ in our '28 base case (~30% CAGR) on the back of increasing speed/investment in AI data centers. As traditional networks reach their limits, new optical technologies are poised for adoption, potentially adding $23bn in TAM, taking the industry to ~$90bn.
Key Takeaways
In our base case, optical markets are set to grow to $65bn+ by 2028 from ~ $30bn in 2025.
We have done significant work to size new optical technologies as physical limits emerge, creating an additional ~$23bn TAM in '28 and beyond.
■ Our base case, which assumes ~15% co-packaged optics (CPO) penetration in scale-out networks by 2028, could see optical space reach ~$90bn in coming years.
Unknowns are time for a packaging ecosystem to emerge in CPO and how long certain laser markets stay in tightness, supporting pricing.
At current valuations, GLW is the best LT opportunity amid eventual copper to fiber transition; most cautious LITE on permanence of margin strength/valuation.
We estimate expanding optical reach as speed increases create ~$23bn in incremental optical opportunities over the coming years. We estimate optical markets (fiber, scale-out, scale-across) total ~$30bn today (2025), having grown at a ~40% CAGR over the last 4 years as AI data center investment took off. Speed upgrades with traditional architectures, and innovations like NVLink, have largely kept AI networks scaling to date. However, looking forward, with the bandwidth requirements set to continue to grow, and theoretical limits being reached on existing technologies (e.g. copper transmission lengths, lane / laser speeds, I/O constraints), new technological evolutions need to take place. We outline five markets in this note that are set to see more optical investment as speeds of the network increase, namely 1) transition to optics from copper in scale-up, 2) co-packaged optics (CPO) (on-chip), 3) CPO (on-board), 4) optical circuit switching (OCS), and 5) passive optical for out-of-band management. We also detail some of the existing markets. We estimate these innovations total a $23bn opportunity by 2028, though timing of many of them like copper to fiber may take place later. While some of these, particularly on-board co-packaged optics eat into current optical profit pools, we still see the space reaching a ~$90bn TAM in our base case. We believe this note can be a good roadmap for when and how these market opportunities develop, what the impact will be, and better reflect what is and isn't being priced into these stocks.

Biggest unknown is pricing, pace of CPO adoption, and pricing of lasers / transceivers. However, there are two dynamics at play that could significantly impact market estimates for this space: 1) CPO in scale-out, which obviates the need for a transceiver, and 2) tightness of the Electro-absorption Modulated Laser (EML) market. The reason for these being the greatest unknowns. First, while in CPO, about half of the BOM moves onto the switch, laser revenue should continue to grow, but transceiver revenue would see headwinds. We estimate in our base case only ~15% CPO adoption in scale-out in our base case, largely based on Yole estimates. We will explore a bear and bull case in future pieces. The second is tightness of the EML market. The EML / laser market traditionally has priced down 10-15% a year. However, with record tightness, and a quicker than expected transition to 200G lanes, there have been little in the way of pricing discounts for 2 years, with price increases instead (evidenced most clearly in LITE's 800bps+ increase in gross margins Y/Y). This creates a double impact to market size in coming years if capex data points are not revised up, capacity increases are large enough to keep, and portion of the market that moves to alternative laser types increases (see Appendix for more detail). Executive Summary
What is priced into optical currently. With the four optical names up 300%+ over the last year and ~50% YTD, there is significant enthusiasm about optical right now, as the market remains in tightness, demand remains high, which is leading to more long term supply agreements being reached. The combination is leading to record profitability for the space currently, along with increased visibility, taking some of the concerns around volatility out of the space and attracting new investors. We attempt in this piece to give a sense of what these new and existing market opportunities could be, and adjust our estimates to better reflect these upcoming opportunities. We fully acknowledge this is something we have been on our back foot on for much of the last six months as these names have taken off, being too concerned with long term profitability vs. peak earnings power today. We also recognize that many of these names could continue to run, particularly as we head into optical conferences over the next month like OFC / Nvidia's GTC, which bring some optical names to the forefront. What is the cloud optical opportunity today? As we have written about extensively, and noted in market estimates below, the optical market today is largely scale-out (in the form of transceivers connected with optical fiber that plug into switches) and scale-across / data center interconnect (in the form of either pluggable modules connected with fiber or transponders, which are connected by fiber and largely connect into routers). See Exhibit 14 and 15.
This data center market used to be a relatively small market relative to the telco/ enterprise market (<30% of the market in 2021), but with cloud / AI, it has increased in importance, set to become 78% of the market by 2028.
This market has historically traded on upgrade cycles that have happened in the cloud every 2-3 years, or telco every 4-6 years, with outperformance driven by who was gaining share at subsequent generations. We saw this with the 100G / 400G / 800G cycle on transceivers, and the 100G / 400G / 800G cycle on DCI.
With concentrated customers, and a diversified ecosystem of suppliers, this space has exhibited significant volatility, with investors tending to pay special attention to gross margins, given it is a space where there are traditionally 10-15% price downs a year, with where the market is in those price downs largely dependent on where the market is in the cycle (e.g. tends to be more attractive in first ~2 years vs. second ~2 years).
With the strength of capex data points, and more consolidated set of suppliers (LITE acquiring Oclaro and NeoPhotonics, II-VI acquiring Finisar and Coherent, Nokia acquiring Infinera), we are seeing pricing increases, differently than in past cycles, allowing market participants to see levels of earnings growth far in excess of revenue growth.

Copper to Fiber Transition (~$5bn of copper cabling transitioning to fiber, $1-2bn of AEC / DAC transceivers transitioning to optical – Will total to more than existing market)
What are the limitations of copper? Copper has traditionally been preferable in data center architectures, given it is cheaper (at least up front), is more reliable (electrical vs. optical signals), and uses less power. Scale-up distances are typically short—less than 1–2 meters— covering chip-to-chip, board-to-board, backplane, or direct attach cable (DAC) connections. However, as the chart highlights, the speeds of AI networks are beginning to push the limitations of copper (~5m with AEC, but likely cut that in half at Rubin). While NVDA has committed to keeping copper through Rubin, the next generations will likely require a move to optics.
How much more expensive is optical? In terms of reliability / power consumption, Microsoft Research noted in a recent study that power consumption / failure rates could be up to 100x worse with optics vs. copper. More so than upfront cost, this loss of reliability and increased power is the greatest overhang. There are also multiple types of technologies being experimented with in order to reduce the power drain and increase the reliability of the network, such as MicroLEDs and hollow-core fiber (HCF).
Relative scale of opportunity. Current DAC / AEC market is ~$1 bn (according to Omdia). On average, today optical cables at the same speed are at least 2x the cost of complementary copper ones. If we cut the difference, and assume 50% uplift, this adds another $500mm to the market. Corning highlights a meaningfully higher fiber density as well, which will not be linear, but given driven by volume vs. costs, it has the opportunity to provide meaningful uplift as well. We would note we don't have ACC market estimates, so this likely underestimates the cabling opportunity to some degree.
What about the fiber opportunity? Good market data is not available for the cabling market. You can look at three different sources and get widely different answers, but arrive at north of the $5bn the copper content is expected to be by 2030. Co-packaged Optics (CPO) – Scale-Up (~$3bn market by 2028)
Move to optics from copper in scale-up is an occasion to introduce CPO (not on-chip yet, but eventually on-chip). There are still a lot of technical challenges to be figured out with CPO. 1) CPO puts the optics on board, which are higher failure than the electronics, increasing the cost of failure and preventing "hot-swapping". 2) Second, high manufacturing costs – CPO carries ~8-10x higher ASPs than traditional pluggable solutions 3) A lack of ecosystem maturity and standardization, as CPO concentrated with AVGO and NVDA vs a broad ecosystem of transceiver vendors, concentrating economics of the ecosystem even further. 4) CPO's technical complexity currently results in low yields. However, in order to accommodate the I/O and bandwidth needs, CPO will be needed (moving away from just onboard optics to on-chip optics).
Eventually, on chip co-packaged optics helps solve the I/O problem. The core idea of CPO is to integrate optical engines (such as optical transceiver modules) directly into the package of a switch chip or other processing chips, thereby enhancing data transmission speeds, shortening the transmission distance, reducing power consumption, and minimizing latency. CPO brings together a wide range of expertise in fiber optics, digital signal processing (DSP), application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) switches, and packaging & test to provide disruptive system value for the data center and cloud infrastructure. While there are multiple forms of CPO, for the purposes of scale-up, bandwidth needs are advanced, creating an I/O problem, that Optical I/O or 2.5D / 3D CPO is the direction this market would be headed.

How big is this opportunity? OCS works like a traditional telephone switchboard, where a connection is formed until broken and reformed with another cross-connect. By eliminating conversion of traffic to packets, and keeping in the optical plane, reducing the OEO conversions, latency of the network is reduced. As a result, OCS can be more challenging where traffic flows are more dynamic. As a result, it tends to not be used for spine replacement, but as an overlay to add reliability to the network (applications 2 and 4 in Exhibit below).

Out of Band Management (2-5% of Ethernet Switching Market – $3bn)
What is Out-of-Band Management? Out-of-Band Management has traditionally been a top-of-rack (ToR) switch that runs outside of the traditional production network. The purpose is to provide a dedicated alternate access method into network infrastructure, allowing for monitoring, management, troubleshooting, rebooting devices in the production network. This has traditionally been handled by a relatively expensive ToR switch (does not need to be high speed) and copper cabling (again – not high speed). The cost is seemingly 2-5% of the rack (networking and optics), likely closer to 2-3% for AI given how expensive the racks are. At 2-3% of ~$150bn cloud networking market (between switching / transceivers), you get a $3-4bn market.
Ciena's DCOM improves scalability and reduces space and power. Ciena has co-designed a data center out-of-band management (DCOM) solution with META and Ciena is seeing interest from other hyperscalers and neoclouds for a similar co-design given the power and space savings. Ciena's DCOM solution reduces the number of active network elements and replaces traditional Ethernet aggregation switches with a more efficient and scalable alternative based on Passive Optical Network (PON) technology. PON technology allows for 99% space savings and 30% power reduction when power remains a major constraint for the cloud providers. While Ciena worked with META on this technology for 2 years, the company expects a co-design with another hyperscaler or neo-cloud could take closer to 6 months as there might be some changes needed for other cloud customers (e.g., like changes in split ratios). DCOM (datacom out-of-band management) is another fully incremental opportunity in near term for Ciena. Ciena has a couple hundred million in revenue for DCOM and with two other hyperscalers in deep technological discussions. While AI opportunity is strong, DCOM is a product that could be made my multiple parties who have traditionally competed in the PON market (like NOK).

Coherent Lite
Coherent lite bridges the gap between IMDD and traditional coherent. Coherent lite is a lower cost, lower power form of coherent optics designed for shorter reach, bridging the gap between Intensity Modulated Direct Detect (IMDD) and full long haul coherent. While IMDD remains lowest cost and power, it becomes increasingly reach and performance limited at higher speeds. Traditional coherent offers better reach and robustness but with higher cost and complexity. Coherent lite removes some of the heavier functionality of traditional coherent. As speeds increase to 800G and 1.6T, the power gap between IMDD and coherent narrows, making Coherent lite economically viable. Coherent lite use cases are for campus, OCS architectures, and inside the data center for high speeds. Campus is particularly a clear use case given 2-20km exceeds IMDD's reach at higher speeds, but doesn’t require traditional coherent.
