The Morgan Stanley Agentic Playbook
We detail our agentic playbook/frameworks across leading platforms and downstream players (e-com, travel, gig). META/ AAPL/AMZN/EBAY/SHOP among cleanest winners. We also size META Muse and explain why GOOGL needs to ship products to avoid disruption, OTAs seem oversold, CHWY and the rideshare industry more at risk.
The Agentic Debate is Back: The recent launch of Meta’s Muse agent (see here) and Instinct's rise in popularity have pushed the agentic debate back to the forefront...as consider that “agentic battleground" names like the online travel agencies have fallen 6%-12% over the past \~2 weeks as fears of agentic disruption have re-ignited across the market.
We Think Muse is an Important Development as it 1) Is garnering adoption (9 straight days in the top 5 downloaded apps), 2) Is doing a good job at driving personalized interactions and potentially monetizable behavior while leveraging its FB/IG and other data sets (see here) 3) Is integrating mobile web browsing and interaction into its use cases (see Exhibit 8 - Exhibit 10 showcasing how Muse navigates to transact) and 4) Is high utility and free, with 100mn free weekly tokens (which could likely support 100+ queries per day). Many of these capabilities have been possible in the past with other tools (with Gemini, Grok and others)...but META's go to market seems (for now) to be hitting stride.
So What is the Morgan Stanley Agentic Playbook? In Internet: Agentic Shoppers Are Coming… Who Could Win or Lose? we detailed our views and frameworks for how we think about agentic winners and those at risk. Given Muse is here, we want to remind investors of 7 key points about our agentic frameworks and thoughts.

2. Which Platforms are Best Positioned to Drive and Benefit From the Rise of Horizontal Agents? We see scaled distribution and large unique data sets as being critical to personalized agentic offerings and adoption, and continue to believe leading tech platforms like META, GOOGL, and AAPL are among the best positioned. AAPL (covered by Erik Woodring) and GOOGL’s software to hardware integration (iOS and Android) gives them an edge in enabling applications and capabilities across their ecosystems, while META’s advantage lies in personalization powered by its own 1P data. Driving adoption is ultimately a change management and behavior modification exercise, so go to market execution and showcasing sources of utility will matter too. It’s early (<10 days in) but META's Muse has shown some encouraging traction so far.
3. How Big Could Meta Muse Be? Every 100mn Users Could Add 1% to '28 EPS: While Muse could monetize with subscription revenue (charging \$20/month for 500m weekly tokens and \$100/month for 3bn weekly tokens), in our view, the larger opportunity is around monetizable behavior and transactions. We expect META to eventually charge a commission for Muse enabled transactions. While we don’t know the commission percentage, we think search is a helpful analogy as search CPCs are effectively real-time advertiser bid-based commissions.
Putting numbers behind the scalability of this, consider that if Muse can reach 100mn users and generate 5 queries per day, 10% of them monetizable (vs. 5 per day on GOOGL and 20% of them monetizable)...and charge an effective CPC that is 20% of GOOGL search, it is likely to generate over \$1bn of annual revenue and \~ \$0.35 of EPS (1% upside). Continued strong go to market, new transactional partnerships to remove friction, and incrementality will be key, but our sensitivity work shows the scalability of this high margin business...with the potential for 15%+ upside to '28 EPS. Agentic commercial behavior seems to be budding too with Instinct reportedly running \$1,300/month in purchases per user through Instinct, admittedly biased given early tech adopters tend to be higher income consumers. Please let us know if interested in our interactive Meta AI search model, which we hope will be helpful as we learn more about adoption, monetizable behavior and commission rates.

7. Could GOOGL Search Face Headwinds From An Agentic “Take-Rate”
Transition?: As detailed here, we think the launch of these improving agents makes it even more important that GOOGL ships next generation agentic products across its leading base of search users. This is because if other scaled platforms like META (or other private players) are able to scale agentic offerings and capture meaningful commercial behavior and dollars away from Google Search, GOOGL may be forced to disrupt its current leading search monetization model to adapt.
Consider that we estimate current search per transaction "effective take rates" (ad spend as a percent of GMV) are \~5-10X higher than early agentic take rate offerings from earlier this year. We acknowledge take rates may change, but this in our view speaks to the monetization risk if GOOGL losses share of traffic.

Given this dynamic, it's critical that GOOGL continues shipping next gen products and agentic offerings across its leading corpus of users (and leveraging its leading data) to stay at the top of the agentic funnel… and drive more transactions for advertisers. But as a further offset, consider that 80%+ of retailer online traffic is still unpaid/direct. We expect this to fall over time as agentic offerings scale…and our sensitivity shows how shifting unpaid traffic toward paid commission traffic can offset lower "per transaction take rates". As an example, a 500bp shift in unpaid to paid traffic can offset a 1,400bp decline in effective take rates. However, it would require a 3,800bp shift from unpaid to paid channels for GOOGL to break even at a 5% take rate. This again speaks to how retailers need to focus on incrementality, transaction profitability, and preserving their high margin direct business…because GOOGL's incentive to use its next generation agents to eat into that high margin direct business is likely to only grow over time.
