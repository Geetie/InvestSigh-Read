Building on our 1Q26 AI Tracker, this 2Q26 Tracker focuses on: 1) AI capex and data center projects; 2) GPU, ASIC and CPU market and server market model update; and 3) financial performance of suppliers in the AI supply chain.
Since June, the AI supply chain has entered a correction, driven by delays to Nvidia’s Kyber rack, Kimi K3 shock and broad deleverage of momentum stocks. Despite this pullback, memory, PCB, and testers are among the top gainers, with sampled stocks price up \~100%-200% YTD. This compares to negative stock performance for CSPs. Despite ongoing noise around spec changes for future AI accelerators and rack architectures, component suppliers are accelerating capex this year to get ahead of demand in 2027-28. IC Substrate companies in particular are receiving customer support through mechanisms such as prepayments, capex sharing, and LTAs (Exhibit 1).
Total investment in planned and in-construction data centers is around US\$1.2Tr. Post-2Q26 earnings, consensus raised 2026 capex estimates for major CSPs by nearly 15% vs. Mar 2026, and model total capex to grow 61% CAGR to \~US\$1110B by 2027. More new AIDC projects were announced, including Meta’s \$50B Hyperion project (up from \$27B in Nov 2025), a \$14B venture with BlackRock (not covered) in El Paso datacenter, as well as a \$9B AI data center in Canada. Softbank Group announced it would launch neocloud services “SB Neo”, scaling in phases toward a 10GW capacity target by around 2030; also, the Group will invest up to \$85B to develop 5 GW of data center capacity in France. ASIC adoption is also accelerating, Broadcom striking a long-term deal with Google to develop future generations of TPU, and Marvell ramping in ASICs for all four major CSPs.
Global server market is set to break US\$1Tr mark by 2028. We revise up total server and GPU AI server shipments CAGR to 15% and 22%, respectively, over 2025–28E (Model). We expect 8-GPU equivalent servers shipment to grow 48% this year, and total rack shipment to be 61K/88K in 2026/2027 (Exhibit 16). Both Rubin rack and AMD Helios rack is set to start shipment in 4Q26. We project ASICs to comprise c.45% of the total CoWoSbased AI chip shipment & TPU shipment to grow c.90% YoY this year. The momentum will stay strong in 2027 too. And our Asia Semis team rates MediaTek Outperform as the company has won 2 TPU projects & is on track to bring its first one to production later this year (report, model). Agentic AI also drives a significant demand for CPU benefiting Arm and Advantest, among others.
In YTD 2026, among 29 AI server supply-chain stocks, 7 have seen higher valuation multiples and 21 have seen P/E compression (Exhibit 29-Exhibit 30). Valuation contraction has been broad-based across the AI supply chain in recently, particularly for memory and PCB—likely reflecting AI bubble concerns, a general risk-off rotation in technology stocks, and lingering ROI concerns for CSPs and frontier labs. Despite valuation contraction, consensus 2026/27 EPS estimates have risen for most of our sampled stocks. Our top picks in Asia hardware coverage are Unimicron (near-term) and Delta (long term).

FOR ACCELERATORS (XPU), DEMAND INFLECTS TO ASIC, BUT GPU STILL REPRESENTS THE MAJORITY. ADDITIONALLY, PRICE & BUSINESS MODEL OF HBM IS BECOMING A SIGNIFICANT UNCERTAINTY ON THE SIZE OF THESE MARKETS.
ASIC adoption continued to accelerate, as hyperscalers moved from pilot deployment to scaled rollouts across training-adjacent and inference workloads. We see 2026 as another important inflection, when AI capex stays elevated and custom silicon becomes a more explicit lever for improving performance-per-dollar, lowering total cost of ownership, and reducing dependence on external GPU supply chains. MediaTek has recently revised up its 2026 ASIC revenue to “exceed US \$2B” and expects a US\$80B TAM and 15-20% market share in 2027 (MediaTek 2Q26: Another upward revision to ASIC & more). Broadcom has struck a long-term deal with Google to develop and supply future generations of TPU through 2031 (news), and has extended partnership with Meta (news). Marvell points to ramp in custom accelerators for all four major CSPs (news). All of these developments suggest ASICs are no longer just a tactical supplement to GPUs, but an increasingly structural layer in AI infrastructure planning.
We project ASIC can be 20% of XPU market in 2027, up from 10% last year, but how HBM is priced & sourced presents a significant uncertainty to this projection. We currently project the TAM of XPU, i.e. GPU+ASIC, will be US\$500B in 2027 (Exhibit 6), with GPU still commanding a lion’s share in this TAM thanks to NVIDIA’s superior performance in training workloads, but ASIC benefitting from the inflection from training to inference & will grow faster. We forecast ASIC to double its market share from 10% last year to 20% in 2027 (Exhibit 7). In terms of accelerator shipment, we forecast TPU shipment to grow over 90% YoY in 2026 and account for \~42% of the total CoWoS-based ASIC shipment (Exhibit 8). More importantly, the increased intention to serve more tokens at more affordable costs recently makes us see an upside risk to the ASIC adoption, but HBM makes modeling the upside difficult now. That is because HBM is bound to have a price hike YoY in 2027, but the size of the hike is still being negotiated. And some ASIC suppliers (e.g. Broadcom) sourcing HBM for customers and bundling it as part of the ASIC revenue makes the size of ASIC market dependent on the HBM price hike. The same uncertainty also exists in GPU, as GPU suppliers (e.g. NVIDIA) also have the same practice and has HBM as part of the cost & price of their GPUs.
Ignoring the likely distortion from HBM price, we currently model TPU to be 60% of AI ASIC market, representing at least a US\$90B opportunity in 2028. If we ignore the uncertainty from HBM price, AIDC ASIC market is modeled to reach US \$140-160B in 2028, and c. half of that is HBM (Exhibit 9). Specifically for TPU, we expect it will be nearly 70% of the ASIC market in 2027 and c. 60% in 2028 (Exhibit 10 - Exhibit 11). All these however are subject to the HBM price, as well as how ASIC supply chain deals with HBM going forward, especially higher HBM price should prompt customers to source HBM directly to avoid possible mark-up charged by ASIC service providers.
