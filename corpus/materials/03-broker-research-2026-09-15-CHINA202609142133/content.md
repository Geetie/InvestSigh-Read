China's AI Path
Consumer AI: Beyond the Chatbot Race
China leads global 2C AI adoption thanks to strong distribution and clear use cases. While standalone AI-native apps capture early mindshare, in-app AI offers better ROI. We see AI accelerating existing mobile-internet monetization and estimate a Rmb300bn China 2C AI monetizable TAM by 2030.
AlphaWiseα
China is ahead at the consumer-adoption layer, driven by distribution and usecase fit. Our AlphaWise survey shows 80% weekly personal AI usage in China vs. 54% in the US. Basic access is free in both markets, but China's advantage is that free access is coupled with ecosystem distribution, CNY campaigns and a broader range of everyday use cases. AI is riding an already mature mobile-internet layer and is increasingly embedded into existing apps.
AI-native apps win early consumer mindshare, but in-app AI has better ROI. ByteDance's Doubao remains the clear AI-native app leader, at ~400mn MAU, but our AlphaWise survey also shows that multi-homing is deepening rather than fading. We continue to prefer in-app assistants over AI-native apps, from an economic perspective, because in-app assistants can monetize existing traffic, proprietary data, permissions, merchant supply and payment/fulfillment without rebuilding user habits from scratch. The next competitive metric should increasingly move from MAU to task completion, AI-attributed conversion, and GMV.
We estimate ~Rmb300bn of China 2C AI monetizable TAM by 2030. Unlike traditional internet platforms, which benefit from high fixed costs and low marginal costs, AI products face a structurally different cost curve – every additional query consumes inference compute. We view consumer AI primarily as an accelerant for existing mobile-internet revenue rather than a standalone revenue pool. We size China's 2C AI monetizable TAM of ~Rmb300bn by 2030, with 99% coming from merchant-side intent monetization.
Stock implications – favor distribution and vertical execution; stay disciplined on standalone-app spend. We favor Tencent (WeChat moat looks more likely to be enhanced than displaced by Doubao) and vertical leaders (Meituan, Taobao, OTA, etc.) whose existing products already own demand, data and execution, where incremental acquisition cost is low and conversion can be measured. BABA remains our top pick given its full-stack AI capabilities, but our view specifically on its consumer AI initiatives is more balanced: Taobao/Qwen Shopping Assistant and the broader commerce stack are strategically attractive, while the economics of heavy standalone Qwen-app investment warrant continued discipline. We are more cautious on Baidu, TME and iQIYI because AI can disrupt their core business – search, music and video content creation.

Executive Summary
Tech Diffusion
A Morgan Stanley Research Key Theme of 2026
What the market had assumed…
• AI-native apps will become the dominant consumer AI entry point.
• Consumer AI monetization will mainly resemble US freemium software: paid subscriptions first, with advertising added later.
• The best foundation model should determine the consumer winner.
• AI-native apps are the main disruption threat to incumbents apps.
What our analysis reveals instead…
Distribution and use-case fit explain more of China’s consumer-adoption lead than frontier-model superiority. Basic access is free in both China and the US; China has combined it with stronger ecosystem distribution, CNY promotions and a broader set of daily-use scenarios. Our AlphaWise survey shows weekly personal AI use at 80% in China vs. 54% in the US. China personal AI daily-use scenarios remain concentrated in information, creation, decision support and, increasingly, commerce-related tasks.
• AI has also diffused faster than mobile internet did at a comparable stage. China’s generative-AI user base rose from ~249mn in Dec-2024 to ~602mn in Dec-2025, consistent with AI scaling as a software layer on top of an already mature mobile infrastructure.
• AI-native apps are efficient at user education and top-of-funnel intent capture, but multi-homing is accelerating rather than fading. QuestMobile July data show Doubao at ~399mn monthly average users (MAU), Qwen ~161mn, DeepSeek ~124mn and Yuanbao ~51mn; 64% of our AlphaWise survey respondents used five or more tools, which keeps switching costs low and limits winner-take-all economics.
In-app AI assistants have the clearest near-term ROI because they start with existing traffic, data and execution rights. Tencent illustrates the distinction. Our AlphaWise survey shows 83% past-month usage of in-app AI; 75% of respondents find AI search/chatbot features in shopping apps useful, and 57% say these features would make them purchase more often. Tencent illustrates the distinction: Yuanbao remains subscale as a standalone app, while WeChat’s social graph, Mini Programs and payments give AI a much stronger path to task completion and monetization inside the core app.
We view consumer AI as an accelerator to existing internet monetization. We estimate ~Rmb300bn of China 2C AI monetizable revenue TAM by 2030E, with ~99% coming from merchant-funded intent monetization through advertising and AI-attributed transaction commissions. Because much of that revenue already exists today, the incremental upside comes from better conversion, monetization and share gains. In-app AI can still earn attractive returns because it leverages an existing user base and distribution.

1. China has moved from user education to mass adoption faster than the US because AI is a software layer on top of mature mobile infrastructure.
China's consumer AI penetration is already mainstream. Our latest AlphaWise survey shows China's weekly personal AI usage rising to 80% from 75% in two months, and weekly work-related usage to 77% from 74% in three months. The latest US wave is ~54% for work and personal AI usage. The comparison is not perfectly like-for-like across market structure and survey timing, but the direction is clear: China has diffused consumer AI faster despite the US retaining an advantage in frontier compute and several frontier models.
We believe five factors continue to explain the gap:
• China has stronger consumer distribution channels because AI is being inserted into super apps and vertical apps rather than requiring a new download.
• Free access lowers trial friction in both China and the US. China gains more from it because free AI is pushed through large ecosystems, reinforced by CNY campaigns and attached to a wider set of everyday use cases.
• Many mainstream use cases are low-to-mid token intensity.
• China's platforms have stronger commercial closed loops.
• Daily consumer use cases often require a model that is good enough, fast and accessible, rather than the absolute frontier model.

...but the entry-point race is broadening
Our AlphaWise survey strengthens the multi-entry-point thesis. Past-month usage penetration reached ~94% for AI-native apps, ~83% for in-app AI, and ~80% for on-device AI, while agentic tools rose to ~44%. The average consumer is therefore not choosing a single AI gateway. The more important question is: which surface can convert intent into a completed task with acceptable customer-acquisition and inference costs?
...and we believe in-app AI has better ROI
Our AlphaWise survey asked whether AI inside a vertical app changes commercial behavior – 75% of respondents find AI search/chatbot features in shopping apps very or somewhat useful, and 57% say such features would make them purchase much or a little more often. That said, AI creates more economic value when it improves an existing high frequency workflow, than when it creates a standalone funnel that must be acquired and monetized from scratch.

Recent company developments validate the in-app thesis more than the standaloneapp thesis.
• Tencent: Tencent disclosed a small-scale Xiaowei prototype inside WeChat, powered by a customized WeLM model for privacy, WeChat-specific use cases, and inference efficiency. The agent can use WeChat content, Mini Programs, merchant reach and payments; management explicitly described a future agent-to-agent transaction loop. We view this as a stronger strategic pathway than trying to make Yuanbao a standalone super-app.
Alibaba: Qwen Shopping Assistant launched inside Taobao in May and was described as providing end-to-end assistance from inspiration through after-sales. Management said losses narrowed sequentially as Qwen marketing expenses fell. This makes the ROI distinction between ecosystem AI and standalone-app spend unusually visible.
• Meituan: 2Q26 earnings call emphasized moving Xiaotuan from 'answering' to 'getting things done,' with order placement, ride-hailing and reservations, while LongCat 2.0 and merchant/operational agents provide model and workflow support. The direction is consistent with our thesis that vertical platforms should focus on real workflows rather than token volume as an end in itself.
3. Monetization – Freemium at the User Layer, Intent Monetization at the Commercial Layer
Doubao's introduced professional tier formalized a three-step paid structure at Rmb68/200/500 per month while maintaining a free base tier. Qwen introduced paid office-assistant tiers at Rmb19/49/128 per month in August. In both cases, the paid proposition centers on heavier productivity or generation workloads rather than basic chat. This is consistent with our original unit-economics framework: charge the users and tasks that consume more inference, while keeping broad consumer entry points free enough to preserve scale.

Why is AI diffusing faster than mobile internet?
China’s mobile-internet adoption required a long infrastructure and device cycle: mobile internet users numbered~50mn in 2007 and reached ~557mn by 2014. Consumer AI has scaled much faster, with AI users rising from ~249mn in Dec-2024 to ~602mn in Dec-2025.

China’s higher 2C AI penetration is driven more by distribution and use-case fit than by frontier-model capability. Although the US still leads in frontier models and compute, China has been faster in pushing AI into mass-market consumer scenarios through superapp ecosystems, low trial friction, low-cost use cases and stronger commercial closed loops.
• 1) China has stronger consumer distribution channels. AI adoption is not limited to standalone chatbot downloads. It is increasingly embedded into high-frequency apps such as WeChat, Douyin, Taobao, Alipay, Amap, Meituan, and Ctrip, etc. We believe the next phase of adoption is likely to happen inside existing apps, where users already search, consume content, shop, pay, book travel and order local services.
2) Free access lowers trial friction but is not unique to China. What is more distinctive is how Chinese platforms combine free usage with ecosystem traffic, CNY promotions and a wider range of daily-life functions. During the CNY period, Doubao, Yuanbao and Qwen together acquired around 130mn new users, while Qwen downloads reached 170mn in February 2026. This helped convert AI from a novelty product into a broader consumer habit (see more in China Internet: China Consumer AI – 2026 CNY Campaign Review (2 Mar 2026)).
• 3) Most current 2C AI use cases are not very token-intensive. Search, Q&A, short-form writing, shopping comparison and travel planning usually require only short prompts and relatively short outputs (see more for consumer AI use cases here: 2. Competitive Landscape and use cases ). These use cases are much cheaper than long-document analysis, deep research, coding agents or multi-step autonomous agents. As a result, platforms can theoretically support free or lowcost usage and monetize indirectly through ads, traffic routing or transaction conversion ( Exhibit 17 ).
• 4) China’s platforms have stronger commercial closed loops. Chinese super apps combine content, search, merchants, payment, fulfillment, customer service and advertising in one ecosystem. This allows AI to move directly from answering questions to recommending products, booking services, placing orders or handling after-sales. In the US, individual verticals, such as search, social and e-commerce,

Entry Points: AI-Native Apps Win Mindshare, In-App AI Wins ROI
China’s consumer AI race is no longer only about who builds the best chatbot. It is becoming a race for AI entry points: AI-native apps for mindshare, in-app AI for monetization, and OS-level AI for long-term orchestration. We prefer in-app AI assistants from an economics perspective because they can monetize existing traffic, proprietary data and closed-loop transactions without rebuilding user habits from scratch.
AI-native apps have been the fastest way to educate users and capture top-of-funnel intent, but multi-homing and low switching costs limit defensibility. While Doubao, Qwen, DeepSeek and Yuanbao have captured early mindshare, their longterm defensibility varies by product iteration, traffic access, ecosystem depth, user stickiness and monetization path.
Doubao clearly leads on user scale through ByteDance’s traffic engine and fast product iteration;
• Qwen has the strongest transaction-loop advantage through Alibaba’s ecosystem;
• Yuanbao remains behind as a standalone app, but WeChat Agent could become Tencent’s real AI entry point;
DeepSeek remains a model-first outlier, driven by technical capability rather than user acquisition.
In-app AI assistants have the clearest near-term ROI because they sit inside existing user habits and transaction flows, with proprietary data, merchant supply, payment, fulfillment and attribution.
OS-level AI assistants may become the long-term command layer, especially for local context and cross-app tasks, but execution still depends on app permissions, commercial agreements and cloud inference for complex workflows.

OS-level AI Assistants
OS-level AI should be treated as a hybrid architecture, not pure on-device AI. Light, private and latency-sensitive tasks can run locally, while complex reasoning, longcontext analysis, multimodal generation and agentic workflows will still rely on cloud inference. This creates both a strategic advantage and a monetization limitation.
The advantage is default access: OS assistants can use local context, notifications, screen, keyboard, voice and device data, subject to user permission. Over time, they may control the cross-app command layer.
The limitation is execution and monetization: OS agents need app permissions, commercial agreements and APIs to complete transactions, while the economics of orders, ads and commissions often remain inside apps.
We see OS-level AI as a strategic control point, with monetization from device premium, cloud-service bundles, traffic routing and ecosystem lock-in rather than immediate transaction take rate.
Advantages:
• Default entry point: Users can invoke AI from the phone, voice, screen, keyboard, notification layer
• Low user acquisition cost: Can be exposed to the full existing user base, but actual activation depends on entry-point design, user permission and habit formation
• Local context/data: OS can access the most private/authentic behavioral data on the phone (location trails, contacts, phone albums, health metrics, app usage behaviors etc.) based on user permissions, which can improve personalization
• Privacy/latency: Simple tasks can be processed locally with lower latency and lower marginal cloud cost
• Cross-app orchestration: OS can theoretically call multiple apps to complete tasks
• Monetization through hardware price premium
Disadvantages:
• On-device model constraints: Memory, battery, thermal and context-window constraints limit reasoning depth
• Cloud still needed: Complex agentic tasks require larger models and server inference
• App permission bottleneck: OS cannot complete transactions unless apps expose APIs / permissions
• Monetization less direct: OS controls routing, but transaction economics often remain inside apps
• Hardware cycle: Full experience may require newer devices, slowing penetration

What is the next phase of consumer AI?
We expect China’s consumer AI penetration to continue rising from broad trial to habitual usage. While total AI-assisted consumer touch points should be meaningfully higher once in-app assistants and OS-level agents are included.
The next phase of consumer AI is likely to shift from answer generation to task completion. We see three stages:
• First, AI answers questions and generates content;
• Second, AI helps users compare options and make decisions;
• Third, AI executes tasks across apps, including booking, ordering, paying, refunding, rescheduling and after-sales service.
This shift should favor platforms that own both user intent and execution rights.
• Existing in-app assistants are best positioned for closed-loop execution because they already control product catalogs, merchant relationships, payment, logistics, customer service and attribution.

Monetization: Freemium at the User Layer, Intent Monetization at the Commercial Layer
The core monetization challenge for consumer AI is that scale creates costs before it creates revenue. Traditional internet platforms benefit from strong operating leverage as user growth spreads fixed costs over a larger base, but AI products face a structurally different cost curve: every additional interaction consumes inference compute. This weakens the traditional internet playbook of subsidizing user growth first and monetizing later.
In China, subscription is unlikely to become a mass-market revenue engine and may function more as a cost-sharing mechanism for heavy users; advertising presents a larger opportunity given Chinese users’ higher tolerance for ads, but chatbot ad load is structurally limited by trust and user experience; and transaction commissions may offer the largest long-term upside if AI agents can move from answering questions to completing purchases.
Consumer AI is likely to accelerate existing mobile-internet monetization rather than create a separate revenue pool. We therefore start with existing consumerinternet revenue and GMV pools, and then layer in AI adoption, commercial-intent, closed-loop attribution/conversion and pricing/take-rate assumptions. On that basis, we estimate ~Rmb294bn (~Rmb300bn) of China 2C AI monetizable revenue TAM by 2030E, rising to ~Rmb1.6tn by 2040E as AI penetrates more consumer workflows.
The core challenge: Consumer AI products create costs before creating revenue
Traditional internet products are usually characterized by high fixed costs and low marginal costs. Once the product, content infrastructure, recommendation system and traffic engine are built, the cost of serving one additional user or one additional session is relatively low. Historically, this has made scale highly attractive for internet platforms: as MAU, DAU, time spent and ad inventory grow, fixed costs are diluted across a larger base and margins can expand.
Consumer AI products have a different cost curve, and do not benefit from the same degree of automatic operating leverage. Training and infrastructure investments still have fixed-cost characteristics, and inference efficiency can improve over time, but the core user experience is usage-based. Every additional AI query, every image generation, every PPT creation, every data-analysis task, and every agentic workflow consumes incremental compute. As usage scales, inference cost scales with it. Engagement is therefore both a monetization opportunity and a cost driver.

This is why AI monetization should be framed as a unit economics question. Sizing China 2C AI Monetizable TAM - Definitions and Methodology
We define Consumer AI monetization revenue TAM as the platform-level revenue that can be captured from AI-assisted consumer activity across AI-native apps, in-app AI assistants, AI search, OS-level assistants and agents embedded in e-commerce, local services, travel, payment and super-app ecosystems.
We separate the opportunity into three revenue pools to avoid double counting:
• Subscription / pay-per-use: user-paid revenue from advanced models, higher quotas, productivity tools, faster response, image/video generation, PPT generation or data analysis.
• Advertising: merchant-paid sponsored exposure, paid ranking, lead generation, CPC/CPM/CPA (i.e., cost per click, thousand, or acquisition) placements or sponsored recommendations triggered by AI search, chatbot, assistant or agent surfaces.
• Transaction commission: take rate, CPS (cost per sale), affiliate fee or referral commission from completed AI-attributed transactions such as shopping orders, hotel bookings, travel reservations or local services.
The addressable user base also differs by monetization model. Subscription revenue is anchored to a narrower pool of users who could plausibly pay directly for AI, whereas advertising can reach a broader audience across AI-native, in-app, and AI search interfaces. We therefore use a narrower subscription-addressable AI MAU and a broader advertising addressable user base. Users do not need to pay for AI to generate monetizable activity through sponsored recommendations, search ads, lead generation, or other merchant funded intent.
Although advertising and commission revenue will likely become increasingly intertwined at the product level, we distinguish between the two based on the monetization trigger. We classify exposure-, ranking-, click-, or lead-driven revenue as advertising, while revenue generated only after a completed order, booking, or payment falls under transaction commissions. Together, advertising and commissions form AI intent monetization, which contributes ~99% of our 2030E base-case monetizable revenue.

Model 1: Subscription – Selective monetization for heavy users
US players popularized freemium: basic access is free and power users pay for advanced models, higher limits, faster responses or specialized generation. China follows a similar free-base / paid-premium structure, but distribution through large ecosystems, consumer campaigns and daily-life use cases has made free access more effective as a mass-adoption tool.
Subscription is the most straightforward monetization model for AI products, but it is unlikely to become the dominant mass-market revenue engine in China in the near term.
• US AI players have largely adopted a freemium model: basic features are free, while users pay for more advanced models, larger context windows, deeper reasoning, faster response, image or video generation, and higher usage limits. This model works best when a meaningful share of users perceive AI as a productivity tool and are willing to pay for higher-quality output.
• China is different. Consumer AI apps have been free from the outset, and users have been conditioned to expect free access to basic chatbot functions.
The strongest payment levers remain higher accuracy, faster response and advanced features - all of which are associated with higher inference costs. We therefore continue to model subscription/pay-per-use as selective monetization for heavy users and prosumers rather than the mass-market consumer revenue engine.
Our model therefore treats subscription and pay-per-use primarily as a compute-cost sharing mechanism. The formula is:
AI MAU x paying user ratio x monthly ARPPU x 12
By 2040E, we assume 1,073mn AI MAU, a 3.2% paying user ratio and Rmb81 monthly ARPPU, implying Rmb33bn subscription/pay-per-use SAM. This represents only 2.1% of total 2C AI SAM.

Model 2: Advertising – Supported by high ad tolerance
AI’s impact on advertising is more about “reallocation + efficiency gains The total size of the traditional internet advertising market is fundamentally constrained by corporate marketing budgets, total consumer GMV, advertising ROI, macro consumption trends, and merchants’ profit margins. The emergence of AI, at least in the short term, is unlikely to cause companies to materially increase their overall advertising budgets. Rather, it means that over time, a meaningful share of existing advertising revenue may shift from “traditional keyword or feed-based ads” to “AI/intent-driven ads.”
Advertising appears more naturally suited to China’s consumer internet market, given users’ relatively higher tolerance for ads and the maturity of China’s advertising ecosystem. ~64% of our survey respondents either do not mind ads in a free AI service or are willing to tolerate a certain level of advertising if an ad-free option exists; only ~17% indicated they would avoid AI apps that display ads. Ad tolerance has softened slightly in the past three months, but remains high in absolute terms.

AI can turn previously hard-to-monetize user intent into commercial queries. Traditional search and feed ads monetize explicit keywords or inferred interest. AI conversations can surface longer, more complex and more context-rich intent. A user asking an AI assistant to plan a family trip, compare skincare products, choose a laptop, or identify the best hotel near a theme park is revealing high-quality purchase intent. This represents not only additional advertising inventory, but also higher-quality intent capture.
• AI can shorten the path from intent to transaction. As AI moves from answering questions to recommending products, comparing options and completing purchases, monetization shifts from CPM and CPC toward CPA, CPS, affiliate revenue and transaction commissions. This is where the distinction between advertising and commissions begins to blur.
The more attractive opportunity lies in intent-based advertising. Under this model, the AI platform does not need to maximize ad impressions. It needs to identify high-value commercial intent and match users with relevant merchants, products or services. A single AI-assisted travel-planning query, for example, may be more valuable than multiple lowintent feed impressions if it leads to hotel a booking, flight purchase or local-service transaction.
We therefore model AI advertising using commercial intent rather than raw time spent:
ad-exposed DAU x AI interactions/day x commercial query mix x monetized commercial query rate x ad units per monetized query x eCPM x 365
By 2040E, our base case assumes 320mn ad-exposed DAU, 13 queries per day, 36.5% commercial query mix, 63% monetized commercial query rate, 4.8 ad units per monetized query and Rmb190 eCPM. This yields Rmb318bn AI advertising SAM, or 7.2% of China online advertising TAM.
This is deliberately more conservative than assuming AI becomes a another feed. The value of AI advertising comes from higher-quality intent signals rather than very high ad load. A travel-planning, product-comparison or food-delivery query may be worth significantly more than a passive feed impression, but the number of sponsored units per answer has a natural ceiling.
For advertising, AI is more likely to improve the monetization of existing traffic than create a new inventory pool. Better intent capture and conversion can support higher pricing and faster share gains.

Model 3: Transaction commissions – The most attractive long-term monetization path
Transaction commissions may offer the most attractive long-term monetization path for consumer AI, because they connect revenue directly to user intent and completed transactions. Unlike subscriptions, commissions do not require users to pay directly for AI. Unlike display advertising, commissions do not depend on high ad load. Instead, the platform monetizes when AI helps users make or complete a purchase, and this is particularly valuable in categories with high information friction, frequent comparison, or fragmented supply.
Our AlphaWise survey already shows evidence of this behavior. 50% of respondents always or often use AI recommendations for purchases, including 9% who always use them and 41% who often use them. Usage is higher among prime-age and higher-income consumers: 53% of consumers aged 30-39 and 61% of consumers earning above Rmb20k/ month always or often use AI recommendations for purchases.
Sizing AI transaction commission SAM: The key distinction is between AI-assisted GMV and AI-attributed closed-loop GMV. Many users may consult AI before purchasing, but not every purchase can be monetized by the AI platform. Commission revenue should apply only when the AI flow can be attributed to a completed order, booking or payment. Our formula is addressable online GMV x AI-assisted GMV share x closed-loop attribution/ capture rate x effective AI take rate.
By 2040E, our base case includes ~Rmb1.26tn of AI-addressable commission SAM, led by e-commerce, local services and OTA/travel. Much of this represents existing platform economics shifting into AI-assisted journeys; the incremental upside comes from higher conversion, transaction frequency and take rates than under a no-AI baseline.
China’s leading AI apps are already moving in this direction.
• Doubao embeds Douyin Mall product cards in replies and can potentially earn commissions from transactions completed through Douyin Mall.
• Qwen is integrated with Alibaba’s ecosystem, including Taobao, Taobao Instashopping, Alipay, Fliggy, Freshippo, Amap and Damai, giving it a stronger path from question to action.
Yuanbao’s standalone app remains less differentiated, but a future WeChat AI agent could connect AI intent with Tencent’s social graph, mini-programs, payments and merchant ecosystem (currently Trip.com, Tongcheng, Meituan, JD.com, KFC, etc. have announced partnerships with WeChat AI agent).
However, transaction monetization is harder than advertising because it requires more than intent recognition. It requires product catalogs, real-time pricing, inventory, merchant relationships, payment, fulfillment, customer service, attribution and after-sales support. Standalone chatbots usually lack these capabilities. They can recommend products, but they cannot easily guarantee price accuracy, complete checkout, resolve disputes or manage the transaction lifecycle.
This is why the reverse path may be more practical: Instead of standalone AI apps trying to become e-commerce platforms – e-commerce, OTA, local service, payment and superapp platforms can embed AI deeply into their native transaction flows. In this model, AI

Tencent — WeChat’s moat should be enhanced, with AI adding monetization upside
Tencent is one of the clearest positive read-throughs in our consumer-AI framework. We do not expect Doubao or other AI-native apps to displace WeChat’s social operating layer: the social graph, messaging, Official Accounts, Video Accounts, Mini Programs, payments and merchant services create high switching costs and a proprietary execution environment. The more important opportunity is Xiaowei inside WeChat, which can connect user intent to WeChat content, native functions and Mini Program services without requiring users to build a new standalone habit.
AI can also translate into revenue upside through the existing WeChat ecosystem. As LLM-powered Weixin Search expands query coverage and identifies more commercial intent, Tencent can monetize a larger pool of explicit-intent traffic through sponsored search, product recommendations and qualified leads. Longer term, if Xiaowei converts recommendations into attributable Mini Programs, Mini Shops or Weixin Pay transactions, Tencent could participate in referral or commission-sharing, payment and merchantservice economics. The caveat is ROI discipline. Tencent's new AI products and compute procurement are significant investments, and the company separately disclosed lower operating profit when including New AI Products. Encouragingly, management expects Xiaowei's cost envelope to remain below the investment in Yuanbao over the past year and described Xiaowei costs as manageable, and expects returns to outweigh investment as the product experience improves. Cohort gaps are narrowing. Weekly personal usage among ages 40–49 and 50–59 rose to a respective 81% and 72% (from 65% and 56%); work usage for the same age groups rose to 80% and 74%. Higher-income users remain the most engaged.
Multi-homing deepens as AI becomes a routine layer. 64% used five or more AI tools in the past month versus 32% in Wave 37.
