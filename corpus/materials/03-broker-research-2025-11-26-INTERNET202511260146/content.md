Reports indicate GOOGL could start selling TPUs into external 
data centers. In a world where NVDA is selling ~8mn annual 
chips, could TPUS carve out 500k-1mn sales per year? Maybe. 
Every 500k would add ~10% to '27 GOOGL Cloud rev, 3% to '27 
EPS and drive multiple. We also detail key takes for semis.
What Happened: Reports indicate GOOGL is talking with META and other cloud 
customers about letting them run GOOGL's TPUs in their data centers. META 
specifically is reportedly in talks with Google about spending billions of dollars to 
use TPUs in its data centers in 2027, as well as rent TPUs from Google Cloud next 
year. Notably, META has talked to GOOGL about using these TPUs for training 
rather than to power META's existing inference workloads. This comes as GOOGL 
has developed software, known as TPU command center, designed to make using 
TPUs easier for customers to use. Customers can use Pytorch to interact with the 
TPU command center software that controls the TPU server clusters. This news also 
follows a recent agreement between Anthropic and Google Cloud, in which 
Anthropic agreed to access ~1mn TPUs from Google.
Every 500k 1P TPU Sales Would Represent ~3% Upside to ‘27 EPS:
 Anthropic's 
recent agreement with GOOGL to access ~1mn TPUs, and now reports that META is 
in talks to access the chip signals further progress as GOOGL looks to leverage its 
~12 years of TPU innovation to deploy and sell them externally. We estimate 
Broadcom produced ~1.8mn TPUs for GOOGL in ‘25, the majority of which were 
used internally. That said, our Semis team expects production to scale, reaching 
~3mn in ‘27. Further external traction (per reports) could drive further upside to ‘27 
TPU purchases which will be important to monitor. The extent to which GOOGL 
sells TPUs through a 1P model can indeed move the needle, as our sensitivity 
work shows that every ~500k chips they sell externally could represent $13bn 
(~11%) upside to our above Street '27 Google Cloud Revenue estimates
 and ~3% 
($.37) upside to '27 GOOGL EPS. Faster GOOGL cloud growth and expansion into 
this market would also likely lead to (or support) a high multiple on GOOGL (as we 
have observed in past couple months).  In a world where Joe Moore and our NVDA 
team expect NVDA to ship ~8mn GPUs in CY27, 500k-1mn chips may not be 
unreasonable considering the extent to which 1) production capacity is available and 
2) GOOGL’s TPUS continue to overcome the historical lack of developer familiarity/
coding flexibility.

3 Key Unknowns To Monitor About GOOGL’s External TPU Strategy:
1. TPU Business Model: GOOGL has indicated a willingness to meet 
customers where they are and find flexible arrangements to provide access 
to their TPUs (GCP rental, licensing, chip sale, lease, etc.). This has 
implications for how these deals are ultimately recognized as revenue and as 
a result the potential margin and capex GOOGL recognizes as well. While 
our calculations above imply GOOGL is selling these chips directly to META 
to be used in META's data centers, any exact pricing models, contract 
arrangements or incremental services attached to the TPUs remain 
unknown. 
2. TPU Pricing: Chip gross margins fall in a wide range, with NVDA generating 
~75% gross margins and AMD closer to ~50%. This ultimately has 
implications for GOOGL's TPU pricing. In our sensitivity we assume GOOGL 
TPU pricing implies chip gross margins at the lower end of this peer gross 
margin range as the company looks to prove out its offering and gain initial 
traction with customers.  Additionally, we base our pricing and in turn 
revenue to GOOGL on a per chip calculation, however if GOOGL is selling 
rack scale products to META, pricing could be different.  
3. TPU Workloads: While it is notable that META is contemplating using TPUs 
for training, training runs can be broken down into multiple parts, and it is 
still unclear the extent to which META will rely on TPUs to support its 
Llama model training. Should TPUs become an integral part of the Llama

What Does This Mean for META? Potential Way for More Cost Efficient 
Compute: We have seen META has looked to manage its capex/opex growth as it 
invests to build a state of the art LLM and improve its core offerings. We view TPUs 
strong performance per dollar as a key catalyst in META's potential decision to run 
workloads on these chips. The decision to run workloads through TPUs may help 
serve as a potential capex offset in '27 given the strong cost performance ratio of 
these chips. How many chips META purchases (team Internet currently expects 
META to buy over 1mn NVDA chips in '26) and how much more efficient they are 
will play a role in materiality.
 Implications for Semis: The expanding use of TPU at Google is a boost for AVGO, 
but when it comes to broader adoption by other customers, the implications are less 
clear. As of today, AVGO has disclosed five XPU customers; we think those 
customers are Google, Meta, ByteDance, Anthropic, and OpenAI. With Meta 
potentially using TPUs, that makes three—and potentially four—out of the five 
customers (OpenAI being the potential addition) using that product. So at this point, 
it looks like TPU is becoming another merchant competitor to Nvidia in the XPU 
market, but for AVGO it’s not necessarily an endorsement of the ASIC strategy.
As Meta looks to potentially utilize TPU in a more significant way than we had 
thought previously, it may come at the expense of the MTIA ramp expected in the 
second half of 2026, reducing the potential EPS upside. That could be viewed as a 
positive development for Nvidia, assuming the challenges of ASIC development 
against a one-year cadence from Nvidia are pushing customers toward a more 
mature ecosystem in TPU—especially as product development is as much about 
chips as about delivering full rack-scale connectivity, something Nvidia has led in 
commercializing.
In the near term, Meta using TPUs doesn’t materially affect our viewpoint on Nvidia 
despite the narrative headwind it creates, as we are seeing upward revisions 
everywhere in AI. AVGO has the edge on growth next year (with the caveat that 
some of that is lower-margin rack business), but that comes at a much higher 
multiple. Right now, we are bullish on both stocks, because while we are seeing a 
clear acceleration in the TPU supply chain, we are also seeing Nvidia’s supply chain 
accelerate.
As far as Google’s success on the LLM side, we think Nvidia has gained share at 
Google this year. With Google spending about $20bn or so on Nvidia vs. spending in 
the teens on TPU. That will shift back next year to some degree, but we don’t expect 
growth to be winner-take-all. For now, we don’t think having Gemini overtake GPT-5 
as the leading model means competitors will slow down, as we’ve heard time and 
time again from leaders in the tech industry how important this race is, and we 
expect it to remain competitive - especially as scaling laws seem to be intact. That 
said, if the competition’s market share is impacted, so will their ability to raise 
money to continue investing.

Where does this leave AMD? Largely in the same place, in our view. In the short 
run, one of AMD’s more vocal customers pursuing another alternative isn’t going to 
inspire confidence in the coming MI450 ramp. But we don’t think Meta’s aspirations

TPU looking more like a merchant alternative, that advantage is somewhat reduced. 
We are excited to see what they can deliver with MI450, but without those ROI 
proof points, it remains difficult to get excited.
