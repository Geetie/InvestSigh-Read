2. Muse's Commercial Potential in Practice
Subscribed
Meta has long excelled at discovery-driven social advertising,where the path from discovery to purchase is often short.
It has lagged Google in categories that depend on explicit purchase intent,notably travel, financial services,automotive, B2B and local services.
Meta is already highly effective in shopping. The social ad format, combined with advertisers' growing use of CRM data for retargeting, can make Meta even more efficient at monetization than Google.
The categories where Meta is weaker depend heavily on users telling the platform exactly what they want.
1. In travel, for example, knowing a user's destination and dates makes it much easier to serve relevant hotel, flight and restaurant ads.
2. In automotive, the platform needs to know when someone has started comparing models and considering a purchase.A consumer weighing a Model X against a Model S is likely to use Google or ChatGPT. Meta has rarely been part of that research process.

3. Similarly, consumers looking for a credit card turn to Google to research rewards, benefits and fit with their spending habits. Few would conduct that search on Meta. Yet credit cards are among the most direct and readily monetizable categories in financialservices advertising.
4.The same applies across lead-generation categories,including education and training,enterprise software and construction equipment.
Muse could change that. It can work with users' messages and calendars,and a user might ask it to plan a Thanksgiving trip.In the process,Meta could gain visibility into that user's purchase intent.
We have already found numerous examples among the 654 cases. They suggest that Muse could bring Meta into the purchase process earlier, when consumers first express a need,and influence their choice of brand,merchant or service provider. That could create more opportunities for intent-driven advertising and allow Meta to compete for budgets that have historically gone to search. Even in retail, where Meta is already strong, screening products against specific requirements and comparing prices across merchants could create incremental value.
Travel: The Monetization Opportunity
Hotel bookings: A user planning a two-night stay in New York wanted to compare IHG, Hyatt and Marriott properties, considering standard cash rates,corporate rates and points redemptions. After giving Muse the requirements, the user asked it to keep checking daily and send an alert if rates fell or another suitable nearby hotel became available. Muse also estimated the value of points redemptions to help the user decide whether to pay cash or use points.
Trip planning: Another user planning a European trip had not settled on arrival and departure cities.Muse compared flights from San Francisco to 11 Spanish cities and return flights from 10 Italian cities, searching multiple dates for both cash fares and award availability. According to the user, it ran 146 searches and presented several payment options: approximately \$890 in cash, 40,000 points plus \$455,or 15,000 points plus \$577. The user could then weigh the cash budget against available points and compare arrival cities,return cities and flight combinations without repeating searches for every city and date.

Both cases illustrate the opportunity. Once Muse has helped plan a trip, it knows the itinerary and the user's flight and hotel preferences. Those details could support relevant advertising later in the process.
Travel has historically been a weak category for Meta relative to Google.

3.Driving GMV Growth Through Intent-Driven Ads
We conducted a particularly useful agency expert interview this week and strongly recommend that clients read the full transcript. We summarize the key points below.
Meta already understands shopping intent well. The harder problem is timing.
1. People go to Google or Amazon because they want something now; they often open Facebook or Instagram because they are bored.
2. Nearly all advertisers retarget using website or CRM data.If a user visits a Nike running-shoe page, Meta can infer that the user is shopping for running shoes and allow other brands to serve relevant ads.
3.Meta may identify the intent only seconds after Google,but its cost per thousand impressions (CPM) is lower because users did not come to the platform to shop.
4.Muse could materially expand that capability. Users may connect email,calendars,WhatsApp and other everyday apps to help manage their lives.
5.Meta could then identify events such as prenatal appointments,a move or half-marathon training and proactively help with nursery furnishings,a moving truck or running shoes.
6. Muse is designed to take the initiative rather than wait for someone to click an ad.

Many of these signals already sit in inboxes and calendars. School emails can point to needs for supplies or clothing; family gatherings create restaurant-booking opportunities; gym appointments may suggest equipment purchases. Users who develop greater trust may eventually grant access to bank statements or their browser as well.

a.Meta works well for lower-consideration decisions such as finding a tutor or weekend activities for children.
b. Higher-priced education products typically require longer searches and more detailed questions and answers,making Google the primary advertising channel.
If Muse gains broader adoption and gives Meta access to these intent signals,we see a credible path to more than 20% incremental GMV. These are precisely the advertising categories in which Meta has historically been weaker than Google.
4. Increasing Meta's Take Rate

The more a platform contributes to a sale, the more value it can capture. Our agency expert put it this way: “lfα platform can take a consumer from a vague interest all the way to a purchase, it may capture 10% or more of GMv.If it is merely one of many ads, with weak attribution, it may capture only 3%."
Commissions and booking fees are two possible mechanisms.
1. For example, Meta could charge a 3-5% commission on gross merchandise value (GMV) generated through Muse,or charge service fees on bookings and ticket purchases completed through the agent.
2. Commissions and advertising ultimately draw on the same merchant marketing budget, however. Higher commissions could displace some ad spend. This matters particularly in shopping, where Meta is already stronger than Google and has greater exposure in its ad revenue mix.
Still,if Muse makes a greater contribution to completed sales,it should be able to capture incremental value.
The case is clearer in lead-generation categories such as automotive.
By moving Meta further up the purchase funnel, Muse could create revenue from stages of the transaction that Meta does not currently monetize.
1. Muse also gives Meta a way to participate in more transactions without first bringing merchants, product catalogs and fulfillment onto its own platform.
2. It can access merchant websites through connectors or a browser, leave payments to partners and fulfillment to merchants, and focus on helping users choose products and complete purchases.

3.That lowers the barrer to expanding Meta's role in commerce. Completing a purchase for a user does not, however, automatically entitle Meta to a commission. The economics will depend on the revenue-sharing terms agreed with merchants and payment partners.
5. Expanding WhatsApp Monetization
We have tracked the monetization of WhatsApp Updates in successive earnings previews.During the first quarter after launch,we heard considerable positive feedback and aggressive revenue forecasts. Almost every quarter since then has fallen short of those expectations.
WhatsApp Business has made a clearer contribution, improving conversion for lead-generation ads and higher-ticket products.
Muse could contribute even more directly to WhatsApp revenue.
1.Meta already has an established model for charging businesses per message through WhatsApp Business.
a. Muse fits naturally as a WhatsApp contact that users can message directly.
b. Consider tutoring.A user could ask Muse to find a tutor,review three profiles, then tap a button to start a WhatsApp conversation with the selected provider. The advertiser could pay a few cents per message.
c. Muse would connect the entire commercial process: identifying the need,screening providers,choosing one,opening a conversation and generating messaging revenue for Meta.

2. A pay-per-click model is another possibility. Muse could present three options, with the selected business paying an illustrative \$1 when the user clicks.Muse itself might be free or cost, for example, \$20 a month without ads,while many of the actions it facilitates lead into channels that businesses pay to use.
3. Historically, in the WhatsApp Business advertising funnel, a user saw an ad before starting a conversation. As users begin asking Muse for help directly, WhatsApp Business could gain a new source of demand that does not require an initial ad impression on Facebook or Instagram.
This is also Muse's biggest advantage over other personal Al agents.
1. A native in-app account can improve both speed and accuracy.
2. Voice messages within WhatsApp are also more convenient than phone calls or a separate app.
3. Native WhatsApp integration could extend to group chats. Friends or family planning a Thanksgiving trip could speak with Muse together in a shared chat and work toward a final itinerary.
6. Connecting Muse with Business Agents
Our META 2Q26 Preview, a Premium Report for institutional clients, examined Business Agents' progress and product strategy in detail. We recommend reading it for background.
Muse could bring Business Agents two sources of demand:
1. Users arriving from Facebook and Instagram ads.
2. Users delegating purchase tasks directly to Muse.
3.The first improves conversion on existing advertising; the second could generate new sales leads for merchants.

Our agency expert described the opportunity: “Muse provides an agent for the consumer, while Meta is building chatbots for advertisers through Business Agents. That could enable A2A [agent-to-agent] transactions, including conversations behind the scenes to negotiate prices or ask follow-up questions. Meta is building bots for both sides of the transaction, unlike services that require merchants to build their own."
Meta could therefore participate on both sides of the interaction.
1. Muse knows what the consumer wants,the budget and the timing.
2.The Business Agent answers for the merchant, clarifies terms and helps qualify prospective customers.
3. Connecting the two would spare consumers from repeating the same requirements to multiple businesses and reduce the staff time merchants spend handling initial inquiries.
4. The home-improvement case above offers one example. After receiving a project brief, Muse could find suitable contractors and ask their Business Agents about service scope,availability and the information needed for a quote. The agents could handle the initial exchange before the user decides whether to arrange a site visit or request a detailed estimate.A contractor would receive a lead with a defined need,much closer to an actionable job than a single ad click.This is our extrapolation from the expert's comments; it still depends on product capabilities and merchant adoption.

The exchange also depends on merchants' willingness to connect.
1. For a contractor, restaurant or training provider, the incentive is straightforward: Muse brings in customers.
2. The trade-off is more complicated for travel and restaurantbooking aggregators. If users delegate search,comparison and selection to Muse,aggregators could lose some direct customer interaction, making the terms of cooperation harder to negotiate.
3.Meta's advertiser relationships should help,but they do not guarantee that every merchant will open its APls. Reliable access to prices, inventory and booking availability will directly affect Muse's ability to complete transactions. The website blocks and merchant refusals in our case database show that the constraints extend beyond model capability.
The interaction itself could generate messaging revenue. As the expert explained,"Consumers can ask questions through Muse,while businesses respond through Meta Business Agents and pay per message." The expert added that "a three-round conversation could generate incremental

messaging revenue while qualifying α potential buyer." The number of billable messages would depend on the product and applicable pricing rules.The underlying business model is clear: Muse brings the demand, the Business Agent handles the merchant's response,and WhatsApp provides the communication channel.
The larger upside for Meta may be an increase in total advertiser spending. The expert described a progression: "When smallbusinesses adopt Meta Business Agents, the ROl on their core Facebook and Instagram campaigns typically improves, and they increase spending. They are then encouraged to turn on Al-powered messaging. Initially， some existing budget shifts into messaging. When messaging delivers a good ROl, the business increases spending again."
This distinction matters when assessing Business Agent revenue. Early messaging growth may simply reflect a reallocation of existing budgets. Merchants have a stronger reason to increase total spending only if they can close more sales for the same acquisition cost or reach customers through Muse that they could not reach before.The combination ultimately needs to deliver better-qualified leads, higher conversion rates and greater total merchant spending.
The expert also suggested that usage-based pricing or paid features could eventually create merchant subscription revenue. Specific features and pricing were still unclear at the time of the interview. Rather than assume a new subscription stream,we would focus on whether Muse consistently generates commercial demand and whether Business Agents turn that demand into outcomes merchants will pay for.
7. The Role of Subscription Revenue
Many Muse reports focus on subscription revenue.We regard it as the least important opportunity for Meta and the revenue stream it should be least inclined to pursue.

Relative to personal agents such as Instinct and ChatGPT, Meta's greatest advantage is its far larger pool of advertiser spending and ad inventory.
1. A single-digit percentage increase in click-through rate (CTR),a handful of high-value purchases handled from start to finish, or a few dozen additional commercial conversations on WhatsApp Business could generate the equivalent of a monthly subscription fee.
2. For the past decade, much of the attention has gone to new enterprise software products and,more recently, enterprise Al agents.Meta now has an opportunity to win with a free consumer internet product,monetized through its extensive commercial relationships and user activity.
3. We see this as the second major opportunity in a decade for a free internet product to create substantial commercial value. TikTok and the rise of short-form video were the previous one.
4. In our view, Meta should make Muse free and compete through stronger advertising monetization rather than charge a subscription.
How much would subscription revenue really add by comparison?
8. The Path to Monetization
Meta currently states that Muse conversations are not shared with its advertising systems.
Meta also notes: "When Muse browses the internet, it will appear as your activity." Its example is straightforward: if Muse visits a clothing brand's website to buy a shirt for you, that brand may use the visit to target an Instagram ad. Restaurant bookings and Facebook Marketplace searches made through Muse may also indirectly affect the ads you see.

Reels offers a historical reference point. It carried no ads for roughly six to nine months, then introduced brand ads at a low ad load,followed by performance ads and a further increase in brand ad load. Moving from no monetization to an ad load of roughly 25% took around four to five years.
Gemini-powered conversational ads and Direct Offers also suggest a different monetization model. As a conversation progresses, Gemini can infer the user's interests and increase ad load when purchase intent strengthens.More ads at that point could improve satisfaction rather than detract from the experience.For our definition of conversational ads and their monetization potential, see our GOOG 1Q26 Preview and GOOG 2Q26 Preview, both Premium Reports for institutional clients.
Given the way consumer products grow,we believe Meta should prioritize Muse adoption for an extended period before pushing monetization.
Intent signals could reach the advertising system through merchants' existing Meta Pixel or Conversions APl integrations. Any data sent back should be information the merchant actually obtained and has the right to use for that purpose.
Access to more data will depend on trust. The expert expects users to start with inexpensive purchases and,after several successful experiences, grant more permissions and delegate higher-value transactions.
9. Technical Differentiation and Broader Implications
We do not see a clear technical gap among personal Al agents.
1. Commitment and budget matter more.

2. Many use cases can be addressed with enough investment and staffing,much as they were in earlier generations of internet products. Progress does not depend entirely on the underlying model,as Manus's initial success illustrated. Management focus, speed of execution and an understanding of users may do more to improve a personal agent's capabilities.
Muse could also affect other industries.
1. Meta's decision to host users' agents also has implications for CPUs,NAND flash and HDDs. Based on FUNDA's estimates,one CPU could support roughly 200 daily active users (DAUs).
2. Muse also points to a shift in internet traffic from human activity toward agent activity.
a.Personal agents could drive a sustained increase in web and APl requests. More of these requests originate from machine identities and involve automated access, APl calls, authentication and transaction execution. Websites and businesses will need better ways to identify agents, verify their permissions,manage bot activity and secure APls,alongside real-time processing and security checks at the edge.
b. The long-term monetization opportunity for Cloudflare, Akamai and Fastly could therefore shift beyond traditional CDN bandwidth toward agent authentication, bot management, APl security and edge computing.
c. At this stage, we see Cloudflare (NET) as best positioned, given its broader, more integrated product portfolio across these layers.Akamai (AKAM) and Fastly (FSLY) are also expanding into this layer of agent infrastructure.

3. Meta Business Agents: Adoption and Revenue
a. By my estimate,WhatsApp Business currently accounts for a low-single-digit share of Meta's total revenue. In our business, it is roughly 7%,or about three times the typical level. Our experience may point to where the broader market is heading over the next few quarters.
b. WhatsApp Business spending mainly comes from midsize and large companies using their own call centers or Al,along with some small businesses that handle messages themselves. The typical charge is 2-3 cents per message. Meta Business Agents should become the largest growth driver, but their role extends beyond getting small businesses to spend on WhatsApp.
c. When small businesses adopt Meta Business Agents, the ROl on their core Facebook and Instagram campaigns typically improves,and they increase spending.They are then encouraged to turn on Al-powered messaging. Initially, some existing budget shifts into messaging. When messaging delivers a good ROl, the business increases spending again. Adoption happens in several stages.

4. Could Higher Oil Prices Weigh on Ad Spending?
a. It is possible, but my instinct is that they probably will not unless the increase is both large and sustained. Higher oil prices could raise household spending on fuel, heating, cooling and food,leaving less for other categories and reducing advertising demand.
b. Greater volatility, however, can also push companies to accelerate Al projects,including call-center automation, replacing sales staff with chatbots,working more with influencers and adopting WhatsApp Business.

c. Many CMOs seem ready to pause spending at any moment, but CEOs and CFOs are pushing them to accelerate the strategic shift toward Al, even if that means spending more on marketing. In that sense, a weaker economy could still benefit digital advertising.
