Consumer agents are the newest AI story, and this week’s market reaction shows investors are taking it seriously. Meta’s Muse has drawn an enthusiastic response, with CPU and cybersecurity names also rallying sharply as investors look across the companies that could benefit. We have spent much of our recent research explaining why Muse could matter: it brings AI into everyday tasks such as shopping, travel, phone negotiations and personal administration, with a more direct connection to what users actually want to get done.
For Meta, that could change the return-on-investment discussion. Our review of 654 Muse use cases shows how an agent can learn about purchase intent earlier, including in categories where Google has historically been stronger. Our agency work explores how that could translate into advertising revenue, while WhatsApp and Business Agents offer further routes to transactions. There are implications across the infrastructure and software stack too: persistent agents need CPUs to run browsers and orchestrate APIs, and wider deployment creates more work around identity, permissions and security.
The next thing to watch is the arrival of similar products from OpenAI and other providers. We will be looking at what users adopt, how reliably the agents complete tasks, and whether early enthusiasm turns into sustained use and monetization. Falling inference costs should make these services more economical to scale; our work on tiered pricing and Jev explains why we remain constructive on adoption. The rally has raised expectations, so the next product launches and evidence of real usage will matter for both the platforms and the related stocks.

Research | ABF Substrates: Beneficiaries of Expanding CPU Demand
Subscribed
Agent-driven CPU demand and larger substrate areas on new platforms are tightening the ABF supply outlook, with major producers expected to be fully utilized in 26Q4 and lead times at 48–52 weeks. Our note expects only 10%–15% capacity growth in 2027 and identifies 27Q1 contract renewals as a potential pricing catalyst for suppliers including Ibiden, Unimicron, AT&S, Nan Ya PCB and Kinsus.
Research | AI Hardware: Singapore Roadshow Feedback and the Next Specification Upgrade
Investors we met in Singapore generally see AI hardware valuations as reflecting 2027 earnings and want the Vera Rubin Ultra, TPU v9 and Trainium T4 roadmaps to support upward revisions to 2028 estimates. Power and ABF rank ahead in their preferences, with high-end material pricing, liquid cooling and power architecture upgrades among the catalysts to watch.

Research | SMTC: Rising NPO Expectations Drive Shares to a New High
Chinese CSPs are requesting Semtech TIAs in 3.2T NPO test samples targeting 2027 production, while our note places NVIDIA’s 6.4T NPO testing before year-end, initial production in 2H27 and a larger ramp in 2028. A rising 1.6T mix is also expected to support gradual grossmargin improvement from the October quarter.
