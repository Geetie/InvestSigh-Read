Investment Thesis 
Investment summary
With NVIDIA GPU supply shrouded in uncertainty, top China AI players are 
striving to introduce indigenous AI chip suppliers to cope with booming token 
demand. Instead of single chip performance, we believe there are two major areas of 
concern, especially for China CSPs: 
•Is there enough, sustainable and secure supply to meet rising demand, given 
technology export controls from the US government?
•Can indigenous AI chips fit into a heterogeneous cluster, and work with the other 
US and local AI chips? 
We believe Iluvatar CoreX, one of the first indigenous GPGPU (general purpose 
GPU) suppliers, is offering products to resolve both concerns. Moreover, the prefilldecode (P/D) disaggregation trend is an incremental tailwind for Iluvatar CoreX to 
gain substantial market share at top Chinese CSPs, in our view. 
Less impact from foundry availability constraint 
With exponential AI token growth in China, and uncertainty of NVIDIA GPU 
supply, local AI chip supply (rather than single chip performance) is especially 
important to key AI and CSP players in China. We believe foundry supply is a key 
bottleneck for China’s indigenous AI chip suppliers. Tier-1 players with more than 
100k of unit shipments have strived to secure a certain level of foundry capacity, 
while tier-2 players are more challenged, in our view. 
However, we find Iluvatar CoreX better positioned among tier-2 peers as its product 
portfolio enables the company to leverage the capacity of both domestic and 
international foundry suppliers. We believe the specs of existing products (e.g. 
Tiangai150/Gen 2), have not yet triggered the threshold of US government 
technology control – meaning international foundries are eligible to offer service in 
volume. Meanwhile, the company has started to work with local foundries to produce 
next-generation AI chips with enhanced performance and higher ASP, and we expect 
volume production could start to ramp up from 2H26. 
We estimate Iluvatar CoreX will ship 100k, 175k and 264k chips in 2026, 2027 and 
2028, respectively, which compares to around 15k shipments in 1H25, according to 
the company’s IPO prospectus. We estimate >40% of Iluvatar CoreX’s shipments 
could come from locally produced chips by 2028E, delivering higher performance 
specs and favorable ASP.

Beneficiary of new inference paradigm 
The recent debuts of NVIDIA Rubin CPX and Huawei Ascend 950PR/DT suggest a 
clear trend in AI inference: prefill-decode disaggregation. Prefill generally consumes 
more computing power, while decode asks for high HBM capacity/bandwidth. With 
decode as the key bottleneck of inference, dedicated AI chips for prefill loading and 
decode loading, respectively, could greatly improve inference efficiency and 
optimize hardware investment. 
In this respect, the (optimized) design of Iluvatar CoreX’s Tiangai chips is well 
suited for dedicated prefill loadings; meanwhile, by working with GPUs equipped 
with higher HBM specs (such as NVIDIA H20), the whole inference system could be 
more efficient in cost/performance. We expect such solutions to be gradually 
deployed by CSPs, and this would bring an incremental tailwind for Iluvatar CoreX 
to grow its client base and sales in the coming years, in our view. We model a 129% 
sales CAGR for Tiangai chips over 2026-28E, thanks to rapidly growing shipments 
and an improving (and more favorable) blended price.

Design-wins at top CSPs to unlock upside potential
Almost all of the leading CSPs and AI companies in China adopt heterogeneous 
infrastructure for AI training and inference. This means various GPGPUs, offered by 
NVDIA, AMD or the company itself, need to work side-by-side with various domain 
specific architecture (DSA) designed by Huawei or Cambricon. These AI chips will 
be applied in the NVIDIA CUDA developing ecosystem, hence full (or high-level) 
CUDA compatibility is essential for CSP project design wins. 
We believe Iluvatar CoreX’s products are highly compatible with CUDA operators 
and APIs. Moreover, its GPGPU architecture is more than “general” thanks to the 
availability and agility of foundry service (another merit of its product portfolio), and 
Iluvatar CoreX offers customer-optimized GPGPU design, which can maximize the 
performance for specific clients – especially CSPs. 
The existing client base of Iluvatar CoreX is relatively segmented and applicationspecific (e.g. AI-enabled traffic control, patient diagnosis, finance, research 
institutions, etc.), while we believe breakthroughs at major CSPs would help the 
company to accelerate its top-line growth and achieve economies of scale. We expect 
the company to make a profit in 2027E and model a 228% earnings CAGR over 
2026-28E.

Supply is currently the key bottleneck for China’s AI chip 
supply chain
Even though tightening regulations have controlled the availability of advanced AI 
chips from NVIDIA or US vendors, accelerating qualification and adoption of 
domestic high-end compute chips is well underway. We expect domestic candidates 
with sufficient inventory and reliable capacity support to outperform with market 
share gains or order wins at customers in the short to medium term. This positions 
Iluvatar CoreX as our preferred pick since it benefits from double capacity support 
from both international and domestic foundry partners. We forecast a 102% sales 
CAGR during 2026-28E and ~3% domestic market share by 2028E (from below 1% 
currently). 
The significantly changing competitive landscape 
Following the DeepSeek shock in Jan 2025, the question garnering increasing 
attention among investors is how China will reinforce its AI competence in the face 
of hardware constraints. Following a crowded period of IPOs among Chinese AI chip 
companies, we believe we are now in a phase of commercialization or 
industrialization of domestic GPUs and AI ASICs, given the level of intensive 
investment expected in AI infrastructure.

Prefill-decode disaggregation a new paradigm of AI 
infrastructure
After entering the expansion stage of inference demand and proliferation of AI 
applications, we believe tailored hardware deployment (based on specific demand) 
will provide meaningful cost/performance efficiency and optimize investment return, 
especially given hardware constraints. We expect decoupling the workloads from 
prefill and decode phases of inference to become the industry standard. Domestic AI 
chips with desirable compute power could be adopted as prefill nodes, given the 
memory-bound decode phase is likely to be a key bottleneck, and considering the 
relatively inferior memory capacity/bandwidth of domestic chips vs. NVIDIA H20 
(as decode nodes). 
Separate inference phases require specialized hardware 
Hardware constraints on deploying an AI model – the basics 
Given the proliferation of AI applications is largely driven by inference demand, the 
first constraint on the deployment of large LLMs is memory capacity. Then the 
inference speed (or service level) is primarily decided by the GPU’s computing 
power and memory bandwidth. Simply put, there are two stages of AI inference: 
•The prefill phase of inference is compute-intensive, which processes the user’s 
input prompt and determines Time to First Token (TTFT). 
•The decode phase is memory-intensive, which generates the response tokens and 
determines the service level or Time per Output Token (TPOT). 
The fundamentally opposite hardware requirements are worth noting here, especially 
in a unified system (or running both on the same chip). When the system receives a 
long prompt, it requires high FLOPS and GPU utilization, which would occupy the 
GPU resources all at once and impact other uses currently receiving a response (at 
decode steps). On the other hand, the decode operation generates tokens one by one, 
while waiting for data to move from the memory (typically HBM) to the processor, 
so the GPU is mostly idle. With that said, high memory bandwidth could facilitate 
decode steps to improve the service level, but the GPU utilization remains low.
