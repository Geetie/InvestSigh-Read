The Morgan Stanley AI Guidebook: Navigating the Age of Inference
Collaborative work across 7 teams details our views on 8 key GenAI debates, why we expect investor dollars to flow toward enablers/software and where we are seeing AI adopter signal (tech, comm services, financials, healthcare). We also detail next AI debates to consider for '27 and beyond.
8 GenAI Debates as We Enter the Age of Inference: Today's collaborative guidebook across 7 Morgan Stanley teams details our views and frameworks on the myriad of GenAI debates swirling the economy including: 1) hyperscaler capex trajectory; 2) GW compute capacity coming online; 3) GenAI ROIC; 4) the $50- $60tn knowledge work and consumer TAMS to be digitized; 5) early GenAI adoption signal across the economy; 6) the importance of open-weights models; 7) AI credit financing; and 8) regulatory concerns and other bottlenecks to monitor. We hope this guidebook is helpful in detailing our expansive views and please do let us know if interested in any of our interactive models across the space.
Thematically, Where Do We See Investment Dollars Flowing Next? From an equity perspective, we continue to believe we are in the early days of a fund flow rotation following the typical multi-year tech cycle… as flows move marginally out of hardware/semis/memory and into the enabler and software layers. While we don’t necessarily expect the hardware layer stocks to categorically fall materially (as they are not expensive and this investment cycle is still going to be multi-year), we expect larger revisions and multiple expansion opportunity further up the stack as capex growth slows (which we expect in '28) and revenue/EBIT beats grow up the stack. This makes us constructive AMZN, META, GOOGL, MSFT and other GenAI enablers.
What About the Adopters? We also expect outperformance from early adopters across the economy. Our latest survey work shows that the technology, comm services, financials, healthcare and industrials sectors are furthest along in adopting and quantifying benefits from GenAI.
Also Remain Constructive Bottlenecks: Time to market on power remains a bottleneck, which also leaves us constructive onsite generation names like BE, INIO and SEI and powered shell names like WULF, HUT, CIFR, and RIOT. On the data center regulatory front, while we see state/local data center political scrutiny as largely manageable (essentially leading to higher costs of power for hyperscalers that are already in our out-year forecasts), we are highly focused on the build up to the '28 election and political risk to data center expansion from the executive branch if the Democratic Party wins.

8 Key GenAI Debates As We Enter the Age of Inference
1) Forward Hyperscaler Capex: We see data center capex growing 60% ($550bn) in ‘27 but see a deceleration in capex growth going forward, with ~12% growth in ’28 ($170bn). Expect this (and accelerating hyperscaler/software revenue) to lead to investor fund rotation.
2) Forward Compute Capacity Growth: We see total compute capacity growing ~4X from 35 GW in ‘25 to ~145 GW in ‘28. GOOGL will add the most capacity, and we see ASICs growing in the mix, rising from 34% of incremental capacity in ’25 to 66% in ‘28.
3) GenAI ROIC: We paint paths to 25-50% GenAI ROIC across hyperscalers and models with APIs. The highest returns accrue to the model layer running on top of owned infrastructure (META, GOOGL, SPCX).
4) The $50-$60tn Global Knowledge Work & Consumer TAMs: We see a $20-$30tn opportunity in the global knowledge work spend to be digitized through GenAI tools. We also see ~$30tn of consumer spend (across retail, travel, autonomous, advertising, etc) to be digitized. We also analyze hyperscaler and Facebook adoption curves as analogies to consider… speaking to the trilliondollar opportunities ahead.
5) GenAI Adoption Signal: We focus on diffusion across the economy. So far, we see most signal within the tech sector, followed by financials, healthcare and industrials.
6) Open-Weights Models: Leading cost to serve will matter through the age of inference. We think open-weights and lower-cost models are critical to driving GenAI adoption across the economy. These will pressure leading labs to innovate to capture share of inference spend, while also reenforcing the value of the hyperscaler concierge layer (AWS Bedrock, MSFT Foundry, GOOGL Vertex) that optimizes compute efficiency by task.
7) The AI Credit Financing Market: Despite ~$450bn of AI-related debt issuance globally year-to-date, we think there's still significant capacity to absorb additional issuance, especially from high-quality hyperscalers. Rather than constraining issuance, the market adjustment is likely to come through wider spreads. Hyperscaler ’27/’28 OCF is also expected to be 7-8X larger than required debt.
8) Regulatory Concerns and Bottlenecks: We view state and local pushback to data centers as largely manageable, although remain focused on the buildup to the ’28 election as a political risk to exacerbating this bottleneck. Time to market on power will remain a significant bottleneck, which leaves us constructive on power suppliers.

How Much Compute Capacity Do We See Coming on?
Our View: We see the capex spend leading to ~80 GW of capacity coming online in ’27/’28 (38 GW/44 GW). For perspective, this implies we reach ~145 GW of total compute capacity in ’28, up ~4X from ’25.
We see custom ASICs rising from 34% of incremental capacity in ’25 to 66% of incremental capacity in ’28, driven primarily by Trainium and TPU, of which we think a combined 12 GW will come online in ’28 (vs. 13 GW for NVDA).
Stock Implications: Compute capacity remains a scarce resource that will be monetized as it comes online (new GenAI enabled tools, agentic workflows, etc). This means hyperscalers, labs and enablers (AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT), and companies benefiting from diffusion across the economy benefit.

What Are Open-Weights Models?
Open-Weights models give users more control over model weighting and are: 1) free to download by any end user; 2) able to be fine-tuned/re-trained on a user’s proprietary data; and 3) deployable wherever the user chooses (on-prem or in the cloud).

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)
Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid.
