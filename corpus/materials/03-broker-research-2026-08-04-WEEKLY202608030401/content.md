Weekly Warm-up: From Early Cycle to Mid Cycle
One of the most significant momentum drawdowns in history appears to have ended, shifting the focus to what leads next. Our work points to EPS quality at the factor level, and today we highlight a more targeted AI adopter screen.
• An Early-to-Mid Cycle Transition Has Begun, Favoring Quality… As the business cycle matures and post-recession operating leverage moderates, we believe leadership should rotate from low quality toward companies with more stable earnings, strong margins and operational efficiency. With high quality representing 42% of the S&P versus 28% for low quality—and median stock earnings growth accelerating to 14% as revisions breadth improves—the setup should support greater index resilience, broader participation and 8,000 on the S&P by year-end, in our view.
One of the Worst Momentum Sell-Offs in History… Momentum extended its decline through the first three days of last week, bottoming as a large player reportedly exited most of its public-equities portfolio—a typical endpoint for this kind of unwind. Semis troughed almost exactly where our Silver-stocks analog suggested and we believe likely have further near-term upside, but may struggle to reclaim leadership this year based on the analog itself and our call for a mid-cycle transition (Semis are an early cycle sector). Meanwhile, momentum is rotating toward quality, Insurance and Healthcare Equipment & Services, and away from the Semiconductor trade based on our 3-month versus 12-month momentum analysis.
We Still Favor Hyperscalers and Mag 7 over Semis with an Important Caveat… Hyperscalers combine attractive relative value—their forward multiple is in just the third percentile back to 2023—with meaningful optionality: resilient core businesses, upside from leadership in the AI application layer and underappreciated scope to reduce opex if needed. As both AI enablers and adopters, they offer a compelling multi-month risk/ reward, which is further supported by our Internet analyst Brian Nowak and team’s work on the ROIC opportunity. The Hyperscalers have outperformed Semis by nearly 20% on an equal weighted basis over the last 4 weeks, and we believe that will continue over the next several months. However, we also expect there to be dispersion among the Hyperscalers going forward with ROI, balanced capex communication and quality key differentiating factors. Last week's large performance spread between MSFT and META is a recent example of that dispersion, and it was based on the capex/sales factor, in part. Whether this leads to a more conservative stance on capex guidance remains to be seen, but that risk is a potential overhang to revisions breadth for certain capex beneficiaries, in our view.

From Early Cycle to Mid Cycle
As discussed last week, we believe the business cycle is transitioning from early to mid cycle. As our factor work reinforces, this is typically a period when leadership shifts from lower to higher quality as cyclically driven operating leverage begins to slow, and the market places greater emphasis on earnings stability, strong margins and free cash flow generation. Consistent with what we have seen recently, mid-cycle transitions are also typically marked by a central bank shifting from rate cuts to holding policy steady—or even considering a hike. This also helps to drive the market away from low quality leadership, which had been the dominant trend since April of last year. It's worth emphasizing that high-quality cohorts represent roughly 42% of the S&P, versus about 28% for low quality. Near-term consolidation remains possible if the war escalates and/or back end rates move toward 5%, but the quality rotation should ultimately support index resilience and broader participation into year end. Further, our earnings broadening thesis continues to play out as median stock earnings growth is now 14% ( Exhibit 1 ) and highfrequency earnings revisions breadth is bouncing materially ( Exhibit 2 ). Our rolling recovery economic narrative from the rolling recession trough in April 2025 remains very much out of consensus, in our view. Hence, why our call for a transition to mid from early cycle is also underappreciated and an opportunity for investors.
As we transition to mid-cycle, we expect the composition of the momentum factor to shift as well. We think it's worth reminding investors that the sector and style composition of momentum does shift over time, even if price momentum as a strategy remains in favor. Looking out 3-6 months, Semiconductors may no longer be the dominant group in the high momentum bucket as leadership changes. We saw the same thing earlier this year with other commodity sectors led by Precious Metals, Rare Earths and Energy stocks. As we discuss below, we think the Hyperscalers are a potential candidate within the AI enabler complex to re-enter this cohort into year-end. However, it's likely the market will be more discerning on the quality factor within this group as well. Just as the business cycle is maturing, the AI capex and investment cycle is maturing as well. The cycle still has plenty of runway, but volatility bands are widening in both an up and down tape. The market is oscillating between rewarding infrastructure providers and imposing greater capex discipline, particularly when investors press for convincing evidence of ROIC and adoption. Investors are increasingly being asked both to capture these rotations and to own the right enabler trades when the market returns to rewarding AI infrastructure providers as evidence of ROIC and adoption crystallizes.

The other key event for the markets last week was the July FOMC meeting. The initial reaction post the meeting was that equities would continue to sell off on concerns that the Fed was losing control of the back end of the yield curve. That remains a risk for the S&P 500 index in the near term if the market continues to challenge new Fed Chair Warsh's resolve to get inflation back to 2% without better communication on how he intends to actually do it. In this risk scenario, nominal 10- or 30-year Treasury yields could continue to rise at an accelerating pace with the 10-year yield potentially reaching 5%. Such a move would weight on the index at which point Warsh will likely have to respond in a way that relieves this tightening in financial conditions. Absent that risk scenario where the 10-year accelerates toward 5% in a very short time period, it's worth focusing on the notion that two-year yields ended the week below pre-FOMC levels. This produced a steeper curve, as back-end nominal yields remained sticky amid elevated inflation expectations, suggesting the bond market is attentive to inflation risks.
This is not necessarily a bearish rates backdrop for equities as long as bond volatility and funding markets remain in check. In fact, the strongest return environment for stocks is typically one in which nominal yields rise alongside stronger ISMs. Put differently, this setup brings back the “run it hot” theme even if the bond market isn't ok with it at the moment. That can be constructive for equities because revenue growth and inflation are highly correlated as we have discussed in our research on many occasions. The risk is that the rise in back-end yields, even if driven more by inflation expectations picking up, continues at such an accelerative pace that cost of capital becomes an issue regardless of the driver of the rate move (i.e., a mix of growth being stronger and inflation running hotter).
