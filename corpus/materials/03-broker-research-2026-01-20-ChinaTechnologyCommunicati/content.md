China Technology & Communications
2026 Outlook: Embrace AI Opportunities in Computing, Networking, Edges and Agents
CITI'S TAKE
We are positive to HK/China tech despite three years of outperformance (vs. MSCI China) but are aware of high expectations and macro/sector risks ahead. Valuation had re-rated exiting 2025; we prefer H shares to A shares among tech and communication names. In 2026, we continue to embrace AI opportunities across computing, networking, edges and agents, in which China localization also plays a key role. We highlight risks on upstream supply, material cost increase, downstream project delivery issues, etc. Stock picks: Among hardware/components, we prefer Luxshare, DSBJ, Lens Tech, Conant, TCL. Among semi, we prefer Montage. In software, we like Kingdee. In communication infrastructure, we like Eoptolink and TFC.
Key markets addressed — (1) We forecast -5%/-3% YoY growth for global/China smartphone shipment in 2026E, with ASP up 7%/5%. (2) We anticipate 20mn/29mn/45mn (+3%/47%/54%) units of foldable smartphone shipment in 2025E/26E/27E, driven by foldable iPhone. (3) We project handset camera lens shipment to reach 4.4/4.2/4.4bn units (+2%/-4%/+4% YoY) in 2025E/26E/27E. 200MP camera shipments are projected to increase to 85mn in 2026E from 35mn in 2025E. (4) We estimate 15mn/33mn (+123%/76% YoY) of global AI glasses shipment in 2026E/27E. (5) We forecast that total AI-PCB demand (incl. GPU, ASIC and switch) in 2026E/27E could be Rmb96/174bn (or US$13/24bn), implying 101%/81% YoY growth with GPU & ASIC PCB roadmap mostly well-known. (6) We anticipate the high-speed optical transceiver module market could reach US$37.4/59.1bn in 2026E/27E, with 69mn/103mn shipment units, which include 800G at 40mn/52mn and 1.6T at 20mn/46mn. (7) We estimate China AI chips shipment could reach 3.5mn with domestic accounting for 60%. (8) We estimate that display panel area demand in 2026E could grow 2.1%, with TV up 2.6% and IT panel flattish. (9) We believe third-party IDC players could see 10-20% growth YoY in 2026E, driven by double-digit % capex from CSP and the telco IDC business could continue to see c.10% YoY.
H/A-sh and subsector preference — Among our coverage, H shares are trading 0.5SD above 10-year historical forward P/E averages, while A shares are trading above 2SD. In 2026, we believe there will be some volatility in 1H26, given high expectations for the AI chain and possible peaking in GTC/OFC. We prefer H shares to A shares, and many IPOs are scheduled for 1H26, which could potentially provide more quality tech names for investors to allocate positions. We prefer semiconductor, followed by communication, hardware, and ITS/software the least.
Stock picks — Among our coverage, we like the Apple chain and prefer Luxshare, DSBJ and Lens Tech, which benefit from resilient demand from Apple, despite memory price hike, and foldable iPhone for content upgrade. Besides Apple, OpenAI AI edge should also benefit the Apple chain. For edge AI, we see AI glasses as a new growth driver and prefer Conant Optical. A cyclical name, TCL, is also preferred. For the AI chain, we prefer optical transceiver to PCB and top picks are Eoptolink, TFC Optical and DSBJ. We also like Montage, which benefits from AI demand and localization. For agentic, we like Kingdee on ToB AI agents.

Foldable iPhone likely a new ASP driver
iPhone 17 sales were better than expected, with the 2H25 build plan increased from the original 85mn to 96mn by year-end based on our estimates. Our forecast 1H26 iPhone production also increased 12% YoY thanks to the strong iPhone 17 demand. We would like to highlight the potential risk of a YoY decline in 2H26 given iPhone 18 Air delay and SKU adjustment. Based on our supply chain checks, it seems that only iPhone 18 foldable, iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max may be launched in 2H26, i.e., 3 models instead of the usual 4 models in the product cycle. Net-net, we expect full year production volume to be stable YoY with 12% YoY in 1H and down 8% YoY in 2H. The key highlight for 2H26 iPhone will be the foldable iPhone, the major upgrade includes (1) foldable display with ultra-thin glass (UTG) cover, (2) metal hinge and precision parts inside, (3) titanium casing, (4) larger and integrated vapor chamber design heatsink, (5) bigger battery and stainless steel cover and (6) more FPC adoption. Major beneficiaries include Lens Tech (UTG), followed by FII (titanium casing and foldable phone assembly), DSBJ (FPC/PCB) while Luxshare will likely gain higher content in top/bottom modules and BYDE will also benefit from titanium casing in 2H26.

Chinese memory vendors could gain market share
Chinese memory makers, led by Changxin Memory Technologies (CXMT; not listed) for DRAM and Yangtze Memory Technologies (YMTC; not listed) for NAND flash will likely take up a larger share of the global memory supply, in our view. Based on our industry checks, we estimate that: 1) CXMT had production capacity of 260-270k wpm, and 2) YTMC had production capacity of 130-140k wpm, as at the end of 2025. While we believe the effective supply increase from Chinese memory makers should be limited in 2026 and not meaningful enough to alter the memory price uptrend, we expect more potential output increase in 2027, which could start to impact memory pricing in the middle/lower end.
For CXMT, we believe its DRAM output is still constrained by:
1. Weaker performance (18mn/16nm production) and larger form factor (30-40% larger die sizes);
2. Production yield issues from technology migration (DDR4 to DDR5, G3 to G4);
3. Limited fab space, as Hefei Fab 1 and Fab 2 are nearly full.
However, for YMTC the supply increase could be more meaningful, given its strong technology, ample fab space for equipment installation, and improving domestic SPE capability (especially in etching and deposition). It is reported that CXMT (Reuters, Dec 31, 2025) and YMTC (Bloomberg, Oct 22, 2025) are both planning initial public offering in China in 2026, with the proceeds to fund their capacity expansion and greenfield facilities. (The companies did not comment on the media reports.)
Currently Chinese DRAM adoption remains limited at mainly entry-level Android smartphone and feature phones. However, the persistent memory price hike and supply shortages could become catalysts for accelerating adoption, in our view. We estimate DRAM cost accounts for 10-15% of BOM costs in high-end smartphones but could reach 20-30% in entry-level models, presenting significant cost pressure on vendors of lower-end smartphones. Worst still, a supply shortage could interrupt production and result in vendor market share losses. We believe OEMs may begin adopting Chinese memory products out of necessity should the price uptrend hike further in 2026.

Communication: AI Datacenter Driving Network Infrastructural Upgrade
US demand proxy: AI optics
Per our last sector report, we re-calibrate our global 1.6T transceiver volume forecast from 8mn to 20mn, while we slightly cut our 800G volume from 45mn to 40mn units. Total 800G/1.6T optical transceiver modules could reach 60mn, which is apparently reasonable in consideration of EML and CW laser supply and yield loss. But demand is way above these numbers. Meanwhile, we are more positive over transceiver demand visibility in 2027 with significant YoY growth likely, backed by the sustained narrative of a higher attach rate amid larger cluster size and potential networking infrastructure upgrade (dragonfly), scale-up opportunity (such as NPO). Although tight EML laser supply is likely to linger, we think SiPh solutions could see higher penetration ahead, and Innolight and Eoptolink could see a >50% SiPh mix in 2026E. We also see urgent order fee could potentially persist throughout the year given tight supply. Net-net, we anticipate high-speed optical transceiver module market could reach US$37.4/59.1bn in 2026E/27E with 69mn/103mn shipment units.
Key trends in 2026-27
n Scale-up opportunity likely coming: Scale-up optics could likely kick in earlier than expected, spearheaded by Google then Nvidia. It’s possible to replace some spine switches with OCS (optical circuit switch), which yields cost savings and power savings as addressed in Google’s papers. And OCS can be complementing NVLink switch in NVL rack for scale-up opportunity. The optical module will migrate to first LPO (linear pluggable optics) along with higher speed transmission like 1.6T/3.2T, then gradually migrate to NPO (near-packaged optics) for 3.2T/6.4T and then CPO (co-packaged optics) for 6.4T or above, which could drive the content value of transceiver names in AI clusters. We expect Innolight and TFC Optical to enjoy the most business benefits from the trend.

CPO take off could start from 2028, while the dominant solutions should remain optics, until the emergence of 6.4T solutions in 2030, in our view.

Demand: Large-to-medium-sized (LM) LCD panel demand area could increase by 2.1% in 2026E. Subsector Expectations and Stock Ideas
We see several potential opportunities in 2026 – US interest rate cut, ramping domestic AI datacenter, new AI edge devices, first foldable iPhone, etc. Yet, China’s fixed-asset investment growth and consumer goods retail sales growth have decelerated, which should weigh on domestic consumption expectations. In the 15th Five-Year Plan, accelerating scientific and technological self-reliance is one of the top 3 goals to support new, quality productivity for the economy. We expect AI (infrastructure and monetization) as well as localization to be the key themes for 2026.
In the semiconductor sector, we believe localization will continue to be the key theme aligned with policies, especially AI chips, memory and semiconductor equipment.
In communication, we think AI datacenters could continue to drive demand; supply constraints could be an issue (such as US electricity, US construction labor, EML supply). We expect supply constraints in optical communication to persist and improve only in 2H27. Regarding domestic AI datacenters, we expect China leading CSP (cloud service providers) and 3 telecom operators) projects to accelerate in 2H26E, which may benefit IDC yet weigh on chip supply.
For the hardware sector, we expect continued upgrades in AI server supply chain and AI edge devices in 2026. Yet, we are aware of risks such as memory price hikes to consumer electronics, upstream T-glass shortages, advanced manufacturing capacity tightness and high expectations in AI server shipments. For AI hardware, demand is robust, but supply constraints are an issue. We believe PCB content will increase in Rubin and ASIC platforms, and the complexity of server design should benefit ODMs. There are many Chinese hardware supply-chain companies that aim to pursue heatsink, cooling and power management component opportunities that we expect more new players to join the sector. For edge devices, we expect foldable iPhone, AI smart glasses and AI edge devices to be highlights, while the overall smartphone market could be under pressure due to memory price hikes.
For the ITS and software sector, we expect a continued recovery in earnings given normalized impact from tightening IT budgets among government and enterprises. More AI agentic applications could be launched for enterprises in 2026E, and we expect certain software companies to see 10%+ AI-related revenue, potentially driving stock re-ratings. Yet, we believe the sector will still be driven by the macro economy and policies, which may cause volatility.

Guide to Citi Research Fundamental Research Investment Ratings:
Citi Research stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks. Risk rating takes into account both price volatility and fundamental criteria. Stocks will either have no risk rating or a High risk rating assigned.
Investment Ratings: Citi Research investment ratings are Buy, Neutral and Sell. Our ratings are a function of analyst expectations of expected total return ("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus the dividend yield for a stock within the next 12 months. The target price is based on a 12 month time horizon. The Investment rating definitions are: Buy (1) ETR of 15% or more or 25% or more for High risk stocks; and Sell (3) for negative ETR. Any covered stock not assigned a Buy or a Sell is a Neutral (2). For stocks rated Neutral (2), if an analyst believes that there are insufficient valuation drivers and/or investment catalysts to derive a positive or negative investment view, they may elect with the approval of Citi Research management not to assign a target price and, thus, not derive an ETR. Citi Research may suspend its rating and target price and assign “Rating Suspended” status for regulatory and/or internal policy reasons. Citi Research may also suspend its rating and target price and assign "Under Review" status for other exceptional circumstances (e.g. lack of information critical to the analyst's thesis, trading suspension) affecting the company and/or trading in the company's securities. In both such situations, the rating and target price will show as “—“ and “-“ respectively in the rating history price chart. Prior to 11 April 2022 Citi Research assigned “Under Review” status to both situations and prior to 11 Nov 2020 only in exceptional circumstances. As soon as practically possible, the analyst will publish a note re-establishing a rating and investment thesis. Investment ratings are determined by the ranges described above at the time of initiation of coverage, a change in investment and/or risk rating, or a change in target price (subject to limited management discretion). At times, the expected total returns may fall outside of these ranges because of market price movements and/or other short-term volatility or trading patterns. Such interim deviations will be permitted but will become subject to review by Research Management. Your decision to buy or sell a security should be based upon your personal investment objectives and should be made only after evaluating the stock's expected performance and risk.
Catalyst Watch/Short Term Views (“STV”) Ratings Disclosure:
Catalyst Watch and STV Upside/Downside calls: Citi Research may also include a Catalyst Watch or STV Upside or Downside call to indicate the analyst expects the share price to rise (fall) in absolute terms over a specified period of 30 or 90 days in reaction to one or more specific near-term catalysts or events impacting the company or the market. A Catalyst Watch will be published when Analyst confidence is high that an impact to share price will occur; it will be a STV when confidence level is moderate. A Catalyst Watch or STV Upside/Downside call will automatically expire at the end of the specified 30/90 day period. The Catalyst Watch will also be automatically removed if share price performance (calculated at market close) exceeds 15% against the direction of the call (unless over-ridden by the analyst). The analyst may also remove a Catalyst Watch or STV call prior to the end of the specified period in a published research note. A Catalyst Watch/STV Upside or Downside call may be different from and does not affect a stock’s fundamental equity rating, which reflects a longer-term total absolute return expectation. For purposes of FINRA ratings-distribution-disclosure rules, a Catalyst Watch/STV Upside call corresponds to a buy recommendation and a Catalyst Watch/STV Downside call corresponds to a sell recommendation. Any stock not assigned to a Catalyst Watch Upside, Catalyst Watch Downside, STV Upside, or STV Downside call is considered Catalyst Watch/STV No View. For purposes of FINRA ratings distribution-disclosure rules, we correspond Catalyst Watch/STV No View to Hold in our ratings distribution table for our Catalyst Watch/STV Upside/Downside rating system. However, we reiterate that we do not consider No View to be a recommendation. For all Catalyst Watch/STV Upside/Downside calls, risk exists that the catalyst(s) and associated share-price movement will not materialize as expected.
