1 . Automotive illustrates why similar revenue exposure can mask very different roles in the purchase process.
a. A consumer researching a first EV may compare models on Google or ChatGPT, then visit automaker websites.
b. When the consumer later opens Facebook or Instagram, advertisers can use CRM data to retarget, book a test drive and move toward a sale.
c. Google and Meta earn ad revenue at different stages. If Muse handles the earlier research and comparison work, Meta could monetize those stages as well as the conversion activity it already captures.
2 . Meta’s higher revenue exposure to health and wellness and education also needs context.
a. In health, lifestyle products such as supplements, vitamins, wellness drinks and wearables are well suited to Meta advertising.
b. More involved healthcare needs, such as finding a hospital or buying medication, are primarily served through Google.
3. Education and training:
a. Meta works well for lower-consideration decisions such as finding a tutor or weekend activities for children.
b. Higher-priced education products typically require longer searches and more detailed questions and answers, making Google the primary advertising channel.
If Muse gains broader adoption and gives Meta access to these intent signals, we see a credible path to more than 20% incremental GMV. These are precisely the advertising categories in which Meta has historically been weaker than Google.
