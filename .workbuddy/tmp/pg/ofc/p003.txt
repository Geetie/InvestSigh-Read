With CY1Q26 nearly complete, market consensus for a surge in DRAM pricing aligns with our view. Regarding HBM, despite some prior market noise suggesting potential exclusion from NVIDIA’s HBM4 roadmap, our supply chain checks indicate that qualification remains on track. We anticipate a successful qualification, securing Micron’s share in the HBM4 ramp. Even in a bear-case scenario, robust demand from Google TPUs and other AI ASICs/GPGPUs ensures full capacity absorption.
In summary, against a backdrop of structural undersupply and sustained pricing momentum, we remain constructive on Micron’s upcoming earnings report. We actually believe that by the third quarter of this year, we could see an $80 run-rate EPS.
META Layoff- The biggest news over the weekend was Meta's layoffs. The Magic 7 have all seen notable declines recently. Although each company faces different circumstances, whether it's the hyperscalers or companies like Meta, the main issue revolves around ROI. As we discussed in more detail in our 4Q25 Review, Meta's challenge is really about how to value the stock — how do you assign a valuation when you have 20% topline growth but only 10% bottomline growth? The layoffs are the first step toward addressing this problem — the first step toward narrowing the gap between bottomline growth and topline growth.
We believe the biggest catalyst ahead for Meta is Manus. I'm believethat Manus can provide significant value to Meta's WhatsApp and Messenger ecosystem. Although The Information previously reported on WeChat's agent use cases, We believe WeChat had already completed the work reported by The Information as early as six months ago.
