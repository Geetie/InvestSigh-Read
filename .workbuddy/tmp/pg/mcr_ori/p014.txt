These switches also require the use of specialized switch chips, namely Broadcom’s Jericho3-AI, which provides deep buffers to absorb these large and bursty traffic flows, as well as the Ramon3 chip, which acts as the spine switch to forward the traffic sent by the Jericho3-AI chips.
At the data-plane level, the two main characteristics of DSF are that it relies on Virtual Output Queuing (VOQ) and cell-spraying. In a naive switch, every input has one queue. If the packet at the head is destined to a congested output, everything behind it stalls, even packets destined to idle outputs. VOQ fixes this part by allocating each ingress its own virtual queue for every egress. In DSF, it means that if one link is congested, packets wait for their dedicated queue while packets destined for other links continue to flow.
