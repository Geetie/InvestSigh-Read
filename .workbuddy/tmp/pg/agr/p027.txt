The consumer-facing companies at highest risk of an agentic rugpull are those that had high switching costs until 
yesterday. Some of those costs are because there are only a few alternatives. Others were effective at weaponizing human 
nature and biases to ensure that you never churned.
Critically, these techniques don’t work against AI agents.
“Are You Still Paying for That?”  
The first companies on the chopping block are companies that, at least in an agent’s eyes, could be viewed as excessive 
spend. The publishing industry is an easy target, particularly as these subscriptions have evolved into digital delivery, 
paving the way for simple, agentic-led cancellations.
New York Times 2Q26 Investor Presentation
In our subscriber chat, we flagged The New York Times (NYT US) as a short candidate under this framework. Digital 
subscriptions account for a majority of NYT’s subscriber base, with promotional subscriptions driving a +3% boost to ARPU 
during their latest earnings release.
However, there is a plausible scenario in which subscriber numbers remain firm, but ARPU slips as agents negotiate 
retention discounts and trade down to standalone Games or Cooking subscriptions.
