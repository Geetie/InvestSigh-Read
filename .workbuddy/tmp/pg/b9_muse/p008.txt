Taking Everyday Tasks Off Users’ Hands
Grouping savings, administrative tasks, travel, shopping, dining, family, health and home tasks as “life admin” gives 411 cases, or 62.8% of the sample. Work and business account for 11.2%; technology and setup, 8.3%; personal organization and social activities, 7.2%; content and research, 5.7%; and entertainment, 3.4%.
Life admin’s share fell roughly two percentage points from 64.9% a week earlier, with work and business gaining 1.1 points and technology and setup gaining 0.8 points. This reflects a broader user base rather than fewer chores. More freelancers, small-business owners and developers arrived in the second week, bringing more productivityoriented tasks. Still, more than 60% of cases involve things people need to do but would rather avoid: spending an hour with customer support, filling out a government form, comparing three insurance policies or calling five stores. These tasks require little intellectual effort but involve considerable friction, and putting them off has a real cost. When someone says, “I put this off for three months and Muse did it in ten minutes,” the appeal is the relief of finally getting it done.
