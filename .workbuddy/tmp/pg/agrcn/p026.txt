For Gen, the pressure comes from a customer who now has help scrutinizing every renewal. Norton and LifeLock have useful features, but an agent can identify overlapping coverage, compare alternatives and help the customer switch. The services can remain useful while the price consumers are willing to pay for the package declines.
Intuit faces a different version of the same problem. Credit Karma’s free service monetizes the journey from financial information to a product recommendation. If an agent takes over that journey, referral economics become more contested. An application routed away from Credit Karma can still produce revenue for a bureau. TurboTax adds exposure to the declining cost of financial administration: gathering documents and navigating a cheaper preparation service become less daunting when the customer has assistance.
Experian also has an opportunity to verify the agents themselves. Its announced Agent Trust framework links verified consumers to agents acting on their behalf, with collaborators including Visa, Cloudflare and Skyfire. That remains a developing commercial opportunity, but it gives us a concrete example of agent adoption creating another use for identity information.
There are crosscurrents. Experian has consumer subscriptions and marketplaces of its own. QuickBooks’ accounting records and embedded workflows give Intuit a more durable business, and Intuit is already distributing its products through Claude. Our bet is that verified data retains more pricing power as the consumer interface changes.
Retail
The disaggregation mechanism will also unfold across the retail ecosystem. Companies like Uber (UBER US) and Instacart (CART US) are textbook aggregators – packaging a collection of non-exclusive suppliers (drivers, grocers). Their business model banks on the fact that people are either too impatient or too lazy to go through the process of evaluating their options, making a decision, calling the restaurant or visiting the store, and making the transaction.
