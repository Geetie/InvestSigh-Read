As we said back in February:
"Agents accelerated both sides of the destruction. They enabled the competitors and then they used them. The DoorDash moat was literally'you' re hungry, you' re lazy, this is the app on your home screen.' An agent doesn' t have a home screen. It checks DoorDash, Uber Eats, the restaurant' s own site, and twenty new vibe-coded alternatives so it can pick the lowest fee and fastest delivery every time."
Now, an agent-native food delivery platform has yet to emerge from this megatrend, but the disintermediation risk is legitimate. AI agents are far more likely to go directly to the restaurant itself, rather than bank on the convenience of DoorDash’s sleek UI.
We are simply highlighting this reasoning again - UBER, CART volumes probably go unchanged as people go from using the apps to using agents…but that could impact advertising revenue in the short term. And in the long term, we do believe that we will see an agentic ecosystem where every player in the chain has an agent and there is enough incentive for new entrants to spin up competitors like Empower.
Under this pairing, we’d short the intermediaries (UBER, CART) against a long expression of the origin. Those are grocery stores and chains like Walmart (WMT US) and Costco (COST US).
