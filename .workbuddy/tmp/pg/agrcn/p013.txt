Separately, we are still bullish on Cybersecurity – specifically agentic identity vendors like SailPoint (SAIL US), Rubrik (RBRK US), and Okta (OKTA US) as well as SASE platforms like Zscaler (ZS US) and Netskope (NTSK US).
We were also likely wrong about the impact to the card names (AXP, V, MA) – people have had no qualms thus far about handing their credit cards to agents. Although Muse’s integration with Stripe’s Link is a comforting backstop. Visa (V US) and Mastercard (MA US) are set up to be winners under this regime, given the ubiquity of their network. Stripe too, appears to be a winner – taking share from competing PSPs as a first mover in the agentic commerce market. Agents will likely increase transaction volume and, as long as people are content to trust them with card details, should be net positive for the networks.
Stripe navigates agentic commerce with the help of Cloudflare (NET US) – a name we’ve been pounding the table on for a while. We’ve been adamant on the company’s unique position for this era since we wrote Agentic Utilities back in March. Their CDN peers in Fastly (FSLY US) and Akamai (AKAM US) should also see more bullish action, but NET specifically is still best and first in class.
What’s the first reaction that companies will have to all of this? Easy, they' Il fight back. They will do whatever they can to ensure that humans, rather than machines, are the ones that have to do the things they’re relying on humans being too lazy to do. They won’t go quietly into that night of LTV decline. And one company is positioned to benefit from all of their efforts.
It’s not like these merchants can simply ban all agents, also. They need to distinguish an authorized customer representative from scraping, fraud or abusive automation. Cloudflare already works with Visa and Mastercard on agent commerce. The commercial question is whether this produces additional paid security and infrastructure usage.
