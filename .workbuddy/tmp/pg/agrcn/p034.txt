These risks also confront Sirius XM (SIRI US). About 95% of the subscribers are “self-pay,” meaning the accounts are tied to a recurring payment from a consumer rather than a promo with a new car. In their Q2 earnings release, they talked about their subscriber loyalty:
"Self-pay monthly churn improved to approximately 1.4%, the lowest level in SiriusXM history, reflecting the continued benefits of the continuous service initiative and lower vehicle-related and non-pay churn, partially offset by slightly higher voluntary churn."
It was good while it lasted.
The relics of the cable model are just as vulnerable. These are the streaming services that make you think: How are they still around? And how do they make any money? Starz (STRz US) was formerly owned by Lionsgate before spinning out as a standalone, premium subscription. While they’ve aimed to bundle their offering with the modern vMVPD apparatus, the reality is that their model, along with competing niche streamers like AMC Networks (AMCX US) and Angel Studios (ANGX US), is at risk from the exact double-sided LTV/CAC threat we detailed earlier. These vendors – and the larger streamers too – are constantly refreshing their content library, which in practice is just a customer acquisition expense.
