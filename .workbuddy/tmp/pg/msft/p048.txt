Microsoft’s rail-only architecture – the path to 524,288 GPUs
Microsoft takes this concept even further, deploying a rail-only topology to build a network that can connect up to 524,288 GPUs on a two-layer network! In a rail-only network topology, we apply the concept of splitting links into many ports across an individual GPU to splitting links across an entire compute tray. Instead of splitting 800G per GPU into 8 links, we are now splitting 3,200G per compute tray into 32 links – one connecting to each of 32 planes.
This network utilizes 24,576 switches to connect 524,288 GPUs, achieving the same GPU to switch ratio of 21.3 as for the 131,072 GPU network, but connecting four times as many GPUs!
However, each of these planes are completely independent, meaning that individual GPUs in the same compute tray are no longer able to communicate with each other over the scale-out network. Instead, they need to communicate through PXN over the NVLink network. This does, however, introduce a challenge as it is difficult to overlap different communications over the same network[CE1] , though Microsoft’s MRC protocol was designed to optimize such traffic and schedule this traffic at the training level.
