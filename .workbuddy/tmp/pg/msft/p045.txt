OpenAI’s Chip Effort
Microsoft has no excuses, they’ve had plenty of foresight into the model architecture through their early and prescient investment into OpenAI. But at this rate OpenAI, who had some input into the Maia 200 design, may have better custom silicon than Microsoft despite starting their custom silicon efforts several years later. However, this may prove to be a good thing for Microsoft: Microsoft’s rights to OpenAI IP extend to everything except for consumer hardware, which includes access to the OpenAI Titan ASIC line. Given the trajectory of OpenAI’s ASIC developments look much better than that of Microsoft Maia, it maybe that Microsoft just ends up using Titan to serve OpenAI models. This situation is similar to Microsoft’s situation with OpenAI models. While they have access to OpenAI models: they are still trying to train their own foundation model with Microsoft AI. Like the models, access to the chip roadmap is not persistent given those IP rights do not go into perpetuity. Being reliant on the OpenAI’s ASIC does not achieve the goal of being self sufficient in hardware that is a common goal shared among hyperscaler ASIC programs.
We also believe that this leads to yet another Broadcom custom ASIC customer. The top end of the AI chip market is quickly becoming a two-horse race for value capture: NVIDIA vs Broadcom.
