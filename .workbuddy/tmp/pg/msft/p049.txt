The AI WAN: Moving beyond the datacenter building – Campus Scale-Across
While Microsoft could deploy up to 524,288 GPUs using 32 planes, in practice, each building within Fairwater 2 (i.e. Building A and Building B) will have only up to 300MW of capacity, corresponding to about 160,000 GPUs. Instead of focusing on connecting more GPUs on the same multi-plane network, Microsoft instead uses an oversubscribed uplink from the spine layer into an AI Wide Area Network (AI WAN).
Microsoft has specifically built the AI WANs such that training jobs are conducted in a way that leverages and is aware of the WAN connectivity. The intent is to eventually carry out distributed training jobs across the two Fairwater Campuses (Atlanta and Wisconsin) and even across the Phoenix, Iowa and Abeline campuses.
To build this network, Microsoft connects this “smaller” number of GPUs while allocating ports on the BT1 (spine) switch for uplink to the OCS while reserving some empty ports for expansion. Such a network could be built up using 32 planes, with 128 BT1 (spine) switches and 154 BT0 (leaf) switches per plane, with OCS switches above the BT1 (spine) layer.
