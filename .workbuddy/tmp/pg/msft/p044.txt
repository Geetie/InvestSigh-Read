The takeaway is that all the hyperscalers will have deployed ASICs that support real workloads except Microsoft. Microsoft’s silicon team is not just competing with the hyperscalers: the real competitor is Nvidia. Good luck. If Microsoft is completely reliant on renting out Nvidia GPUs because they don’t have their own accelerator that has any external demand, then they are relegated to competing with likes of Oracle, CoreWeave, and Nebius. Meanwhile, Google and Amazon have the opportunity to earn better margins from their differentiated technology stacks.
As we mentioned, the accelerator silicon is the single largest cost item when it comes to deploying AI infrastructure. This is because for the gold standard of Nvidia GPUs – the cost comes with Nvidia’s hefty gross margin of 75% and above. This is a 4 x markup on Nvidia’s cost. One of the primary goals of designing custom silicon is to eliminate that margin with the hyperscalers having their own design and going directly to TSMC to manufacture it.
However, the reality is that hyperscalers do not have end to end silicon design capability so need to rely on design partners such as Broadcom, Marvell, MediaTek, Alchip, and GUC. These partners require their margin but it still ends up being less than what Nvidia charges, even with the “first class” Broadcom offering.
