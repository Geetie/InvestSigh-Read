No deep buffer switches are required. Rather, a separate protocol is used to send packets through the OCS to different clusters. Google has already been using the Apollo OCS for its data-center network (DCN), citing the flexibility that it can add for fabric expansion, technology refreshes and adjustments in network topology.
The AI WAN: Long-Distance Scale-Across
In addition to the AI WAN employed for campus connectivity within Fairwater 2, there is a second AI WAN which is a long-haul network that connects the Fairwater 2 campus to other distant Microsoft regions – one in Wisconsin (Fairwater 1) as well as clusters in Phoenix, the Iowa OpenAI clusters as well as Oracle’s Abilene campus. The aggregate bandwidth of long-haul links between these regions is 300Tbps, with an option to scale to 10Pbps.
While FR Optics have the range to connect the two 300MW buildings in Fairwater 2, connecting to the other campuses, which are up to thousands of kilometers away, will likely require the power and range of a transponders and will utilize a reconfigurable line system (RLS) so that dense wave division multiplexing (DWDM) can be used. To make efficient use of long-distance fiber, DWDM is used to multiplex many wavelengths of light – each carrying 800G or up to 1.6T of bandwidth – onto the same optical fiber pair. If 32 channels of the C-band and 32 channels of the L-band are utilized to combine 64 800G links, a single fiber pair could carry up to 51.2 Pbit/s of bandwidth.
