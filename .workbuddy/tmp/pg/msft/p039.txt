As expected of first generation silicon, Maia 100 was not manufactured in high volume or deployed for production workloads. The chip was architected before the Gen AI boom, leaving it short on memory bandwidth suitable for inference. ASIC programs take multiple generations to iterate until meaningful compute can be offloaded compute from merchant systems.

The development of the next generation Maia 200 also stalled with multiple issues that prolonged the design process and the delayed tape out to late in 2024, with mass production only starting from 2025. The total number of units that Maia 200 is expected to ship across 2025 and 2026 is only a fraction of Microsoft’s hyperscaler peers: Google, Amazon, and even Meta. This is because the Maia 200 chip has been assessed a failure internally, forcing Microsoft back to the drawing board for their AI ASIC roadmap.
Microsoft has even given up on software development for Maia 200, instead focusing efforts on future Maia iterations. Now, it looks like late 2027 will be the earliest that Microsoft will be able to deploy something reasonably close to internal performance projections with their 2nm Maia 300. In this timeframe, the competitive bar gets pushed even higher, with Microsoft needing to compete against Nvidia’s Vera Rubin.
